**T15 — Reactive and Harmonic Load | June 2026**

The M view on T15 confirms a reactive burden that is substantial, persistent, and entirely uncompensated. The amperage trace peaks at 1,687.6 amps maximum, with an average running load of 953.6 amps. On a 2,500 kVA transformer at 480V, that average amperage represents a field carrying a significant reactive component on top of its productive real-power work — and the transformer is absorbing the difference as heat and impedance loss around the clock.

The reactive kVAR layer reads a maximum of 671 kVAR from the M view header, cross-referencing with the BT header's 723.5 kVAR MAX. These two readings are consistent and mutually reinforcing — this transformer is generating reactive demand in excess of 700 kVAR at peak, and that reactive load never disappears. The M view shows the green reactive layer maintaining a persistent floor across weekends, overnight periods, and every production shift. The baseline reactive-generating equipment — refrigeration compressors, cold storage motor arrays, HVAC systems — never fully de-energizes, and neither does its reactive draw.

The harmonic picture adds another layer of concern on top of the reactive stress. The iTHD average reads 4.9% across the month, which on its own would be manageable. But the maximum iTHD recorded is 43.4% — and that figure is not manageable. A 43.4% total harmonic distortion event represents a severe injection into the transformer field. Even if that peak is a momentary spike associated with a large motor start, the energy it deposits into transformer core losses and winding insulation during that event is disproportionate. And at 4.9% average, the transformer is operating in elevated harmonic territory continuously, which means the derating impact on insulation life is ongoing and not recoverable without correction.

The combination of high reactive load, persistent harmonic content, and a 81.1% average power factor creates what I would characterize as a compounding decay condition. Each of these stressors amplifies the others. Reactive current increases conductor heating. Harmonic currents increase core losses. Poor power factor means the transformer is working harder to deliver the same productive output. In an untuned field — which T15 is, right now — these conditions do not remain static. They tend to worsen month over month as insulation degrades and the field's natural impedance characteristics shift.
