T10 Air Chiller is a 3,000 kVA transformer serving the air chiller operation at Foster Farms' Cherry Avenue facility in Fresno. The June 2026 dataset reveals a field that is carrying significant reactive burden in an untuned state — and the cost of that condition is measurable, substantial, and growing.

The field is averaging 88.8 percent power factor across the month, with a documented floor of 84.32 percent during its worst operating intervals. For a transformer of this size and loading, an 88.8 percent average is not a marginal concern — it means roughly 11 cents of every dollar of electrical capacity delivered to this transformer is doing no productive work. It is circulating as reactive magnetizing current, heating conductors, stressing insulation, and generating a thermal burden that your cooling infrastructure has to actively fight every hour of every production day.

Peak demand on T10 is reaching 1,455.0 kilowatts, against a 1,545 kVA supply target. That's a heavily loaded transformer. The reactive component — averaging 440.8 kVAR with peaks to 776.0 kVAR — is riding on top of that active load and pushing the apparent power well above what the real work requires. The result is a field that is thermally stressed, electrically wasteful, and — without a tuning assignment — drifting in the wrong direction month over month.

The estimated waste embedded in this field stands at approximately $29,730 per dataset month, projecting to roughly $361,720 per year in direct reactive losses alone. When thermal burden and cooling overhead are included, the total wasted figure climbs to approximately $693,304 annually. That number is not a projection artifact — it is the cost of leaving this transformer untuned while it serves one of your most energy-intensive processes.
