**T16 Compressor — Simulated MPTS Outcome | June 2026**

Three H490 units — representing approximately 1,200 kVAR of installed reactive compensation capacity — are indicated for T16 based on the 957.3 kVAR MAX sizing calculation. The planning-level installed investment for three H490 units is approximately $600,000, subject to final engineering scope and site-specific conditions. This is a planning-level estimate, not a binding quote.

What does correction deliver? The primary outcome is power factor recovery. From a measured 88.9% average, MPTS correction targets a post-correction power factor of approximately 99.5%. That eleven-percentage-point recovery is not a modest improvement — it represents a fundamental shift in how this transformer uses its capacity. At 99.5% power factor, nearly all of the transformer's rated capacity is delivering real power to the compressor load. The reactive current that currently consumes 957.3 kVAR at peak is absorbed by the MPTS field rather than circulating through the transformer windings.

The financial recovery projected from the BT/S analysis is substantial. Total annual waste is currently running at approximately $535,133. MPTS correction targets recovery of the majority of that waste — primarily through reactive loss elimination and thermal burden reduction. The energy waste component alone, approximately $422,956 per year, is directly addressable through power factor correction. Thermal burden reduction follows automatically: as reactive current is eliminated, the heat generated by that current drops proportionally, reducing the cooling overhead of 30.1 kW and approximately $10.73 per hour.

Voltage stability is an additional and significant benefit. With reactive current no longer competing for transformer capacity during high-demand events, the voltage trace stabilizes. The sub-nominal dips in the 452–456V range that are currently clustering around compressor start events should substantially diminish. A stabilized voltage field means less insulation fatigue, fewer nuisance trips, longer drive life, and reduced probability of the field-collapse events that produced the 9.5V minimum recorded in June.

Payback horizon: with approximately $34,763 per month in measurable waste, and a planning investment of approximately $600,000, the rough payback window is in the range of 17 to 18 months at current waste rates — before accounting for maintenance cost avoidance, drive replacement deferral, or production loss prevention. Final payback analysis depends on confirmed site rates and engineering scope.

The outage risk picture also improves. The two confirmed monthly outages — each representing 60 to 90 minutes of production time — are stress-correlated with reactive field instability. Stabilizing the reactive field through MPTS correction directly reduces the probability of stress-induced outage events. In a food processing environment, that outage risk reduction carries value well beyond the energy line item.
