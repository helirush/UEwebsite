**T15 — Foster Farms Cherry Ave, Fresno CA | June 2026 | Untuned Field**

T15 is a 2,500 kVA transformer serving what reads, by every electromagnetic signature in this dataset, like a high-intensity food processing and cold storage operation. And the field it is producing right now is under significant, measurable, and growing stress.

The headline numbers tell the story plainly. Power factor is running at 81.1% on average, with a maximum of 90.8% — and a minimum of 0.0%, which means this field is experiencing full reactive collapse events during load transitions. The peak demand is pulling 1,366.8 kW, and the reactive load is averaging 527.8 kVAR with a maximum spike of 723.5 kVAR. The transformer is being asked to carry both productive work and a massive reactive overhead simultaneously, and it is doing that with no compensation in place.

The financial consequence of that condition is not theoretical. The BT header puts the energy waste at approximately $40,819 per dataset period — annualized to roughly $496,647 per year in direct reactive losses. The total wasted figure, when thermal burden and cooling overhead are included, reaches approximately $840,783 per year. That is the cost of running an untuned field on a transformer this size.

The thermal burden is 587,410 BTU per hour — that is the heat this transformer is generating above what useful work would require. At an associated cooling cost of approximately $36.85 per hour and 104.6 kW of cooling load, the facility is paying in three separate ways: reactive losses, heat rejection, and the mechanical wear that elevated operating temperatures inflict on every motor, winding, and insulation layer in this system.

This is not a marginal condition. T15 is running at an efficiency deficit every single production hour of every working day, with no field correction applied and no reactive support in place.
