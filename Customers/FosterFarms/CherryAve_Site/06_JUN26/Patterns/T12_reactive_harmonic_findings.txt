**T12 — Reactive and Harmonic Findings | June 2026 | Untuned**

The reactive profile of T12 is aggressive by any industrial benchmark. Average reactive demand runs at 712.9 kVAR across the full 30-day dataset. The peak reactive demand reaches 1,043.5 kVAR — that is more than a megavar of reactive current flowing through this transformer at its worst moments. To put that in physical terms: at 1,043.5 kVAR peak, T12 is carrying a reactive current load nearly as large as the productive real-power load it exists to serve. That imbalance is what drives the 83.6 percent average power factor and the 76.29 percent power factor floor recorded at peak stress.

From the M view, the amperage trace tells a visceral story. Peak amperage reaches 2,177.4 amperes, against a dataset average of 1,615.8 amperes. The gap between those two numbers — nearly 562 amps of headroom being consumed by peak demand events — reflects the reactive current component inflating the total current beyond what real work alone would require. The sharp-edged, near-vertical rise characteristics of the red amperage peaks confirm that large motor loads are coming online hard, without soft-start or reactive pre-compensation. Each of those hard starts injects a reactive inrush into the transformer that the untuned field cannot absorb locally.

The green kVAR trace in the M view never disappears. Even during overnight valleys and weekend setback periods, a persistent baseline reactive layer remains. This is the signature of always-on inductive loads: refrigeration compressors maintaining cold-chain temperatures, large transformer magnetizing currents, HVAC systems that never fully de-energize. The utility is billing for that idle reactive demand whether or not the production floor is running.

Harmonic distortion is present but not the primary driver of waste on T12. The iTHD average of 5.8 percent and maximum of 10.8 percent are within manageable bounds for a facility of this type and size. The purple harmonics trace in the M view runs as a low-amplitude, relatively flat band. Harmonics are a secondary concern here — the reactive kVAR burden and the resulting thermal loading are the dominant waste mechanisms that MPTS correction would address first.
