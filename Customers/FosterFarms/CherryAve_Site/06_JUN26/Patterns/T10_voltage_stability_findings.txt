For the motors and compressors on this transformer, undervoltage operation in the 434–437 volt range means they are drawing higher current to maintain torque. Higher current means more winding heat. More winding heat means accelerating insulation aging. The voltage minimum tooltip confirms 434 volts as the floor of these labeled events, and the peak current reading of 1,905.4 amps in the V view — against an average of 986.7 amps — shows the compensating current surge that follows each voltage dip. A nearly two-to-one peak-to-average current ratio on a refrigeration transformer is a significant mechanical stress indicator. Without field correction, each recurring dip event is shortening the service life of the equipment connected to this bus.
