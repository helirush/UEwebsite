In September 2026, the T10 Air Chiller at Foster Farms presented a complex yet telling picture of its electrical performance. This analysis delves into the intricate dynamics of its energy field, focusing on five critical aspects: loading shape, electrical waste, power factor, voltage stability, and thermal burden.

First, let's consider the loading shape. The T10 Air Chiller's transformer exhibited a fluctuating amperage, as depicted by the red line in our charts. This variability suggests that the chiller was subjected to inconsistent load demands throughout the month. Despite these fluctuations, the voltage remained remarkably stable at around 480 volts, as shown by the blue line, indicating a reliable supply that shields the equipment from potential stress due to voltage instability.

Next, we turn to electrical waste. The energy domain charts reveal a moderate level of inefficiency. The difference between the supplied kVA, shown in orange, and the consumed kW, depicted in blue, highlights unconsumed kVAR, represented in green. This gap signifies the presence of electrical waste, with an unconsumed kVAR that contributes to an overall power factor of approximately 89.3%. While this power factor is relatively stable, it points to some inefficiencies in energy usage, as a perfect power factor would be closer to 100%.

The power factor itself, consistently hovering around 89.3%, suggests that while the system is stable, it is not operating at peak efficiency. This stability, however, does provide a certain level of predictability in performance, which is crucial for planning and optimization efforts.

Voltage stability, as previously mentioned, is a strong point for the T10 Air Chiller. The consistent voltage supply, free from significant deviations, ensures that the transformer does not experience undue stress, which is beneficial for the longevity of the equipment. However, the fluctuating amperage indicates that the system is subject to varying load conditions, which could potentially lead to inefficiencies if not managed properly.

Lastly, the thermal burden is an underlying concern. The charts indicate that the chiller's transformer experiences significant electrical waste, which could translate into additional heat generation. This heat burden, if not adequately managed, could impact the overall efficiency and lifespan of the equipment.

In summary, while the T10 Air Chiller at Foster Farms demonstrates stable voltage and a consistent power factor, the presence of electrical waste and fluctuating load conditions highlight areas for potential improvement. Addressing these inefficiencies could lead to enhanced performance and reduced operational costs, ensuring the chiller operates more sustainably in the future.
