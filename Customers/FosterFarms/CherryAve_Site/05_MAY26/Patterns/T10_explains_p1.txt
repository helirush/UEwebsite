Let me walk you through what we're seeing on T10 this May. T10 is a 3,000 kVA transformer — a serious piece of equipment — and right now it is running in an untuned condition. What that means in practice is that the field is generating waste across three categories simultaneously: energy losses, thermal burden, and cooling overhead. When you add those up, we're looking at an estimated $662,250 per year in total waste from this one transformer. That number is not an anomaly — it reflects the structural character of how this field is operating day in and day out. The power factor — the efficiency ratio of how well your transformer converts delivered apparent power into real productive work — is averaging 89.7% across May. That sounds close to acceptable, but the minimum drops to 81.4% during peak production intervals. That gap between average and minimum tells you the field is swinging, and swinging fields are expensive fields. The reactive burden sits at 420.6 kVAR on average, spiking to 760.5 kVAR at its worst. That reactive draw is not doing any work — it's circulating magnetizing current through your conductors and transformer core, generating heat and consuming capacity that could be supporting productive load. The voltage picture is also a concern. Your average voltage for the month is 457.4 volts on a 480-volt system — that's a chronic 22-volt deficit. And during three distinct drop events in May, the field fell to readings in the low 430s. At 10% below nominal, your motors are compensating by drawing extra current, which pushes directly into winding stress and thermal aging. The simulation shows that MPTS correction brings power factor to 99.5% and recovers approximately 358 kVAR on average — fundamentally changing the thermal and financial profile of this transformer.
