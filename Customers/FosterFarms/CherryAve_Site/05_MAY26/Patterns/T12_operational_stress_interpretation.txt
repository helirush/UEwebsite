**T12 — Operational Stress and Facility Impact | Synthesis Across V, BT, M Views**

Pull together everything the V, BT, and M views are showing and a clear operational picture emerges. T12 is a heavily loaded transformer in a demanding production environment, and it is currently operating without reactive compensation in a field that structurally generates high reactive demand. The result is a compounding stress condition that touches every major cost and reliability category simultaneously.

Start with the transformer itself. A thermal burden of 788,678 BTU per hour is not a number you file away and revisit later. That is the heat load this transformer is generating right now, in its current untuned state, during every production cycle. Managing that heat requires approximately 92 kilowatts of dedicated cooling capacity — at an estimated cost of $32.49 per hour. Annualized, that cooling overhead alone accounts for roughly $284,570 per year spent simply keeping the transformer from overheating due to losses that a corrected field would not generate.

The production pattern in the BT view shows eight to nine weekday production blocks spending meaningful time at or above the 1,721 kVA supply ceiling. That means on most active production days, T12 is regularly reaching or exceeding its designed operating headroom — and doing that with a power factor that is wasting, by direct calculation, roughly one dollar in every six delivered to the facility. The orange power factor trace running persistently in the 80-to-85 percent band through production hours is not a meter anomaly. It is a consistent structural feature of the load profile.

For a food processing and cold storage facility like Foster Farms, the operational implications branch in multiple directions. The refrigeration compressors are the most sensitive load class. They depend on stable, nominal-voltage supply to maintain rated torque and thermal efficiency. When voltage sags to 442 volts during peak load cycles — as this field clearly and repeatedly does — compressor motors draw higher current to maintain output, which increases winding temperature and accelerates insulation breakdown. Over a full production season, those cumulative stress events translate into shortened equipment life and unplanned maintenance events.

Beyond the compressors, the VFD-driven loads in this facility are exposed to repeated input voltage excursions that can trigger protective trips, introduce torque ripple in conveyor and processing systems, and generate reflected harmonic currents that feed back into the transformer and worsen the already-elevated 5.7-to-10.7 percent iTHD harmonic floor. In an untuned field, this feedback loop does not self-correct — it tends to intensify over time as equipment ages and reactive losses drift upward.

The total annualized waste picture from the BT header — approximately $610,000 per year — reflects all of these stress vectors combined. That is not a theoretical loss estimate. It is the measured consequence of operating a large industrial transformer at 83.9 percent average power factor with nearly 670 kVAR of uncompensated reactive drag running through it every production hour of every production day.
