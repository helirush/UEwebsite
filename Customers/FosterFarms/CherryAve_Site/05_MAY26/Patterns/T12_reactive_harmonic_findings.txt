**T12 — Reactive Burden and Harmonic Profile | Vision Extraction from M and BT Views**

The reactive profile on T12 is one of the most consequential findings in this analysis. The M view confirms a peak amperage draw of 2,194.1 amps — a substantial current load for this transformer class. Running alongside that amperage peak, the green kVAR reactive trace reaches a labeled maximum of 887 kVAR in the M view, which aligns closely with the BT header's reported kVAR MAX of 973.4 kVAR. The slight difference between these two readings is consistent with view-level sampling variation and does not represent a material discrepancy.

What makes the reactive situation on T12 particularly serious is not just the magnitude — it is the persistence. The kVAR average across the month is 669.9 kVAR. That means the reactive drag is not a spike that occasionally appears during unusual load conditions. It is the baseline operating state of this transformer. On most production hours of most production days, T12 is carrying nearly 670 kVAR of reactive current that is doing zero productive work. It is circulating. It is heating. And it is inflating the apparent kVA demand that the utility measures.

The power factor tells the same story from a different angle. At 83.9 percent average, with a minimum recorded at 68.6 percent, this field is — at its worst moments — converting barely two-thirds of delivered electrical power into productive output. The M view confirms a power factor minimum of 76.45 percent at peak stress intervals. Every percentage point below unity represents current that is circulating unproductively through the system infrastructure.

The harmonic picture adds another layer of concern. The purple iTHD trace in the M view is continuous across the full month — it never disappears, never drops to zero between cycles. The harmonics profile holds at approximately 5.7 percent iTHD average with peaks reaching 10.7 percent iTHD maximum. In an industrial food processing environment running motors, compressors, and variable frequency drives, a continuous harmonic floor at that level means invisible heat is being deposited into transformer cores, neutral conductors, and motor windings on every operating hour. Harmonic distortion at this level is not benign background noise — it is an accelerant for insulation aging and a contributor to elevated neutral current and transformer losses that do not appear on a simple kW demand meter.
