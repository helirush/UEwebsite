Transformer: PL2

Operational stress on PL2 cannot be formally interpreted without data, but the framework for that interpretation is worth establishing clearly, because it will guide how we read the data when it does arrive.

In a tuned field, operational stress has three primary dimensions. First is thermal burden — the heat generated by reactive current circulating through transformer windings and associated conductors. A well-compensated field should show meaningfully lower BTU-per-hour figures in the TBT capture compared to what the TB contextual view recorded before correction. Second is cooling overhead — the kilowatt load and cost per hour associated with removing that waste heat from the facility environment. In warm-season operation or in food processing and cold storage environments, this overhead is not trivial. Third is demand volatility — the degree to which the apparent power load in kVA swings above and below the daily supply target. High volatility signals that reactive loads are pulling the field unpredictably, which increases stress on both the transformer and downstream equipment.

For PL2 specifically, once TBT and TS data are in hand, we would compare the electric operations percentage — the proportion of days the demand column clears the target line — against what TB showed before the MPTS deployment. Improvement in that percentage is one of the clearest operational indicators that the tuned field is performing as intended.

Until data is available, PL2 operational stress is marked as uncharacterized. All three stress dimensions — thermal, cooling, and volatility — require numeric evidence before conclusions can be drawn.
