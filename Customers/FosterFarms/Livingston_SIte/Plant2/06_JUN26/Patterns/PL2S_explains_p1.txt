What you're looking at on PL2-1 South is a transformer that is working significantly harder than it needs to — and paying for that extra effort in heat, in reactive loss, and in voltage instability across the entire June production period. Let's start with the basics. Your daily operating target on this transformer is 1,687 kilovolt-amperes. During active production, you're regularly pushing into the 1,750 to 2,250 kVA range — above that target line — for roughly 60 to 65 percent of your productive operating hours. The transformer is not failing. But it is running in a chronic over-effort zone, carrying reactive burden that doesn't contribute to any useful process output. Your power factor for June is averaging 86.2 percent. That means roughly 14 cents of every apparent-power dollar delivered to this transformer is going to reactive circulation and heat — not to moving product through the facility. At your worst reactive moments, that floor drops to 80.25 percent. Nearly one dollar in five — doing nothing productive. The cost of that is real and measurable. The BT analysis shows approximately $21,488 in energy waste for June alone — projecting to about $261,446 per year in direct energy losses. But energy losses aren't the whole picture. Your thermal burden is running at 554,423 BTU per hour, and managing that heat is costing approximately $139,367 per year in cooling energy — 711.9 megawatt-hours per year — just to cool away waste that MPTS correction would largely eliminate. Your voltage field is reinforcing that story. The recorded minimum voltage this month was 454.8 volts — sitting below the ANSI utilization floor for 480-volt industrial systems. Two confirmed voltage sag events are visible in the data, and that limited voltage headroom is directly linked to the reactive burden on this feeder. Correct the reactive load, and the voltage floor stabilizes.
