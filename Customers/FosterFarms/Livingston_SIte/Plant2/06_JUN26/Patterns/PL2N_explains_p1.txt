Let's talk about what your PL2-3North transformer is telling us this June — because the field is speaking clearly, and what it's saying is that this transformer is working much harder than it needs to. The headline number is 61.3% power factor. In plain terms, that means for every unit of electrical energy you're paying for on this transformer, only about 61 cents worth is actually being converted into useful work. The rest is reactive current — circulating through your conductors, loading your windings, and generating heat without producing anything. On a 2,500 kVA transformer at Foster Farms Livingston HQ, that level of reactive burden accumulates fast. We're looking at an estimated annual waste of approximately $202,380 on this transformer alone. That figure includes reactive energy losses, the thermal burden — which is running near 265,000 BTU per hour — and the cooling overhead your facility is paying to manage that heat. Roughly $7.63 per hour, every hour this field runs untuned. The voltage field adds another layer of concern. Your transformer is running elevated — averaging around 484 volts against a 480-volt nominal — but in mid-June, specifically around the 19th through the 22nd, the field dips in the other direction. Four confirmed sub-nominal voltage events, the worst reaching 449 volts. That's a 6.5% sag, and for compressors, refrigeration equipment, and large motor loads, repeated voltage dips at that magnitude are quietly aging your equipment faster than it should be aging. The MPTS simulation gives us a clear look at the corrected state. Power factor moves to 99.5% and holds there. The reactive peak — currently at 1,163.8 kVAR — drops by approximately 97% to just 36 kVAR. Estimated energy recovery is roughly $125,000 per year.
