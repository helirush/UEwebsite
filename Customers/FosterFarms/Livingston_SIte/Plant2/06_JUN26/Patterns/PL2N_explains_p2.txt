PL2-3North at Foster Farms Livingston HQ is one of the more compelling cases we see — a 2,500 kVA transformer running at just 61.3% power factor in an untuned field, generating an estimated $202,380 in annual waste from reactive losses, thermal burden, and cooling overhead alone. The reactive peak of 1,163.8 kVAR is particularly significant — it's consistent with large inductive load groups starting simultaneously, and it places real instantaneous stress on the transformer and the upstream distribution infrastructure. Add four confirmed sub-nominal voltage dips mid-month — reaching as low as 449 volts against a 480-volt nominal — and you have a field that is both expensive to operate and quietly hard on connected equipment. MPTS correction brings power factor to 99.5%, cuts the reactive peak by 97%, and recovers an estimated $125,000 per year in energy savings. Three H490 units are indicated for this transformer, at a planning-level investment estimate of approximately $600,000 installed — with an estimated payback window in the range of three to five years. Without a tuning assignment, this trajectory doesn't improve on its own. Reactive accumulation in an untuned field tends to worsen over time, not self-correct. --- Pricing note: these figures are planning-level estimates, not final quotes. Final installed pricing depends on engineering scope, labor, and site conditions. For planning, estimated installed investment is roughly $510,000 to $690,000.
