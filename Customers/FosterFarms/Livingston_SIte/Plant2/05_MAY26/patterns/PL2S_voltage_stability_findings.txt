**Transformer: PL2-1South | Voltage Health Assessment**

The voltage field on PL2-1South is technically centered near nominal — a measured average of 481.4 volts against the 480-volt reference — but the average is doing a lot of work to hide what is actually a turbulent and stressed voltage environment. The maximum recorded voltage of 495.1 volts and the minimum of 0.0 volts define a range that no healthy industrial field should be spanning within a single month of operation. That zero reading is not a data artifact. It is a hard confirmation of at least one complete power interruption during the capture window.

The voltage waveform in the V view is not a stable, flat trace riding close to the 480-volt nominal. It is an actively oscillating line that moves continuously throughout the 442-hour capture window. During lighter load periods, the voltage rides the upper band near 495 volts. When production loads engage — motor starts, compressor cycling, process equipment — the voltage pulls down sharply. This voltage-load coupling behavior is a diagnostic signature of a reactive field that is not being compensated. The voltage is chasing the load rather than holding steady, which is exactly what you would expect in an untuned electromagnetic field of this scale.

The triangular drop patterns that appear throughout the waveform are recurring and not isolated. They are distributed across the full capture window, with the densest clustering in the mid-capture period. Each triangular feature marks a load-on event — voltage sags down, then recovers as the load releases. When those drops are tight and back-to-back, it means the field does not have sufficient recovery time between load events. That is the condition you're seeing in the mid-capture clustering zone.

Below-nominal dips are confirmed. The voltage trace repeatedly descends below 480 volts during load-on events. The worst operational dips appear to reach approximately 463 volts — roughly 17 volts below nominal, representing approximately a 3.5% under-voltage condition at the trough. In a food processing environment running motor-driven refrigeration compressors, conveyors, and pumping systems, a sustained 3.5% under-voltage condition is not a benign background condition. Motors drawing at under-voltage pull proportionally higher current to maintain torque. That higher current feeds directly into winding thermal stress and accelerated insulation degradation. The device-health risk posture here is assessed as moderate to high. Every production cycle that includes a sub-nominal voltage event is a cycle that contributes to shortened motor life and elevated contactor failure probability.
