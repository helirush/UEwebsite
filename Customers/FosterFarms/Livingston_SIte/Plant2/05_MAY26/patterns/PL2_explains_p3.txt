Now let me talk about what's definitely happening, even without numbers. PL2C is confirmed untuned. That's not an assumption — it's the field mode designation for this analysis. No MPTS equipment has been installed or assigned. In an untuned field, reactive current — measured in kilovolt-amperes reactive, or kVAR — circulates continuously through the transformer core, the primary and secondary windings, and out through the distribution conductors to the load. That reactive current does no productive electrical work. It doesn't run a motor, it doesn't power a compressor, it doesn't light a space. But it occupies conductor capacity, generates resistive losses, and most importantly, generates heat. That heat is the silent damage mechanism in an untuned field. Transformer insulation has a thermal life. Every degree-hour of elevated temperature above the design rating consumes a measurable portion of that insulation life. On a properly corrected field — one running at approximately 99.5 percent power factor with MPTS in place — reactive circulation drops sharply, thermal burden drops with it, and the transformer runs cooler and longer. On an untuned field, the degradation curve bends the wrong way. Month over month, without correction, efficiency tends to drift downward and thermal exposure tends to climb.
