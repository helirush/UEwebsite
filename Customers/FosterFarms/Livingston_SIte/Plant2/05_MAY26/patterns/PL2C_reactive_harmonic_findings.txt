**Transformer: PL2-2Center (PL2C) — Reactive and Harmonic Load Profile**

The reactive profile on PL2-2Center is persistent and substantial. The average reactive demand sits at 150.5 kVAR, with a maximum of 311.0 kVAR. That maximum figure — 311 kVAR — is the number that drives the MPTS sizing conversation, because it represents the worst-case reactive burden this transformer is being asked to carry at any point in the production cycle. At 311 kVAR peak, you're moving reactive current through those windings at a level that generates meaningful heat, occupies transformer capacity, and depresses power factor to its lowest recorded value.

The M view — Active-Reactive-Harmonic — puts physical context around those numbers. Average amperage through this transformer runs at 331.7 amps, with a peak of 520.7 amps recorded during the disturbance event near the outage. That 520-amp peak is a significant stress event — it's the reconnection inrush pulling hard on an already reactive-burdened field. The green kVAR trace in the M view is continuous and never disappears during operational hours, confirming that this reactive burden is not episodic — it is the constant baseline condition of an uncompensated inductive field.

The harmonic picture adds another layer. Measured iTHD (total harmonic distortion of current) averages 5.3 percent, with peaks reaching 10.4 percent. The 10.4 percent peak is noteworthy. While it doesn't yet constitute a severe harmonic environment, it represents a threshold where transformer core losses begin to increase meaningfully, winding eddy-current losses climb, and sensitive control equipment starts experiencing elevated noise floor conditions. In a facility running variable-frequency drives on refrigeration compressors and processing motors — which is standard for a Foster Farms-scale operation — harmonic content at this level deserves monitoring. In an untuned field, harmonic stress compounds over time rather than being managed.

The combined reactive-harmonic load profile on PL2-2Center is the direct driver of the 158,890 BTU/hr thermal burden measured on this transformer. That heat is not incidental — it is the physical consequence of moving 311 kVAR of reactive current through transformer windings without correction.
