**PL2-3North — Reactive Demand and Harmonic Stress**

The reactive profile on PL2-3North is dominant and continuous. In the baseline view, kVAR average is 164.1, with a maximum of 373.0 kVAR. That maximum is the number that drives equipment sizing — it's the peak reactive demand your infrastructure has to absorb, and it's happening regularly, not as a single exceptional event. The M-view (active-reactive-harmonic) confirms a 333 kVAR label on the reactive trace, which is consistent with the BT header reading at a slightly different display scale. For sizing purposes, the 373.0 kVAR MAX from the BT header is the conservative anchor.

The minimum power factor floor recorded in the M-view is 49.12 percent — that's not a header artifact, that's a confirmed field measurement. It means during peak reactive surge events, nearly half of the apparent power flowing through this transformer is doing no real work. Pair that with an average power factor of 60.7 percent and you have a field that is structurally inefficient at every moment of its operation, with periodic spikes into severe inefficiency territory.

Harmonics are present and persistent. The iTHD average is 5.2 percent, with a maximum of 10.7 percent. That 10.7 percent peak crosses the threshold where harmonic heating begins to meaningfully affect transformer core losses and where drive and control electronics experience elevated stress voltages. This isn't a catastrophic harmonic environment — but it's an additive burden on top of an already thermally stressed transformer. In an untuned field, harmonics and reactive demand don't stay flat — they tend to build as equipment ages and load mix shifts.
