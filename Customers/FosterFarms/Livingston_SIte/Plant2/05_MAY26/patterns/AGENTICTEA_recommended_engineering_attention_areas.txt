The primary engineering action for transformer AGENTICTEA at this time is data collection. Once field data is available, the following specific areas warrant engineering review:

**Reactive demand profile:** The kVAR MAX value will drive MPTS unit count. If AGENTICTEA's reactive demand peaks above 400 kVAR, a single H490 may be insufficient and a second unit should be evaluated. Right-sizing matters — over-correction and under-correction both carry operational consequences.

**Voltage regulation margin:** The V-view waveform should be reviewed for repeating triangular dips below the 277-volt line-to-neutral nominal. If those dips correlate with inductive load switching events — compressor start cycles, conveyor acceleration, HVAC staging — that is direct evidence of field stress that MPTS correction will help stabilize.

**Thermal burden trajectory:** In an untuned field, BTU-per-hour thermal burden is not static. It tends to increase as load grows and as ambient temperatures change with the season. Engineering attention should include a trend review: is thermal burden accelerating? If so, the cost of delay compounds each month correction is deferred.

**Outage correlation:** Once imaging is available, gold-streak outage events should be identified and their timing correlated with load events or grid disturbances. Recurring outages tied to reactive demand spikes are a correctable condition with MPTS in place.

AGENTICTEA's data gap is the engineering priority. Close it before the next analysis cycle.
