Reactive demand is pulling 373 kVAR at peak. Power factor is averaging 60.7 percent with floor events near 49 percent. And one complete outage event — roughly 60 to 90 minutes of downtime — has already occurred within this dataset window.

Each one of those conditions individually is manageable. Together, in an untuned field, they interact and amplify each other. The reactive burden drives higher apparent current — which drives higher thermal losses — which drives higher cooling demand — which drives higher operating cost. The voltage dips from inductive load surges stress motor insulation and drive input stages. The harmonic content adds additional heating to the transformer core. And without MPTS correction assigning a tuning profile, the field's efficiency baseline tends to drift downward month over month, not stabilize.

It's a reliability and risk management conversation. The 277,688 BTU per hour of thermal burden isn't sitting in an abstract electrical system. It's sitting in the windings of motors, the contactors of compressors, the input capacitors of variable frequency drives. Every hour of untuned operation at this thermal level is an hour of accelerated wear that doesn't reverse itself when the bill gets paid. It accumulates — and it eventually presents as an unplanned failure at the worst possible moment.

The annualized waste estimate of approximately $213,478 per year — roughly $6,638 per month — is the visible cost. The invisible cost is the compressed equipment lifecycle and the deferred maintenance liability building in the background.
