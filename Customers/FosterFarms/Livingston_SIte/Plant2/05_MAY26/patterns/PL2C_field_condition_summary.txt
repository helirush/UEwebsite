**Transformer: PL2-2Center (PL2C) — Untuned Field | Foster Farms, Livingston HQ**

PL2-2Center is a 2,500 kVA transformer serving the heart of a large food processing and cold storage operation, and its energy field right now is carrying a load it was never optimized to carry efficiently. The measured power factor averages 83.9 percent — meaning for every dollar of productive work this transformer enables, roughly sixteen cents of capacity is being consumed by reactive current that does no useful work. The field is untuned, which means that reactive burden isn't being corrected — it's accumulating silently, hour after hour, day after day.

Peak demand on this transformer is running at 551.3 kilowatts, against a daily operational target of 434 kVA. That gap between what the field is being asked to move and what it's doing it with tells you something important: the transformer is not overloaded in the traditional sense, but it is working inefficiently. The 311.0 kVAR reactive maximum means that at peak, nearly as much reactive current is moving through your transformer windings as real productive current — and that reactive current generates heat, accelerates insulation wear, and costs money every hour it goes uncorrected.

The financial picture is direct. Estimated energy waste runs approximately $3,802 per dataset period, projecting to roughly $75,356 per year in direct reactive losses. When you add the thermal burden and cooling overhead this reactive field is generating, the estimated total wasted reaches approximately $123,026 per year. That's the cost of running an untuned field on a 2,500 kVA transformer in a production-critical food processing facility. Without a tuning assignment, this number doesn't stay flat — heat burden tends to rise month over month as insulation degrades and field efficiency drifts downward.
