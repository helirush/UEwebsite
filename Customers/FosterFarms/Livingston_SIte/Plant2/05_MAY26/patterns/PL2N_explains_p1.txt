Let's talk about what's happening on your PL2-3North transformer — and why it matters. This is a 2500 kVA transformer feeding one of your primary production circuits here at Livingston. On paper, it has plenty of capacity — but what we're measuring in this field isn't a capacity problem. It's an efficiency problem. A significant one. Your average power factor across the full observation window is 60.7 percent. At its worst moments — and we captured them — it drops below 50 percent. What that means in plain terms is that more than half the current flowing through your conductors, your switchgear, and your transformer windings at those moments is doing zero productive work. It's reactive current — magnetizing motors, energizing coil fields, and generating heat. You're paying for it in your electricity bill, and you're paying for it again in the cooling load required to manage the heat it produces. The numbers are concrete. Peak reactive demand hits 373.0 kVAR. Your thermal burden is running at 277,688 BTU per hour. Your cooling system is spending an estimated $8.16 every hour — roughly $71,000 a year — just managing the heat that reactive current creates. Add it all together and the total annualized waste on this circuit alone is approximately $213,000 per year. The voltage picture adds another layer. Your field runs above 480V nominal most of the time — up to 503 volts — which keeps insulation systems in the upper end of their design range. And during production cycling, we're seeing recurring voltage dip events that pull the field down toward 471 volts. VFDs and premium motors feel every one of those swings. One H490 MPTS unit addresses the reactive burden at its source. Projected power factor after correction: 99.5 percent. Direct waste recovery: approximately $131,870 per year. Planning-level investment: approximately $200,000 installed. That's a strong case for stewardship of this circuit. ---
