Let's talk about PL2-2Center — your 2,500 kVA transformer at the Livingston facility. This transformer is running an untuned energy field right now, and the data tells a very clear story about what that's costing you. Your average power factor is 83.9 percent. That means roughly sixteen percent of every amp moving through those windings is reactive current — current that generates heat, occupies capacity, and does no productive work. At peak, your reactive demand reaches 311 kVAR. That's not a spike — that's a sustained condition your transformer is absorbing every single production day. The financial consequence is direct. Estimated energy waste from reactive losses alone projects to roughly $75,000 per year. Add the thermal burden — your transformer is radiating about 158,890 BTUs per hour in waste heat — and the cooling cost to manage that heat adds another estimated $41,867 per year. Total estimated annual waste on PL2-2Center runs approximately $123,000. And because this is a 24/7 cold storage and processing facility, that accumulation never stops. Your voltage field tells a supporting story. Average voltage is 482.7 volts, but the envelope is wide and continuously active. We confirmed one full field interruption during the period — voltage dropped to zero for an estimated 5 to 15 minutes. When power came back, the reconnection inrush hit 520 amps on a field with no reactive compensation standing by to cushion it. Every motor, drive, and contactor on that circuit felt it. The fix is one H490 MPTS unit — 400 kVAR of reactive compensation, matched precisely to your 311 kVAR peak. Planning-level investment: approximately $200,000 installed. At $123,000 in annual estimated waste, you're looking at a planning-level payback in the range of 18 to 24 months — and a transformer that runs cooler, more stable, and with meaningful capacity headroom from that point forward. ---
