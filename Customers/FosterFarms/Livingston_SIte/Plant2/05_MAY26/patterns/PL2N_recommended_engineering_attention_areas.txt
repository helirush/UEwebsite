**PL2-3North — Engineering Attention Areas**

Four areas warrant focused engineering attention on PL2-3North before and concurrent with any MPTS deployment conversation.

First, the outage event. A confirmed 60-to-90-minute full field collapse at approximately the 260324 timestamp needs root-cause investigation. In a cold storage and food processing facility, that duration of power interruption has product safety and regulatory implications. Whether this was a utility event, an internal fault, or a protection relay operation — the root cause should be documented and addressed independently of energy correction work.

Second, voltage envelope management. The field is running above nominal average (484 volts against a 480-volt reference) while also producing repeated dips to approximately 471 volts during load cycles. An engineering review of transformer tap position and utility voltage coordination would be appropriate. MPTS correction will improve reactive-driven voltage instability, but the above-nominal average baseline should be evaluated separately.

Third, harmonic audit. The 10.7 percent iTHD peak warrants a dedicated harmonic survey to identify the source loads — likely variable frequency drives or nonlinear switching loads — and confirm that transformer derating for harmonic content is adequate. If the transformer is not already derated for the observed harmonic level, that's an additional thermal risk factor that compounds the existing 277,688 BTU per hour burden.

Fourth, cooling system capacity review. At 40.5 kilowatts of cooling load and $8.16 per hour in cooling costs, the thermal management infrastructure tied to this transformer is carrying a significant ongoing burden. A review of cooling system capacity, setpoints, and equipment condition would confirm whether current cooling assets are adequate or approaching their operational limits.
