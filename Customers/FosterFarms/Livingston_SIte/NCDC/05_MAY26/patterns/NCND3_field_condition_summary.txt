**Transformer: NCND3 (NCDC3 NorthE — 2500 kVA) | Foster Farms Livingston HQ, Livingston, CA | May 2026**

The NCND3 transformer is feeding a continuously active, heavy-load industrial field — food processing and cold storage at Foster Farms Livingston — and the May 2026 data shows exactly what you'd expect from an untuned field running full production cycles seven days a week. The power factor is holding at 84.6% on average, meaning roughly fifteen cents of every dollar of electrical capacity being pulled through this transformer is doing no useful work. It is pure reactive drag — kVAR circulating through windings, conductors, and the utility supply without converting to production output.

Peak demand reached 738.2 kilowatts during the month, and the reactive demand peaked at 379.4 kVAR. The supply target ceiling sits at 664 kVA, and the field operates below that ceiling for approximately seventy to seventy-five percent of operating intervals — but the reactive burden is degrading efficiency even when load is moderate. That 84.6% power factor is the core inefficiency signal here.

The voltage supply from Merced Irrigation District is remarkably clean and stable — 480.1V average across the month, riding a tight band up to 497.2V — which is a real operational asset. But two confirmed complete power interruption events during May (near the fourteenth and seventeenth of the month) brought voltage to zero and introduced inrush stress on motors and transformer cores. Those events are the voltage-side concern.

The thermal burden on this transformer is running at 309,876 BTU per hour — a meaningful heat load that feeds directly into cooling overhead. Total estimated waste across all cost categories is running at approximately $234,907 per year. For a 2500 kVA asset this size, that waste stream is addressable — and MPTS correction is the direct path to closing it.
