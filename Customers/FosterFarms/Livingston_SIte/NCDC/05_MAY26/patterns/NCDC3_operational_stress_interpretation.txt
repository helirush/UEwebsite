**NCDC3 — North E Transformer | Operational Stress Interpretation | May 2026**

Pull the individual findings together and what emerges is a clear picture of a transformer operating under compounding, layered stress — and doing so continuously, without seasonal relief, because Foster Farms' cold chain and production loads don't follow a conventional operating calendar.

The thermal burden is the most immediate physical consequence of the reactive and harmonic conditions described above. At 309,876 BTU per hour, NCDC3 is radiating a tremendous amount of heat — heat generated not by productive work, but by the circulation of reactive current through transformer windings and facility conductors. That heat has to go somewhere. The cooling system is pulling 45.2 kilowatts of electrical capacity and costing approximately $8.87 per hour just to manage the thermal output of this uncompensated field. That is not a fixed infrastructure cost — it is a direct consequence of the reactive inefficiency, and it would be reduced substantially with compensation in place.

The electric operations pattern reinforces the stress picture. With the kVA demand band sitting above the 664 kVA supply target for roughly 80 to 85 percent of May, this transformer is operating in a sustained overload-adjacent condition relative to its ideal operating window. That sustained above-target operation means the conductors are warmer than they need to be, the transformer core is working harder than necessary, and the insulation systems on connected equipment are aging faster than their design life assumes. In a food processing environment where equipment reliability is directly linked to product safety and throughput, accelerated aging is a financial risk that shows up — eventually — as unplanned downtime.

The single outage event observed this month — approximately 60 to 90 minutes — is a data point worth filing. One outage in a month is not an emergency pattern. But in combination with voltage dips to 464 volts, a persistent reactive burden, and harmonic peaks reaching 11.2 percent iTHD, it suggests a field that is operating close to its stress boundaries with no margin reserve. Fields operating without compensation in this condition do not naturally improve. Without tuning assigned, the trajectory is drift — slowly worsening power factor, slowly accumulating thermal damage, and slowly increasing the probability of an unplanned event at the worst possible moment.

The estimated annual cost of this uncompensated condition is approximately $234,907 per year, or roughly $12,393 per month. That figure represents reactive losses, thermal burden overhead, and cooling costs — money leaving the facility every month in exchange for no productive output.
