**NCND1 — West11c Transformer | Recommended Engineering Attention Areas**

Several areas warrant focused engineering follow-up based on the West11c field analysis.

**Priority 1 — Root cause of voltage collapse events.** The 9.7-volt minimum recorded in the dataset and the confirmed outage or severe disruption events around May 8th through 9th and May 23rd require investigation. Whether these represent upstream supply interruptions, internal switching events, or downstream fault conditions, understanding the root cause is essential before MPTS installation proceeds. Capacitor banks installed into an electrically unstable bus environment face stress risks that must be assessed.

**Priority 2 — MPTS sizing confirmation and placement.** Two H490 units at 400 kVAR each provide 800 kVAR of total correction capacity against a 445.5 kVAR maximum reactive demand. This provides appropriate headroom. Placement should be confirmed by a site engineering study that accounts for harmonic content, bus topology, and the location of major inductive loads — particularly the refrigeration compressor motors and any variable frequency drive systems on this bus.

**Priority 3 — Motor and drive fleet condition assessment.** Given the confirmed near-zero voltage collapse event and the sustained over-voltage operation above nominal, a thermal imaging and insulation resistance survey of the motor fleet served by West11c is warranted. Variable frequency drives and soft starters connected to this bus should be reviewed for fault logs that may capture prior trip events correlated to the disruption windows identified in this analysis.

**Priority 4 — Demand charge exposure review.** With a peak demand of 551 kilowatts and a supply kVA of 586, the facility may be carrying demand charge exposure tied to apparent power rather than real power depending on tariff structure. Correcting the power factor through MPTS installation may reduce billed demand in addition to reducing reactive energy losses — a financial benefit that should be quantified in the formal engineering study.
