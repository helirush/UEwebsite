**NCDC3 — North E Transformer | Field Condition Summary | May 2026**

What you're looking at with NCDC3 is a transformer that is working harder than it should be — and has been doing so across the entire May dataset, all 744 hours of it. This is Foster Farms' North E transformer, and the field it's carrying tells a story of an industrial load environment pushing reactive drag, thermal accumulation, and voltage variability simultaneously.

The headline numbers set the tone immediately. Peak demand reached 738.2 kilowatts. The transformer's operational band spent the overwhelming majority of May — roughly 80 to 85 percent of the month — running above the 664 kVA supply target. That means the field was consistently overdriving its designed operating window. In a food processing facility with continuous refrigeration, rendering, and HVAC loads, that's not a surprise. But it is a problem that compounds quietly over time.

Power factor is averaging 84.6 percent. That means for every dollar of electrical power being drawn through this transformer, roughly fifteen cents is doing no productive work. It is reactive current — circulating energy that heats conductors, loads transformer cores, and drives cooling costs upward without contributing a single unit of useful output. At a kVAR average of 296.8 and a peak reactive demand of 379.4 kVAR, the reactive burden on NCDC3 is not a rounding error. It is a structural feature of how this field currently operates.

The annual energy waste tally from the BT header comes in at approximately $234,907 per year — or roughly $12,393 per month — driven by a combination of reactive losses, thermal burden running at 309,876 BTU per hour, and cooling costs sitting at about $8.87 per hour drawing 45.2 kilowatts of cooling capacity continuously. That cooling load alone represents a compounding overhead cost that the facility is paying every hour this field runs uncompensated.

This is an untuned field. Without correction assigned, the efficiency trajectory drifts downward and the thermal burden tends to grow month over month as equipment ages under sustained reactive stress.
