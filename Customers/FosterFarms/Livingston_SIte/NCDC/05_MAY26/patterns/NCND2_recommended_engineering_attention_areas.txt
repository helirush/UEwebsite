**NCDC2 — Recommended Engineering Attention Areas | May 2026**

**1. Outage Root Cause Investigation.** The 4 to 5 complete field collapse events observed across May — each reaching a documented 0.0-volt minimum — require root cause investigation before MPTS installation. If these outages originate upstream at the Merced Irrigation District feed point, the corrective path is a utility coordination conversation. If they originate internally — protective relay operation, main breaker trips, or overcurrent events driven by inrush peaks — the MPTS sizing and integration design should account for that dynamic. Installing a correction system into a field with unresolved intermittent collapse events adds commissioning risk and could complicate performance baseline establishment.

**2. Thermal Burden Monitoring and Trend Logging.** The 167,322 BTU per hour thermal burden should be compared against this transformer's nameplate thermal rating and cooling class. If May's thermal burden is running close to the transformer's continuous thermal limit, the summer months — with Central Valley ambient temperatures and increased refrigeration cycling — will push that burden higher. Engineering should establish a thermal monitoring point on the transformer tank or cooling fins to track thermal margin through peak summer load conditions. This data also supports the MPTS business case by documenting pre-correction thermal state.

**3. Harmonic Assessment Prior to MPTS Commissioning.** The persistent harmonic background observed in the M view warrants a dedicated harmonic survey prior to MPTS commissioning. If VFDs or rectified power supply loads are contributing significant 5th or 7th harmonic content to the field, the H490 configuration should include harmonic filtering capability or detuned reactor protection to prevent resonance conditions at the correction capacitor bank. A harmonic assessment now prevents a potentially costly design revision after installation.

**4. Motor and Drive Condition Assessment.** Given the repetitive inrush events from load cycling and the multiple full-field-collapse recovery cycles documented in May, engineering attention to the condition of the largest connected motors and VFD input stages is warranted. Specifically, motors with 200 horsepower or greater ratings — common in cold storage compressor and rendering system service — should be assessed for insulation resistance trends using polarization index testing. Drives with capacitive input stages should be inspected for DC bus capacitor health. These are the components most vulnerable to cumulative inrush and restart stress, and their condition directly affects the reliability of the production environment that MPTS correction is designed to protect and optimize.
