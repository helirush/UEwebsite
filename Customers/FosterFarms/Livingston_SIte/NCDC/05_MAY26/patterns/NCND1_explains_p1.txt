Let me walk you through what we're seeing on NCDC1 at your Livingston facility — and it's a field that's telling a very clear story once you know what to look at. This transformer is a 2,500 kVA unit serving what is clearly a continuous operation. Food processing and cold storage don't take weekends off, and neither does this transformer. Looking at the full month of May, the load traces are elevated above the 586 kVA daily supply target for roughly 85 to 90 percent of available hours. This machine is working hard, continuously. The challenge is that it's doing that work inefficiently. The average power factor is 85.0% — which means for every dollar of electricity the utility is pushing through that transformer, about 15 cents is circulating as reactive energy. It's not producing anything useful. It's generating heat, stressing the windings, and loading your supply circuit with invisible burden. At its worst moments in the month, that power factor dropped to 70.0%. At 70%, nearly a third of your electricity is wasted. The numbers that come out of that inefficiency are concrete. Roughly $11,044 in waste for the May dataset. That annualizes to approximately $209,767 per year. And on top of that, the transformer is running at a thermal burden of about 277,515 BTU per hour — heat you're paying about $7.96 every hour to remove with active cooling. That's 40.5 kilowatts of cooling load fighting heat that MPTS correction would largely eliminate at the source. The voltage picture adds another layer of concern. Repeated dips below the 480-volt nominal — some reaching 10 to 20 volts below — are creating real cumulative stress on your motor equipment, drives, and contactors. And two unplanned power interruptions in May — totaling roughly 1.5 to 2 hours of downtime — are worth investigating in a facility where cold chain integrity and food safety depend on uninterrupted supply. This field needs a tuning assignment. Without one, the reactive burden grows and the waste figure climbs. ---
