**NCDC3 — NorthE Transformer | Recommended Engineering Attention Areas | May 2026**

**Priority 1 — Reactive compensation assignment.** NCDC3 NorthE requires immediate evaluation for MPTS tuning assignment. The 379.4 kVAR MAX and 296.8 kVAR sustained average represent a persistent, unrelieved reactive burden that is generating measurable financial waste and active thermal stress every hour of every day. One H490 unit — approximately $200,000 installed as a planning estimate — is the indicated correction. Engineering should confirm bus topology, protection coordination, and harmonic filter integration requirements before installation.

**Priority 2 — Voltage collapse event root-cause investigation.** The recorded 0.0-volt minimum on NCDC3 requires investigation. Two power interruption events were detected in the BT view — one near May 14-15 and one near May 17 — with estimated combined duration of approximately 3 to 6 minutes. In a food processing and cold-chain environment, identifying whether these events originated on the utility side (Merced Irrigation District feed) or on the facility side (protection device operation, switching event, or fault-initiated interruption) is critical for risk mitigation planning. If facility-side, the root cause needs to be understood before MPTS installation to avoid exposing new compensation equipment to abnormal switching transients.

**Priority 3 — Harmonic content monitoring.** The 5.7 percent average iTHD with spikes to 11.2 percent warrants ongoing monitoring and should be characterized more fully during the MPTS engineering assessment. If VFD-connected loads are significant contributors to the harmonic signature on this bus, the H490 specification should confirm adequate harmonic filtering capability or a supplemental passive filter should be evaluated in parallel with reactive compensation.

**Priority 4 — Thermal inspection of connected motor assets.** The 309,876 BTU per hour thermal burden operating continuously through May — compounded by the two outage-and-restart inrush events — indicates that connected motor windings and cable insulation in this zone are under accelerated aging stress. An infrared inspection of panel connections, motor terminal boxes, and transformer secondary connections is recommended before summer peak loads arrive. Finding a developing hot spot in June is far less costly than an unplanned motor failure in August when the Central Valley ambient temperature is at its annual peak and replacement lead times are longest.

**Priority 5 — Continuous field monitoring through summer.** As noted in the operational interpretation, thermal burden in an untuned field tends to drift upward month over month. The May baseline established here should be used as the reference for June and July monitoring. If kVAR average rises above 320 kVAR or cooling costs rise above $9.50 per hour during summer months, that trend should accelerate the MPTS installation timeline. Do not allow this field to continue uncompensated through a full California Central Valley summer.
