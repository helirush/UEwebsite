**NCND2 — Field Condition Summary | Foster Farms Livingston HQ | May 2026**

NCND2 serves the South D transformer position — a 2,500 kVA unit at the Foster Farms Livingston headquarters facility. This is a large, consequential transformer serving what the load profile clearly identifies as a heavy industrial food processing and cold storage operation. The field is currently untuned, meaning no MPTS correction equipment is in place, and the electromagnetic environment is running as-delivered from the Merced Irrigation District utility supply.

The overall condition of NCND2's field in May 2026 is best described as functionally operational but thermally and electromagnetically burdened. The facility is getting the work done — motors are running, compressors are cycling, processing loads are meeting production demand — but the field is doing it at a measurable cost premium that shows up clearly in the data. Power factor averaging 93.0% means roughly 7% of the electrical current circulating through this transformer's windings every hour is doing no useful work. It is generating heat, stressing insulation, and imposing a reactive burden that the facility is absorbing silently through elevated operating costs and accelerated equipment wear.

The 819 kVA supply target established in the BT view is being met and regularly exceeded during weekday production cycles, confirming this transformer is genuinely loaded. Peak demand reaches 1,001.6 kW — a real industrial draw. Against that backdrop, the thermal burden reading of 167,322 BTU/hr tells us the transformer core and windings are running warm, and that warmth is not entirely attributable to real production work. A portion of it — the reactive portion — is waste heat generated by the uncompensated inductive loads this field is carrying.

Total estimated annual waste from this single transformer: approximately $127,148 per year. That number will be the central reference point for the correction investment conversation.
