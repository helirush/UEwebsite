The reactive power burden on NCND2 runs at an average of 244.9 kVAR every hour of operation, with peaks hitting 351.6 kVAR. Reactive power is electrical current that circulates through the transformer and wiring without doing any productive work — it just generates heat and occupies capacity. At 93.0 percent power factor, about 7 percent of all current through NCND2 is reactive. That reactive current causes 167,322 BTU per hour of waste heat, and the facility spends about $4.82 per hour in cooling costs to remove it. There's also harmonic distortion — averaging 5.7 percent with peaks to 10.6 percent — that adds additional heating stress to the transformer core. **What MPTS correction would do:**
One H490 unit — Unity's 400 kVAR correction system — is the right size for NCND2. It fully covers the 351.6 kVAR peak reactive demand. After installation, power factor would project to approximately 99.5 percent, the reactive burden would be near zero, the transformer would run measurably cooler, and the cooling system would have less work to do.
