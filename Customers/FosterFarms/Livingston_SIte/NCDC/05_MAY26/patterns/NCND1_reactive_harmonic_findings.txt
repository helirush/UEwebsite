**NCDC1 (West11c) — Reactive and Harmonic Findings | May 2026**

The M view on NCDC1 lays out a three-layer stress picture — reactive power, current amplitude, and harmonic content — and none of the three layers tell a comfortable story.

Start with the reactive layer. The kVAR average from the BT header is 267.9 kVAR, and the kVAR MAX is 445.5 kVAR. That maximum figure is the critical number for sizing — it represents the peak reactive demand this transformer must absorb at any given operational moment. Every kVAR circulating in that field is doing no useful work. It is heat, it is current, and it is transformer stress. At 267.9 kVAR average, roughly 30 to 35 percent of the apparent power flowing through this transformer is reactive burden, not productive output.

The amperage picture reinforces this. The M-view red amperage trace runs dense and elevated throughout the month, with a recorded maximum of 701.2 amps. On a 2,500 kVA transformer at 480 volts, the nameplate current ceiling is approximately 3,005 amps three-phase — so 701.2 amps is well within nameplate limits in aggregate. But the sustained density of that amperage trace, combined with the reactive component, means the transformer is carrying significant I-squared-R losses continuously. That converts directly into heat — which is exactly what the 277,515 BTU per hour thermal burden figure reflects.

Harmonic content on NCDC1 is measured at 6.0% iTHD average current harmonics, with a recorded maximum of 10.7% iTHD. The IEEE 519 standard recommends total harmonic distortion stay at or below 5.0% for most industrial bus configurations. At 6.0% average, this field is running above that threshold continuously. At peak moments of 10.7%, the harmonic stress nearly doubles. Those harmonics generate additional eddy-current heating in the transformer core and create interference that degrades power quality across downstream connected equipment.

The combination — 85.0% power factor, 445.5 kVAR peak reactive demand, and 6.0% harmonic content — is a compounding stress signature. Each element makes the others worse. MPTS correction addresses the reactive layer directly and reduces the current amplitude that feeds the harmonic-driven heating cycle.
