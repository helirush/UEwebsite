**NCDC3 — North E Transformer | Voltage Stability Findings | May 2026**

The voltage picture on NCDC3 is one of persistent variability rather than acute crisis — but persistent variability in a cold chain environment carries its own category of risk, and it deserves to be named plainly.

The V view shows a measured average voltage of 480.1 volts across the full month. That average sounds healthy — it's right at nominal. But the envelope surrounding that average tells a different story. The Max Voltage callout reaches 497.2 volts. The Min Voltage callout drops to 464.0 volts. That is a 33-volt swing peak-to-trough on a 480-volt nominal system — a spread of approximately 6.9 percent. A field that breathes 33 volts across a single month is a field that is not holding steady. It is responding to load swings, and its lowest moments are pulling equipment below the voltage floor where motors and control systems operate safely.

That 464-volt minimum is the number worth dwelling on. A drop to 464 volts represents a 3.3 percent undervoltage excursion from nominal. On its own, a single 464-volt event is manageable. But these dips are not isolated. The triangular drop signatures visible across the V view — those brief, pointed downward excursions that recover quickly — are distributed across the full 31-day month. They are not clustered in one bad week. They are woven into the normal operational rhythm of this transformer. That tells me they are tied to recurring load cycling events: motor starts, compressor staging, rendering equipment transitions. Wherever the operational calendar repeats, these voltage dips repeat with it.

For a food processing facility like Foster Farms, the implications are concrete. Refrigeration compressors operating during repeated undervoltage events experience reduced motor torque, elevated winding current draw to compensate, and accelerated insulation degradation. Variable-frequency drives and soft starters — common throughout cold storage environments — are sensitive to input voltage quality. Sustained operation through repeated 464–470-volt excursions adds cumulative stress to insulation systems that is invisible until it isn't. The device-health risk here is assessed at moderate-to-high. Not an emergency shutdown scenario today — but an active degradation force that is working against equipment longevity every time the field dips below nominal.
