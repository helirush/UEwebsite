**NCDC1 — Reactive and Harmonic Stress Profile — May 2026 — Untuned**

The M view for NCDC1 delivers a clear, consistent message: this is a field under sustained reactive stress, with harmonic content layered on top, and no correction mechanism in place to absorb either.

On a 2,500 kVA transformer, 701 amps is not a transformer-level emergency — but it's a meaningful current load, and a significant portion of that current is reactive, meaning it's doing no useful work. It's circulating, generating heat, and loading every conductor between the transformer secondary and the end-use equipment.

The kVAR trace in green is the defining feature of this M view. The Max Reactive callout reads 302 kVAR — and critically, this reactive ceiling doesn't relax. It doesn't drop significantly overnight. It doesn't ease on weekends. The kVAR floor stays elevated through the full monthly period. That behavior pattern is the signature of permanently connected reactive loads — refrigeration compressor banks, large HVAC motor systems, cold storage equipment that runs 24 hours a day, 365 days a year. These aren't production-cycle loads that you can manage by scheduling. They're baseline loads, always drawing reactive current, always burning thermal margin.

While harmonic distortion at these levels is not immediately critical, in an untuned field it accumulates as an additional degradation factor month after month.
