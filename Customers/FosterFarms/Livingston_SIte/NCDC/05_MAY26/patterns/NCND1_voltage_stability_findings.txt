**NCDC1 (West11c) — Voltage Stability Findings | May 2026**

The voltage field on NCDC1 is not failing — but it is under pressure, and the V view makes that pressure visible in detail. The measured average voltage is 481.5 volts, sitting just marginally above the 480V line-to-line nominal. At first glance, that sounds acceptable. But the story is in the shape of the trace, not just the average.

The voltage band across the month spans from a ceiling near 500 volts down to a floor near 460 volts. That 40-volt spread tells you this field is not a stable, well-regulated supply. It is a reactive-burdened field breathing in and out with load cycling, and the triangular voltage-drop signatures visible throughout the trace confirm that inductive load cycling — compressors, conveyors, motor starts — is the dominant driver.

Those triangular drops are not isolated events. They cluster most densely in the 260502 through 260515 window, appearing with enough regularity and consistency to tell you that whatever equipment is driving them is operating on a repeating duty cycle throughout the production shift. And when voltage dips 10 to 20 volts below the 480V nominal — even briefly — that is enough to stress motor windings, cause variable frequency drives to compensate or fault, and push contactors toward dropout threshold.

The two significant disturbance events are worth calling out directly. The event near 260508 is the more severe — the blue voltage trace drops sharply and dramatically, well below the nominal line, consistent with either a supply interruption or a major load switching event. The event near 260523 is less severe but still visible as a distinct voltage disruption.

For a food processing facility of this scale, with refrigeration compressors and processing equipment on continuous duty, repeated under-voltage excursions accelerate insulation aging in motor windings, reduce variable frequency drive headroom, and increase the probability of nuisance trips during peak load intervals. The device-health risk here is rated moderate-to-high based on voltage pattern alone — before harmonics are even factored in.
