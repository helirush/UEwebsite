Let's walk through what your NCND3 transformer is showing us for May. This is your NCDC3 NorthE — a 2500 kVA asset at the Foster Farms Livingston facility — and it is running a continuous, full-production field every single day of the month. Food processing and cold storage don't take weekends off, and the data confirms that. The reactive demand is present twenty-four hours a day, seven days a week, with no meaningful recovery. The core issue is power factor. You're averaging 84.6 percent — and that means roughly fifteen percent of every amp flowing through that transformer is circulating as reactive current that never converts to useful production work. The reactive peak hit 379.4 kVAR during May, and that reactive overhead is generating heat — 309,876 BTU per hour of thermal burden — that then requires 45.2 kilowatts of active cooling to manage. You're paying approximately $8.87 every hour just to handle the heat consequences of inefficiency. Annualized, cooling overhead alone is tracking toward about $77,744 per year. Total estimated waste across all categories runs to approximately $234,907 per year. On the voltage side, Merced Irrigation District is delivering a clean supply — 480.1 volts average in a tight band. But two complete power interruptions happened during May — near the fourteenth and seventeenth — confirmed by the voltage collapsing to zero and two outage markers visible in the data. Each re-energization after a full interruption stresses compressor motors and transformer cores with high inrush currents. On a continuously loaded field like this, those events accumulate. One H490 MPTS unit — rated at 400 kVAR — is sized right for this transformer. At a planning-level installed investment of approximately $200,000, the correction targets the full waste pool and projects the power factor to 99.5 percent. The math is clear and the payback path is strong for a seven-day continuous operation. ---
