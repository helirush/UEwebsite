What I can walk through is the correction logic that will apply once data arrives. The MPTS sizing rule is straightforward: take the kVAR MAX reading from the BT header, divide by 400, and round up. That gives you the number of H490 units required to bring the field to near-unity correction. Each H490 unit carries a planning-level installed cost estimate of approximately $200,000, with final pricing dependent on engineering scope and site conditions.

The benefit side of that equation flows from three directions simultaneously. First, reactive losses drop — the circulating kVAR current that was heating conductors and windings is absorbed by the MPTS field at the point of generation. Second, thermal burden decreases — transformer winding temperatures fall, cooling loads lighten, and insulation life extends. Third, apparent power draw on the utility side shrinks — the transformer is no longer requesting reactive support from the grid, which typically reduces demand charges and in some rate structures eliminates reactive power penalty surcharges entirely.

In untuned fields that have been running without correction for extended periods, the post-MPTS power factor correction to 99.5 percent — versus a typical uncorrected baseline in the 70 to 85 percent range — represents a very substantial reclamation of transformer capacity. That recovered capacity can serve additional productive load without requiring additional transformer infrastructure.
