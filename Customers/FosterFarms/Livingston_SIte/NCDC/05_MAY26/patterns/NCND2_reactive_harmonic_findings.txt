**NCDC2 — Reactive and Harmonic Findings | May 2026**

The reactive picture for NCDC2 is clear and consistent across both the BT and M views. The field carries an average reactive load of 244.9 kVAR throughout May, with a peak reactive demand of 351.6 kVAR recorded in the BT header — this is the sizing-critical number for any MPTS correction planning. The average power factor of 93.0 percent, while respectable on paper, means that nearly one in fourteen kilovolt-amperes flowing through this transformer is doing no useful work. It is reactive magnetizing current — the kind that loads transformer cores, heats conductors, and drives up the thermal burden without producing a single kilowatt of productive output.

The M view — the active-reactive-harmonic view — reinforces this picture with additional texture. The red amperage trace in the M view is dense and highly dynamic, with a maximum amperage reading of 984.1 amps against a reported average of 807.3 amps. That gap — nearly 177 amps between average and maximum — confirms aggressive load cycling behavior: compressors staging on and off, large motors starting under load, refrigeration systems responding to thermal demand changes. The green KVAR trace pulses in synchrony with the amperage cycling, which is a significant finding. It tells us reactive demand is not a fixed background component that can be corrected with a static capacitor bank. It is dynamically coupled to load switching events, which means effective correction requires a system capable of responding to reactive demand changes in real time — exactly what the MPTS architecture is designed to do.

The purple harmonics layer in the M view runs as a consistently present thin band at the lower register of the chart throughout all 31 days. The harmonics are not absent, and their persistence across the full month suggests a baseline harmonic environment consistent with variable frequency drives, rectified power supplies, or other nonlinear loads common to food processing and cold storage operations. While the harmonic amplitude does not appear to dominate the field visually, harmonics in this context are additive to the thermal burden — they increase effective conductor heating beyond what the fundamental frequency load alone would produce, and they can accelerate transformer insulation aging when left unaddressed in an untuned field.

The combined effect — 244.9 kVAR average reactive, pulsing reactive demand tied to load cycling, and a persistent harmonic background — produces the thermal burden documented in the BT header: 167,322 BTU per hour. That is not a theoretical number. That is heat being generated inside this transformer and its connected conductors every operating hour, requiring active cooling to manage.
