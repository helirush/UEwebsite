In a cold-chain food processing environment, a voltage collapse, even if it lasts only seconds, triggers compressor bank trip sequences, inrush current spikes on restart, and potential thermal excursion events in motors and drives that were mid-cycle when the collapse occurred.

When voltage sags while reactive current demand is simultaneously elevated, the apparent current draw increases to compensate — which means more heat generated per unit of productive work, more stress on motor windings, and faster insulation aging across every inductive load on this bus.

The voltage band also exhibits fine-grain, persistent perturbation throughout the entire 31-day window.
