**NCDC2-SOUTH-D — June 2026 | Reactive and Harmonic Findings**

The Active-Reactive-Harmonic view for South-D is where the remaining optimization opportunity becomes most concrete. The red amperage trace dominates the TM chart — running at an average of 968.6 amps with a recorded maximum of 2,790 amps. That maximum amperage figure is striking. It represents extreme transient current events, almost certainly large motor starts or compressor load switching. The Max Amperage reference line is drawn at 1,252.8 amps, and the red trace regularly approaches and periodically exceeds that threshold. These are real stress events on the transformer and on the downstream distribution equipment.

The green kVAR trace holds its average at 302.0 kVAR — consistent with the TBT header confirmation. The Max Reactive callout in the chart reads 372 kVAR on the displayed trace, which is directionally consistent with the operational band. However, the TBT header's kVAR MAX of 1,850.8 kVAR tells a different story about the transient peaks — that figure represents extreme reactive demand spikes that the steady-state average masks. The gap between average and maximum reactive demand is a 6-to-1 ratio, which is unusually wide and strongly suggests that the facility has recurring large motor starts or batch-process load events driving short, intense reactive spikes.

Harmonics are relatively contained. The iTHD average reads 5.7%, with a maximum of 10.8% iTHD. The average is just above the IEEE 519 recommended threshold of 5%, which means on a daily average basis this field is running slightly above the conventional guideline. The 10.8% peak events are notable but not severe. The harmonic profile appears distributed across the month rather than concentrated at specific operational windows — which points to variable speed drives and continuously running motor loads as the primary source, rather than batch-process events. This is a manageable harmonic environment, but it bears continued monitoring, particularly as motor loads or VFD configurations change.

The minimum Power Factor reading of 87.94% — visible in the TM chart callout — confirms that during peak reactive demand events, the field quality deteriorates meaningfully below the 92.6% average. That 87.94% floor is the field at its most reactive, and it's the condition the facility experiences during its most operationally intense moments.
