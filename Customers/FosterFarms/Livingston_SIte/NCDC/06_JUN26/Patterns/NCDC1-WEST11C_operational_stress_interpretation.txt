**NCDC1-WEST11C — Operational Stress | June 2026 | Foster Farms, Livingston HQ**

West11C is operating in a demanding industrial environment — poultry processing with cold storage, refrigeration cycling, and continuous motor loads — and the June data shows a field that has been meaningfully stabilized by the existing MPTS investment. But the stress profile still has material teeth.

The thermal burden stands at 273,556 BTU per hour in the tuned baseline state — with a reference baseline of 256,192 BTU per hour noted in the header. The fact that the current thermal reading is slightly above the baseline reference suggests the facility's production load during June was driving higher reactive stress than the design baseline anticipated. That incremental heat — roughly 17,000 additional BTU per hour — is real energy going into transformer iron and copper heating rather than productive work. In a facility that already operates substantial refrigeration infrastructure, every unnecessary BTU of heat generated by the electrical field adds directly to cooling overhead.

The cooling burden reflects this precisely: 40.1 kW of cooling load is attributable to reactive losses, costing an estimated $7.89 per hour and approximately $69,081 per year. That is a continuous, around-the-clock overhead charge. The facility is spending money on refrigeration and HVAC cooling just to manage heat that an optimized power field shouldn't be generating.

The two to three brief outage events observed in the TBT view — each estimated at a few minutes in duration, totaling approximately 10 to 20 minutes across the month — are not cause for alarm. These appear to be controlled switching events or brief load interruptions rather than fault-driven outages. However, their occurrence in the context of the voltage transient profile — particularly that 9.7 V minimum event — warrants logging. If these switching events are coinciding with heavy motor restarts, the combination of transient voltage collapse and reactive surge creates compounded stress on motor insulation and drive electronics.

At 85.3 percent average power factor with a 70.0 percent minimum floor, West11C is delivering meaningful correction — but the gap between 85 and 99.5 percent represents the remaining optimization opportunity. The field is stable. It is not yet precise.
