At a facility running in California's Central Valley heat in June, every BTU per hour of thermal burden removed from NCDC3-North-E is cooling load relief that the facility's HVAC system no longer has to compensate for. The estimated $84,164 per year in cooling overhead compresses meaningfully as the reactive burden drops.

The voltage stability improvement that flows from reactive correction is also material. Reactive compensation at the point of generation reduces the voltage-depressing effect of inductive reactive current on the distribution rail. The frequency and depth of the triangular voltage sags visible in the TV view should diminish as the reactive burden is addressed — delivering a more stable voltage rail for motors, drives, and control systems across the facility.
