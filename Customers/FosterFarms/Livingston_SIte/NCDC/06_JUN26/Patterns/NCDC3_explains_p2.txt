Here's the executive summary on NCDC3. The transformer is tuned and running in a commissioned state. Power factor is averaging 83.7% — which is better than an uncorrected field, but still carries a 372 kilovolt-amperes-reactive peak that is costing real money. Total estimated waste is approximately $252,000 annually at planning-level, with a thermal burden of 333,852 BTU per hour demanding 48.9 kilowatts of active cooling. Voltage is stable — 468 to 495 volts across June — with routine motor-start dips that are individually manageable. The harmonic environment, at 5.8% average and 11.4% peak current total harmonic distortion, co-occurs with the reactive stress events and elevates cumulative equipment aging risk on motor insulation and drive electronics. The MPTS simulation confirms 99.5% power factor as the corrected outcome. Closing the gap from 83.7% to 99.5% on NCDC3 neutralizes the reactive peak, reduces thermal burden, lowers cooling demand, and recovers the estimated waste stream. The four engineering focus areas going forward: reactive compensation sizing review, harmonic spectrum analysis, voltage dip trending, and outage event correlation with the utility feeder logs. The investment is working. Refinement is the next chapter.
