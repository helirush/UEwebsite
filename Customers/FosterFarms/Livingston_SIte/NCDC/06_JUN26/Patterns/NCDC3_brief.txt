**NCDC3 Energy Field Summary Brief — Foster Farms, Livingston HQ | June 2026**
*Tuned Field Optimization Mode | Vision-Only Synthesis* **Field Condition:**
NCDC3 is operating in a tuned, commissioned state on a 2,500 kVA transformer. Peak demand is 664.1 kW, served through a 655 kVA supply target. Power factor averages 83.7%, with reactive demand averaging 308.2 kVAR and peaking at 372.2 kVAR. Total estimated waste is approximately $252,475 per year at planning-level, with a thermal burden of 333,852 BTU per hour and a cooling load of 48.9 kW at roughly $9.61 per hour. **Voltage Stability:**
Voltage holds between 468V and 495V across the full June period — a 27-volt envelope on a 480V nominal system. Periodic triangular dip events are present, distributed across the month, consistent with motor-start and compressor-cycling operations. The 2.5% dip below nominal at 468V is individually manageable but warrants trending given the concurrent harmonic environment. **Reactive and Harmonic Load:**
Reactive draw is continuous and never drops to zero. The harmonic environment averages 5.8% iTHD with peaks at 11.4%, consistent with VFD and switching load signatures. The Power Factor floor observed in TM reaches 80.75% during peak load-switching events, with harmonics and reactive stress co-occurring. **Operational Stress:**
Stress profile is controlled but layered — thermal burden, periodic voltage dips, and harmonic content combine to elevate cumulative equipment aging risk on motor insulation and drive electronics. Five to seven power interruption events totaling roughly 10 to 20 minutes were observed in June. **MPTS Outcome:**
The TS simulation confirms 99.5% power factor at target. Closing the gap from 83.7% to 99.5% substantially neutralizes the 372.2 kVAR reactive peak, reduces conductor heating, lowers the thermal burden, and relieves cooling demand — recovering an estimated $252,475 in annual waste at planning-level. **Engineering Attention:**
Reactive compensation sizing review, harmonic spectrum analysis, voltage dip trend monitoring, and outage event correlation with Merced Irrigation District feeder logs are the four recommended engineering focus areas for NCDC3 optimization continuity.
