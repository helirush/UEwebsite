**NCDC2-SOUTH-D — June 2026 | Operational Stress Interpretation**

Pulling the voltage, reactive, and harmonic findings together into an operational picture, South-D is running a tuned but still-stressed field. The existing MPTS assets have clearly done foundational work — the power factor is above 90%, the voltage average is on nominal, and the production rhythm is disciplined and readable. That's real value delivered from the prior investment. But the stress indicators that remain are layered and interconnected.

The 2,790-amp transient maximum is the sharpest single data point in this picture. Events of that magnitude — even if brief — send electromagnetic shock through the transformer core, through the distribution bus, and into every piece of connected equipment. Combined with the triangular voltage dip pattern and the 1,850.8 kVAR reactive transient peaks, you're looking at a facility where large inductive loads — almost certainly cold storage compressors or rendering process equipment — are creating repeated, high-intensity demand spikes that the current level of reactive compensation is absorbing only partially.

The four to five power interruption events detected in the TBT chart — estimated at 40 to 60 minutes of aggregate downtime for the month — add another layer to the stress picture. Brief outages of this frequency in an industrial food processing environment carry real operational cost: process restarts, product holds, equipment re-sequencing. Whether these events are utility-sourced or internal switching events, they're worth tracking through continued monitoring.

The cooling burden of 31.3 kilowatts and $6.17 per hour is the thermal consequence of the reactive losses and transformer heating that the current optimization gap is generating. That heat is being paid for twice — once in the electricity that creates it, and again in the cooling energy that removes it. At an annualized estimate of approximately $54,000 in cooling costs alone, this is a meaningful and recurring overhead that tighter reactive correction directly reduces.

The overall operational stress level I'd characterize as **moderate-elevated** — managed by existing tuning, but with identifiable and quantifiable gaps that targeted optimization can close.
