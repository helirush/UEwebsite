**NCDC1-WEST11C | Foster Farms, Livingston HQ | June 2026 | Tuned Field Optimization Brief** **Field Condition:**
West11C is a tuned 2500 kVA transformer with MPTS correction deployed and active. The daily target is 585 kVA, with peak demand at 568.3 kW. Average power factor in the tuned baseline is 85.3 percent — meaningfully improved from an uncompensated state, but with a defined optimization gap to the 99.5 percent TS target. Total estimated remaining waste is approximately $207,086 per year. **Voltage Stability:**
Voltage averages 481.3 V — just above nominal. Triangular dip patterns are persistent and distributed across the full month, consistent with compressor cycling and motor switching loads. A recorded minimum event of 9.7 V flags a severe transient that warrants diagnostic investigation. The lower voltage reference of 465 V is being approached in select events, creating cumulative motor and drive stress. **Reactive and Harmonic Profile:**
Reactive demand averages 267.5 kVAR with a peak of 875.9 kVAR. The peak-to-average ratio of roughly 3.3 to 1 indicates the existing correction handles steady-state well but reactive surges still break through. iTHD averages 6.0 percent, maximum 11.0 percent — manageable, secondary to reactive as the primary thermal driver. **Operational Stress:**
Thermal burden is 273,556 BTU per hour — slightly above the 256,192 BTU per hour baseline reference. Cooling overhead is 40.1 kW, costing an estimated $69,081 per year. Two to three brief switching events were observed, totaling an estimated 10 to 20 minutes across the month. **MPTS Optimization Outcome:**
The TS simulation projects power factor to 99.5 percent with reactive average dropping from 267.5 kVAR to approximately 40.2 kVAR — an 85 percent average reactive reduction. This closes the remaining optimization gap and materially reduces thermal and cooling burden. **Engineering Attention Areas:**
Priority focus on reactive peak suppression at high-load events, the 9.7 V transient diagnostic, power factor floor improvement from the 70 percent minimum, thermal burden trend monitoring, and outage event correlation with facility switching records. ---
