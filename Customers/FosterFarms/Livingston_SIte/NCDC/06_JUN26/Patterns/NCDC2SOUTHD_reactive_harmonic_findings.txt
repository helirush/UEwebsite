**NCDC2SOUTHD — Reactive and Harmonic Findings | June 2026**

The reactive character of South D is the defining feature of this field's optimization story. The TM view confirms a sustained, heavy reactive draw — the amperage trace runs persistently high, with a peak of 1,252.8 amps recorded in the header. During active production windows, the operating band frequently pushes above 900 amps. At 480 volts, that current level represents a substantial load on both the transformer windings and the distribution conductors feeding this field.

The reactive load tells its own story. The TBT header reports a dataset-wide kVAR maximum of 1,850.8 kVAR — this is the peak reactive event reached somewhere across the June operating window. The average reactive draw sits at 302 kVAR. The 372 kVAR maximum captured in the TM operational view represents the sustained upper band of reactive consumption during production cycles, distinct from the spike event captured in the TBT header. Both figures confirm that this field carries a heavy, continuous inductive burden with periodic reactive spikes that reach significantly higher.

The practical consequence of carrying 302 kVAR average reactive at a 92.6 percent average power factor is this: for every unit of useful real power delivered to production equipment, South D's transformer is supplying additional magnetizing current that does no productive work but generates heat, occupies conductor capacity, and inflates the apparent demand draw. That thermal and capacity penalty is quantified in the TBT header — 213,624 BTU per hour of thermal burden, and $6.17 per hour in cooling cost against a 31.3 kW cooling load.

The harmonic signature, as captured in the TM view, runs as a relatively contained low-amplitude band with an average iTHD of approximately 5.7 percent. This is characteristic of a facility with active VFDs and soft-start equipment — background harmonic injection that is present and measurable but not at distortion crisis levels in the current tuned state. The harmonic band does show micro-agitations coincident with the high-current spike events in the amperage trace, confirming that load transient events — likely large motor starts or compressor staging — generate correlated harmonic injection pulses at their peaks. This is worth tracking; if additional variable-speed drives are added to this field without corresponding harmonic mitigation, the iTHD figure can creep upward meaningfully.

The minimum power factor floor of 87.94 percent, as annotated in the TM view, confirms that South D never achieves high-quality reactive compensation even at its best operating moments in the current tuned state. The gap between 87.94 percent floor and 99.5 percent TS target represents the full reactive optimization corridor still available to this transformer.
