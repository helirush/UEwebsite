The TS header confirms 99.5 percent at both the MAX and MIN readings — meaning the optimization simulation holds power factor essentially at unity with negligible variation. That is the target state.

On the reactive side, the TS view projects the kVAR average dropping from the tuned baseline of 267.5 kVAR down to approximately 40.2 kVAR. That is a reduction of roughly 227 kVAR of average reactive burden — reactive current that currently flows through transformer windings, conductors, and switchgear generating heat and loss without performing any productive work. Eliminating that reactive floor means the transformer's apparent current burden drops substantially, conductor heating decreases, and the reactive tax on every operating hour is largely removed.

The thermal and cooling implications of full optimization are significant. The current tuned baseline carries 273,556 BTU per hour of thermal burden and 40.1 kW of cooling load. Moving to the TS optimization target would be expected to reduce that heat burden substantially — the reactive current that generates transformer and conductor heating is the dominant source, and a reduction from 267.5 kVAR average to 40.2 kVAR average represents an 85 percent reduction in average reactive loading. Cooling overhead — currently estimated at $69,081 annually — would decrease proportionally, reducing the facility's cooling infrastructure demand.

The total waste estimate in the tuned baseline state is approximately $207,086 per year. The TS optimization outcome represents the recoverable portion of that figure — the gap between where West11C operates today at 85.3 percent power factor and where it can operate at 99.5 percent. The MPTS investment already deployed has reduced waste from an uncompensated starting point. The simulation shows the remaining optimization gap is still material, and closing it delivers real, ongoing dollar and thermal savings to this facility every month.
