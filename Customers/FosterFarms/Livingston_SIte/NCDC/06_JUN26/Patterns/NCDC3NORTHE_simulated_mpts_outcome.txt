**Transformer: NCDC3NORTHE — Tuned-Field Simulation Outcome (TS) | June 2026**

The TS simulation view is where the optimization opportunity becomes concrete. With the field corrected to 99.5% power factor — the MPTS target — average reactive burden on NCDC3NORTHE drops from 308.2 kVAR to 42.7 kVAR. Peak reactive drops from 372.2 kVAR to 66.7 kVAR. That's a reduction of approximately 265 kVAR in average reactive circulation — current that was flowing through windings, conductors, and the utility metering point every hour, generating heat and cost, and delivering nothing productive.

The financial translation is direct. The TS header reports estimated total savings of approximately $241,019 per year. Energy recovery accounts for roughly $152,770 annually. The remainder flows from thermal burden relief and reduced cooling overhead — because when you remove 265 kVAR of reactive current from the circuit, you remove the heat that current was generating, and you reduce the cooling load required to manage that heat. At $9.61 per hour and 48.9 kW of cooling draw in the current state, the cooling burden reduction is not a secondary consideration — it's a meaningful operational cost lever.

Thermal burden context: the current state carries 333,852 BTU/hr of heat loading. The TS outcome reduces this substantially, consistent with the kVAR reduction magnitude. In a food-processing environment where ambient temperature management directly affects product safety and refrigeration efficiency, reducing the heat burden on the transformer and its immediate environment has compounding value beyond the direct cooling cost line.

From the TBT anchor to the TS outcome, the story is: existing investment has stabilized the field. The remaining optimization gap — quantified at roughly $241,000 per year in recoverable value — represents what continued tuning can capture. The path from 83.7% to 99.5% power factor on NCDC3NORTHE is not theoretical. The simulation shows it. The reactive numbers support it.
