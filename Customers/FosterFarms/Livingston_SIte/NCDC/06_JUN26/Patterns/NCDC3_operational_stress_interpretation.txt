**Transformer: NCDC3 | Stress Integration | June 2026**

Pulling the voltage, reactive, and harmonic findings together for NCDC3, the operational stress picture is one of a controlled but persistently loaded field. The transformer itself — rated at 2,500 kVA — is running at modest utilization relative to its nameplate capacity. The 655 kVA supply target and 664.1 kilowatt peak demand place NCDC3 well below transformer saturation. That is good engineering margin. The risk profile here is not transformer overload — it is the sustained, layered quality of the stressors acting on the downstream equipment fleet.

The thermal burden of 333,852 BTU per hour is the most direct indicator of sustained operational stress. That figure represents heat that the transformer and associated conductors are generating continuously — heat that must be managed through active cooling at a rated 48.9 kilowatts of cooling load at approximately $9.61 per hour. Every hour that reactive burden persists uncorrected, the thermal environment for motor windings, drive electronics, switchgear contacts, and cable insulation is elevated above what a fully corrected field would produce. Over a full operating year, this is not a minor variance — it is a compounding degradation driver.

The Power Factor floor at 80.75% — observed during peak stress intervals in the TM view — is the critical operational threshold. Sustained operation in the lower 80-percent range, even episodically, means the electrical system is drawing meaningfully more current than the productive kilowatt load requires. That excess current heats every component in the current path. Combined with the 11.4% harmonic maximum — which introduces additional I-squared-R heating at harmonic frequencies — the stress on motor insulation and transformer core is real and cumulative. The five to seven brief power interruption events observed in the TBT view, totaling an estimated 10 to 20 minutes of outage exposure across June, add momentary re-energization stress events that compound the existing harmonic and thermal picture. The operational stress profile on NCDC3 is manageable, but it is not minimal. It justifies sustained attention and incremental optimization action.
