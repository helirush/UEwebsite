Let's talk about South D — transformer NCDC2 here at Foster Farms Livingston — and where this field stands after the MPTS investment that's already been made. The good news is real. This is a tuned field, and the June data confirms it. Your voltage trace is holding horizontal and steady near 480 volts for the vast majority of the month. That horizontal discipline — on a transformer this size, running continuous food processing and cold storage loads — is not accidental. It reflects both good upstream supply from Merced Irrigation District and the stabilizing effect of active electromagnetic management. The infrastructure investment is doing its job. Now, here's where the optimization gap comes in. The average power factor for the month is 92.6 percent. That is better than an uncorrected field — meaningfully better. But it is not the 99.5 percent target that full MPTS correction reaches. That remaining gap carries a price tag: approximately $162,160 per year in estimated total waste — energy losses, thermal burden, and the 31.3 kilowatts of continuous cooling your systems are running just to manage transformer heat. That cooling load alone costs an estimated $54,041 per year. The reactive profile is the core driver of that gap. Your monthly average reactive load is 302 kVAR, and that's a steady, structural drain running every hour of every production day. The transient maximum — 1,850.8 kVAR — tells us there are significant reactive spike events, almost certainly large motor starts or compressor bank switching, that push apparent power well above the sustained average in short bursts. There was also a voltage sag event in early June — the trace touched 450.8 volts, which is about 6 percent below the 480-volt nominal. That depth of sag, even when episodic, puts real stress on VFDs and motor starters. It deserves a place in the maintenance log. The field is stabilized. The optimization work ahead is refinement — and it's worth doing. ---
