AGENTICTEA is an untuned field. In practical terms, that means reactive power compensation has not been assigned or calibrated for this transformer. Reactive power — measured in kVAR — is the portion of electrical current that flows back and forth between your power source and your inductive loads without doing any real work. Motors, compressors, refrigeration equipment, HVAC systems — all of these are inductive loads, and they all generate reactive demand. That reactive current still flows through your transformer windings. It still generates heat. It still contributes to the apparent power your utility measures and bills against. The consequence of carrying uncompensated reactive current is measurable in three places. First, your transformer runs hotter than it needs to. The thermal burden — that BTU per hour figure you'd see in a BT header — represents heat generated above what efficient power delivery would require. In a corrected field, that drops sharply from day one. Second, your cooling infrastructure works harder. Every additional BTU of heat in your electrical room or equipment bay has to go somewhere — and your ventilation or air conditioning systems are doing that work. That's a real dollar cost per hour. Third, your transformer ages faster. Elevated operating temperature is the primary accelerant of insulation degradation in transformer windings. A transformer running at the high end of its thermal rating has a meaningfully shorter service life than one running corrected and cool.
