**NCDC2SOUTHD — Tuned Simulation Outcome (TBT → TS) | June 2026**

The TS view for South D delivers the clearest statement of what full reactive optimization looks like for this transformer. The simulation brings power factor from the current TBT state of 92.6 percent to a corrected state of 99.5 percent — that's a 6.9 percentage point improvement representing a substantial reduction in the reactive burden being carried by the transformer and its connected distribution network.

What does that correction actually mean in operational terms? At 99.5 percent power factor, the apparent power demand drawn from the supply — the kVA figure — contracts toward the real kW component. The gap between kVA supplied and kW consumed, which is the reactive burden zone, narrows significantly. For a field currently averaging 302 kVAR of reactive draw, the corrected state recovers the majority of that reactive component at the point of use — the MPTS assets inject compensating reactive power locally, reducing the magnetizing current the transformer must supply from the utility source.

The thermal relief is directly proportional. The TBT header reports 213,624 BTU per hour of thermal burden in the current tuned state. Moving to the TS outcome reduces the reactive current component flowing through transformer windings and distribution conductors — which directly lowers resistive (I²R) heating in those conductors. Cooling costs currently running at $6.17 per hour against a 31.3 kW cooling load see corresponding reduction as the heat generation rate falls.

The energy waste pathway is also directly addressed. The TBT header reports approximately $100,481 per year in energy waste from the reactive and thermal overhead, with total annual waste estimated at approximately $162,160 when thermal burden and cooling overhead are included. The optimization gap between TBT at 92.6 percent and TS at 99.5 percent represents the recoverable fraction of that figure through continued field tuning and reactive compensation refinement. This is not a greenfield opportunity — existing MPTS assets have already captured a portion of the pre-MPTS baseline waste. What remains is the incremental gap still available through optimization progress.

From a field stability standpoint, closing the reactive gap also reduces voltage excursion depth during transient events. The 450.8-volt sag event and the episodic triangular drops observed in the TV view are worsened by reactive current draw during high-load transients — improved reactive compensation at the local level provides a degree of voltage support that reduces the severity and frequency of sag events. This is the connection between reactive optimization and the voltage stability findings — they are not independent; they reinforce each other.
