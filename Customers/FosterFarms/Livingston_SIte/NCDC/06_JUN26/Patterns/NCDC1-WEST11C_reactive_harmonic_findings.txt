**NCDC1-WEST11C — Reactive and Harmonic Profile | TM and TBT Views | June 2026**

The reactive portrait on West11C in June is one of a field that has been meaningfully corrected — but is still carrying a persistent reactive floor that costs real money every hour. The TM view shows the amperage trace running consistently in the 600 to 700 amp range across the month, with a peak of 701.6 amps. That is a substantial continuous current load, and it is not clean current — reactive content is visible throughout.

The KVAR trace in the TM view holds a persistent reactive floor in the 200 to 300 kVAR range, with the tuned-state reactive ceiling annotated at 301 kVAR in that view. The TBT header — which is the primary numeric anchor — reports the full reactive average at 267.5 kVAR and a reactive maximum of 875.9 kVAR. That 875.9 kVAR peak is the uncompensated stress ceiling this transformer encounters during peak load events. Even in a tuned field, those reactive spikes are reaching nearly 900 kVAR. The MPTS correction is absorbing a significant portion of reactive demand continuously, but the peaks still break through at high load moments.

Average power factor of 85.3 percent confirms the field is corrected from what would have been a much lower untuned baseline — but 85.3 percent means roughly 15 percent of the apparent power being drawn by this transformer is reactive waste. At a peak demand of 568.3 kW, that gap translates directly into transformer heating, elevated conductor losses, and the cooling overhead captured in the $69,081 annual cooling cost figure.

On the harmonic side, the picture is manageable. The iTHD average is 6.0 percent, with a recorded maximum of 11.0 percent. The harmonic trace in the TM view is low and flat — consistent with a blend of variable speed drives, refrigeration compressors, and distributed facility loads rather than a single dominant harmonic source. Harmonics here are a secondary contributor to thermal burden, not the primary driver. The reactive load is the story on West11C — it doesn't rest, and it compounds every hour of operation.
