**NCDC2 — South D Transformer | Tuned-Field Outcome and Optimization Gap | June 2026**

The TS view for South D projects the field outcome when MPTS correction reaches its full tuned-state potential — and the gap between where TBT sits today and where TS targets forward defines the remaining optimization opportunity on this transformer.

The TBT header anchors the current tuned state: 92.6% average power factor, 302.0 kVAR average reactive load, 213,624 BTU/hr thermal burden, $6.17/hr cooling cost, and an estimated $162,160 per year in total waste cost. These are real numbers representing real losses flowing through this transformer every operating hour of every production day.

The TS projection targets a corrected field approaching 99.5% power factor — the Unity MPTS design standard. Closing the gap from 92.6% to 99.5% on a transformer carrying 1,159.6 kW peak demand and averaging 302.0 kVAR of reactive load would deliver several compounding benefits simultaneously.

**Reactive load reduction:** Moving from 92.6% to 99.5% power factor on this load profile would substantially reduce the reactive component flowing through the transformer. The 302.0 kVAR average that currently rides as a persistent drag on the field would be largely neutralized at the point of correction — reducing apparent power demand, lowering current through the windings, and shrinking the thermal energy generated in the transformer core.

**Thermal burden relief:** The 213,624 BTU/hr waste heat figure is directly tied to the reactive and resistive losses in the winding system. As reactive current is compensated locally by the MPTS assets, the transformer copper losses — which scale with the square of current — decrease. A measurable reduction in BTU/hr output follows, reducing the work demand on the facility's cooling systems and freeing cooling capacity for process use rather than infrastructure management.

**Cooling cost recovery:** The 31.3 kW cooling load at $6.17/hr represents approximately $54,041 per year in overhead cooling expenditure. As MPTS tuning advances toward the TS target state, a meaningful portion of this cooling overhead becomes recoverable — not all at once, but progressively as the optimization gap closes through field refinement.

**kVAR MAX transient management:** The 1,850.8 kVAR transient maximum is the most challenging element of this field's reactive profile. MPTS correction reduces the average reactive baseline substantially, but large transient spikes during motor starts may still require evaluation for dynamic reactive compensation if the worst-case transient events persist after full tuning. This is a refinement-stage engineering question, not a fundamental barrier.

The sizing reference for this transformer's reactive demand: 1,850.8 kVAR MAX ÷ 400 = 4.63, rounding up to 5 H490 units at the full transient correction level — though the installed assets already address a significant portion of the average reactive burden. The remaining optimization gap is a refinement target, not a greenfield deployment requirement.
