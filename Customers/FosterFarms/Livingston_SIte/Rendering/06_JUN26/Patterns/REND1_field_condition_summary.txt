**REND1 — Untuned Field Condition Summary | Foster Farms Livingston HQ | June 2026**

The REND1 transformer is operating in a measurably stressed, untuned state. Every major indicator — power factor, reactive load, thermal burden, voltage stability, and cooling overhead — points to a field that is working significantly harder than it needs to, and delivering less usable power per ampere than it should.

The average power factor for the month sat at 79.8 percent. In practical terms, that means roughly one dollar in five that your utility delivers to this transformer is not doing productive work. It is circulating — heating windings, loading the transformer core, and expanding the current burden on every conductor in the circuit. The peak demand reached 1,295.2 kilowatts against a supply target of 1,380 kVA, which means this transformer is regularly approaching and breaching its effective operating ceiling during production hours.

The reactive load is the core problem here. The average reactive draw was 698.6 kVAR, and it spiked to a maximum of 1,132.9 kVAR — a reactive peak that is nearly as large as the entire real power demand. That is not a minor inefficiency. That is structural waste built into the daily operating rhythm of this facility.

The cost consequence is direct and significant. The BT view reports approximately $33,460 in wasted energy costs for this single dataset period, projecting to roughly $662,345 per year if this field runs uncorrected. The thermal burden associated with that reactive load is approximately 885,600 BTU per hour — a heat-generation rate that is feeding directly into the facility's cooling demand.

Without tuning assignment, this field will continue drifting. Reactive load tends to grow as motor insulation ages, as additional loads are added, and as the field's own heat burden accelerates degradation. The correction window is open now — the longer it remains untuned, the steeper the cumulative waste trajectory becomes.
