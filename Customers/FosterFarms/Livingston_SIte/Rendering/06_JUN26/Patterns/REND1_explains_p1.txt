What you're looking at with REND1 is a transformer that is working significantly harder than it has to — and paying a real cost for that every single month. The power factor for June averaged 79.8 percent. That means roughly one dollar in every five that your utility is delivering to this transformer is not doing useful work. It is circulating as reactive current — heating conductors, loading the transformer core, and expanding the apparent power demand without moving a single process forward. The monthly price tag on that inefficiency is approximately $33,460 in wasted energy costs for June alone. Annualized, that is roughly $662,000 per year — running out the door in an untuned field. The reactive load is the engine of that waste. The average reactive draw was 698.6 kVAR across the month. The peak hit 1,132.9 kVAR — nearly as large as the real power demand of 1,295 kilowatts. That is a structural imbalance in the field, not a temporary spike. The thermal consequence is 885,600 BTU per hour of waste heat. That heat burden is loading your cooling systems — about $25.60 per hour and 129.8 kilowatts of cooling overhead tied directly to electrical inefficiency on this one transformer. And the voltage picture adds urgency. The operating band runs consistently below the 480-volt nominal. A severe sag to 357.5 volts was recorded early in the month — more than 120 volts below nominal. On a food processing and cold storage operation, that kind of sag event stresses motor drives, risks VFD fault trips, and threatens process continuity. The correction path is clear: three H490 units, delivering 1,200 kVAR of reactive compensation. Projected power factor correction to 99.5 percent. Planning investment approximately $500,000 to $650,000 installed — with substantial recovery of that annual waste rate driving the payback. This field is drifting in the wrong direction every month it goes untuned. ---
