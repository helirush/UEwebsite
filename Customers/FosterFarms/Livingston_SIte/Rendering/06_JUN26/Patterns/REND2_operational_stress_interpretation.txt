**REND2 — Operational Stress | June 2026**

The operational picture on REND2 is one of a high-utilization industrial transformer running well beyond its efficient operating envelope. The 2,500 kVA nameplate is being challenged not just by active load, but by the reactive current burden layered on top. During production surge periods — particularly the June 7 through 9 and June 13 windows — the apparent power demand reaches approximately 2,250 to 2,300 kVA, which significantly exceeds the 1,669 kVA Max Supply target reference. The transformer is being pushed into territory where thermal stress accumulates rapidly.

The thermal burden of approximately 1,333,719 BTU per hour is the most direct expression of that stress. That is heat being generated inside the transformer core, in the windings, in the connecting conductors, and in the switchgear — heat that has to go somewhere. The cooling system is absorbing approximately 195.5 kW of electrical load just to manage that thermal output, at an estimated cost of approximately $38.41 per hour. That is cooling energy being spent not on production, but on keeping an inefficient electrical system from overheating.

Two power interruption events occurred during the June period — estimated at approximately 2 to 3 total hours of cumulative downtime. In a food processing and rendering facility, unplanned power interruptions carry costs that extend well beyond the direct electrical losses: product temperature excursions, production line restarts, potential food safety holds, and labor disruption. The frequency and apparent irregularity of these events — not tied to obvious scheduled maintenance windows — suggests operational vulnerability rather than planned downtime.

The production cycle pattern visible in the BT chart — five to six major production blocks with deep weekly valley periods — indicates a facility running hard during its active windows. The reactive waste layer, represented by the green kVAR band in the BT chart, is visible and persistent throughout every single production block. It never disappears. That means every hour of productive operation is accompanied by proportional reactive waste. Without tuning correction, this field will continue to drift: as the transformer ages under elevated thermal loading, reactive demand tends to increase, efficiency margins narrow, and the cost of doing nothing compounds month over month.
