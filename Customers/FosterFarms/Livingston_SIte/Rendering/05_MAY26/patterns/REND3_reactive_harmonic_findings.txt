**REND3 — Reactive and Harmonic Findings | May 2026**

The reactive field on REND3 is substantial and uncompensated. The M-view confirms what the BT header quantifies: an average reactive demand of 478.2 kVAR running continuously, with peaks reaching 885.4 kVAR MAX. To put that in physical terms — at peak, your reactive burden is nearly as large as your real demand. You are drawing 858.3 kilowatts of real power while simultaneously pulling 885.4 kVAR of reactive power. The transformer does not discriminate between the two; it must carry the full combined load.

The power factor orange trace in the M-view is visibly ragged — not the smooth, flat line you want to see in a well-compensated field. It drops repeatedly and sharply, with the minimum recorded at 67.41 percent. An average of 76.9 percent is already well below the 95-percent-plus range that defines efficient industrial operation. A floor of 67.41 percent means there are sustained intervals where nearly one-third of the current flowing through this transformer's windings and your facility's conductors is doing no productive work whatsoever. It is pure reactive circulation — magnetizing current churning through the system, generating heat, consuming capacity, and appearing on your utility bill.

In a rendering and food-processing environment, this reactive profile is typical of large induction motor loads — grinders, conveyors, refrigeration compressors, exhaust fans — all drawing reactive magnetizing current simultaneously. Without correction, every one of those loads is pulling reactive from the utility side rather than from a local capacitive source. That means the reactive current travels the full length of your conductors, loads your transformer windings, and contributes directly to the 631,000 BTU per hour thermal burden measured on this unit. MPTS correction addresses this at the source, and the S-view projection quantifies what recovery looks like.
