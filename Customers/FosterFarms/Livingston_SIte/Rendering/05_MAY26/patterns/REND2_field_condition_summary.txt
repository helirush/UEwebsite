**REND2 — Field Condition Summary | May 2026 | Untuned**

REND2 is a 2,500 kVA transformer serving what the load profile clearly identifies as a heavy industrial food processing and cold storage operation — almost certainly one of the highest-demand assets on this site. The field is untuned, meaning no reactive power compensation is in place, and the consequences of that gap are measurable and recurring every single day of this dataset.

The average power factor across all 44,640 minutes of May 2026 is 73.39%. That number deserves a moment of context. A well-tuned industrial field runs at 95% power factor or better. At 73.39%, this transformer is carrying approximately 827 kVAR of reactive current on average — energy that circulates through the windings, builds heat, and does zero productive work. At peak, that reactive burden reaches an estimated 1,324 kVAR. Every ampere of reactive current is a thermal and efficiency liability, and on a transformer of this size, the cumulative cost is substantial.

The 95th-percentile kVA demand — which serves as the daily operating target — sits at 1,665 kVA. The average kW drawn is 890.8 kilowatts, meaning the transformer is delivering about 890 kilowatts of real work while moving over 1,200 kVA of apparent power through its windings on average. That gap between real work and apparent load is the reactive waste gap, and at REND2 it is wide. The field is not failing — it is running production successfully — but it is doing so with significant and unnecessary overhead embedded in every operating hour.

Without a tuning assignment, this profile does not hold steady. Untuned fields under sustained reactive stress tend to drift: power factor slips incrementally, thermal burden accumulates in transformer windings and connected equipment, and the cost of doing nothing compounds quietly every month.
