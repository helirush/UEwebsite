We have 44,640 one-minute data points from the full month of May — that's the highest resolution capture you can get, and it leaves nowhere for inefficiency to hide. What the data shows is a 2,500 kVA transformer serving a continuous-duty industrial facility that is chronically under-compensated for reactive power demand. Start with the core efficiency metric. Power factor, confirmed by CSV, runs at an average of 76.94% across the month. That number means that for every unit of apparent power flowing through REND3 — every kilovolt-amp the transformer is working to deliver — only 76.94% of it becomes productive electrical work. The remaining 23-plus percent is reactive circulation: energy that flows back and forth on the conductors, heats the windings, and produces nothing useful. At an average apparent load of 750.6 kVA, you're carrying roughly 173 kVA of pure reactive overhead as a constant burden — and at peak moments, that figure climbs substantially higher.
