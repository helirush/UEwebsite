What the Unity framework tells us about untuned fields with industrial motor loads is this: reactive demand is almost always the dominant form of invisible waste. Real power — the kilowatts doing actual work — is what your process requires. Reactive power — measured in kVAR — is the magnetic field current that motors, transformers, and inductive loads demand simply to sustain their operating flux. The utility delivers both, but only real power shows up as productive output. Reactive current flows back and forth, occupying conductor and transformer capacity, generating heat, and driving demand charges — all without contributing a single useful joule to your process.

In a typical uncompensated industrial field, the ratio of reactive to apparent power (which is what defines power factor) will be pulled down by large motor loads, compressor banks, and any significant inductive draw on the system. A power factor in the 75 to 85 percent range is common in untuned facilities. At 80 percent power factor, for every 800 kilowatts of real demand, you are carrying approximately 600 kVAR of reactive burden — invisible to production output but very visible on your utility bill and on your transformer's thermal loading.
