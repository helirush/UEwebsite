**REND2 — Voltage Stability Findings**

The voltage field on REND2 is elevated but operationally coherent for most of the month — averaging 476.4 volts against a 480V nominal reference. That slight high-side bias is not unusual for a heavily loaded industrial bus, but it does indicate the upstream supply is compensating for load-driven sag by running slightly above nominal at the source. The recorded maximum is 489.9V, which is within acceptable range. The recorded minimum, however, is 9.5 volts — a value that close to zero is not a voltage sag. It is a full power-off event, and it is captured in the dataset as a real measurement. This corresponds to the gold vertical streak observed in the chart body — one confirmed outage event, estimated at approximately 60 to 120 minutes in duration, occurring near the end of the fourth production week.

Beyond that outage, the voltage health story for REND2 is defined by its recurring triangular dip events. The chart annotations identify fourteen distinct labeled dip points across the month, ranging from 460.6V down to a floor of 451.1V. Every single annotated dip falls below the 480V nominal threshold. The deepest event at 451.1V represents a depression of approximately 29 volts below nominal — close to a 6% sag. The MinVoltage reference line on the chart is confirmed at 451V, and multiple events cluster in the 453V to 457V range, meaning this field routinely experiences 4% to 6% sub-nominal excursions.

These dips are not random. They distribute in two-to-three-cluster patterns per production week, strongly suggesting they are tied to recurring high-inrush load events — compressor starts, large motor cycling, or rendering process sequencing. At an average amperage of 1,501 amps and a maximum of 2,596 amps, the electromagnetic stress at the moment of each voltage depression is substantial. Motor windings, variable-frequency drive front ends, and control system power supplies on this bus are absorbing that stress repeatedly — and that shortens equipment life. The iTHD average of 5.7%, spiking to 11.0% at maximum, amplifies the insulation and dielectric stress during each event. This is a voltage environment that warrants direct engineering attention alongside the reactive correction work.
