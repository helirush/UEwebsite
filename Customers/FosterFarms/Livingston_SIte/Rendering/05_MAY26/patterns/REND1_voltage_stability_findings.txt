**REND1 — Voltage Health | May 2026**

The voltage picture on REND1 is one of chronic elevation punctuated by recurring transient dips — a combination that creates meaningful cumulative stress on every motor, drive, and contactor this transformer feeds.

The CSV-derived average voltage of 273.36 V line-to-neutral translates to approximately 473.5 V line-to-line. The image annotation confirms an operating average closer to 476.7 V. Both readings place the average field voltage above the 480V nominal design centerline when you account for measurement point and distribution losses — and the image clearly shows the voltage trace riding above the nominal reference dashed line for the majority of the month. This is a sustained overvoltage operating condition.

Layered on top of that elevated baseline are at least eight distinct triangular dip events annotated directly on the voltage chart. The dip values extracted from image annotations are: 459.8 V, 460.8 V, 458.0 V, 457.7 V, 457.8 V, 460.7 V, 455.5 V, 454.9 V, 459.0 V, and 456.6 V. The most severe recorded dip is 454.9 V — roughly 25 volts below nominal and about 5.3% below the 480V centerline. That 5.3% excursion on a system that is otherwise running elevated is a voltage whipsaw signature.

What makes this pattern particularly damaging is the thermal cycling it creates. Motors running in an overvoltaged field draw elevated magnetizing current. Then when a large load starts — a rendering conveyor, a refrigeration compressor, a large fan bank — voltage snaps downward by 20 to 25 volts. That swing stresses motor windings from both ends: saturation on the high side, torque deficiency on the low side. Drive front-end capacitors, which are rated to handle narrow voltage windows, experience that full excursion.

The dip clustering I observed in the image — concentrated around mid-month and again in the latter half — suggests these are correlated with recurring shift-start load events rather than utility supply issues. That's actually a controllable pattern, but without field correction, it will persist and accumulate. The device health risk rating for REND1's voltage field is moderate-to-high, and it deserves explicit attention in any engineering scope.

Two gold-streak outage events were identified in the BT chart, estimated at 60 to 120 minutes each, for a total of approximately 2 to 4 hours of site power interruption during May. In a cold chain and food processing environment, each of those outage windows carries restart risk, food safety documentation requirements, and equipment thermal re-stabilization time that extends the impact well beyond the outage itself.
