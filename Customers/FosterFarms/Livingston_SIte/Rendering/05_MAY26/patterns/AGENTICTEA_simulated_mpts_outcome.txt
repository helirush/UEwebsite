What I can describe is the framework outcome you should expect once measurement is complete. When MPTS correction is applied to an untuned industrial field, the primary recovery is reactive power compensation. The H490 unit delivers 400 kVAR of correction capacity. By bringing reactive demand close to zero at the point of correction, the apparent power your transformer must carry drops sharply — even though real power output to your process remains unchanged. Your power factor moves toward 99.5 percent, transformer current drops, winding heat drops, and the demand charge component of your utility bill decreases.

The thermal burden recovery is typically substantial. Industrial fields corrected from 80 percent power factor to 99.5 percent commonly see transformer thermal loading reduce by 20 to 30 percent. Cooling overhead — the secondary cost loop described in the stress section — also drops in proportion, because less transformer heat means less cooling demand.
