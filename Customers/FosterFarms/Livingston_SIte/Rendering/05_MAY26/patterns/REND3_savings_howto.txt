How to read the M P T S savings view: first orient to the harmonized chart, next compare before-and-after field behavior, then focus on reliability and recovery signals before planning rollout. **REND3 — Reactive and MPTS Outcome Notes for Unity Faraday / Clerk Reference** When Unity Faraday speaks to the correction and savings section for REND3, here is the framing: The reactive burden on REND3 — 885.4 kVAR at peak — is the root cause of nearly all the waste on this transformer. Reactive current is not working current. It is magnetizing current that inductive loads require to function, but it does not do any useful mechanical work. Without local compensation, that reactive current travels all the way from the utility, through the supply conductors, through the transformer windings, and to the load — consuming capacity and generating heat along the entire path. MPTS correction installs local capacitive banks — in this case, three H490 units providing 1,200 kVAR of local reactive supply — so that reactive current is sourced right at the facility rather than pulled from the utility. The transformer sees the difference immediately: less current through the windings, lower winding temperature, lower thermal burden, and a power factor that rises from the current 76.9 percent average to a projected 99.5 percent. The voltage rail stabilizes because the reactive source is now local — voltage dips driven by reactive inrush become smaller and less frequent.
