**REND1 — Engineering Attention Areas | Priority Summary**

Several specific areas warrant engineering follow-up beyond the MPTS sizing recommendation.

First, the voltage overvoltage condition. The average field voltage running at approximately 473–476 V line-to-line against a 480V nominal in an untuned reactive environment suggests the utility supply tap may be set on the high side to compensate for anticipated drop. After MPTS correction reduces reactive current and improves voltage stability, a transformer tap adjustment assessment should be conducted to recenter the operating voltage closer to nominal. Running persistently above nominal accelerates core saturation effects and motor insulation aging.

Second, the eight-plus distinct voltage dip events. These should be correlated against the facility's production schedule and shift-start logs. If they align consistently with specific large motor starts — refrigeration compressor banks, rendering line initiation, large fan arrays — soft-start or variable frequency drive upgrades on those specific loads may be warranted as a complementary intervention. MPTS correction will reduce the voltage drop depth but may not eliminate the dip signature entirely if the inrush current events are very large.

Third, the two power interruption events identified during May deserve root cause investigation. Were these planned switching events, utility interruptions, or protective device operations? If either outage was caused by overcurrent protection responding to reactive-induced apparent current peaks, MPTS correction will directly reduce that risk. If they were utility supply events, a power quality monitoring escalation to the serving utility is appropriate.

Fourth, the transformer thermal margin. At 880,313 BTU/hr with the transformer running at a significant reactive burden, the available thermal headroom before protection thresholds are reached is narrower than it should be for a 2,500 kVA unit serving a growing food processing operation. An infrared thermal scan of the transformer and primary distribution panels during a peak production window is strongly recommended before the next capacity review.

Fifth, the cold chain continuity risk. In a food processing and rendering facility, power quality events are not just electrical problems — they are food safety events. Implementing a power quality monitoring and alerting protocol that flags voltage dips below 460 V and reactive demand spikes above 1,000 kVAR would provide early warning of field degradation between measurement periods.
