**REND1 — Projected MPTS Correction Outcome | Planning-Level Estimate**

The MPTS savings view for REND1 projects a field transformation that is both technically meaningful and financially significant. With the field corrected to approximately 99.5% power factor — the MPTS target state — the reactive burden drops from a 1,194.2 kVAR peak and 697.8 kVAR average to a level consistent with near-unity operation. That represents a correction of roughly 1,194 kVAR at peak demand.

Sizing the correction: applying the Unity sizing rule — kVAR MAX divided by 400, rounded up — gives us 1,194.2 ÷ 400 = 2.985, which rounds up to **3 H490 units**. At planning-level estimates, three H490 units represent an installed investment of approximately $600,000, subject to final engineering scope, site conditions, and any required switchgear or integration work. This is a planning-level range, not a binding quote.

Against an annual total waste cost of approximately $658,109 — reactive losses, thermal burden, and cooling overhead combined — a three-unit H490 correction targeting near-unity power factor projects a recovery of the majority of that annual waste. Using the Total Wasted figure of $658,109 as the pre-correction baseline, the projected payback window falls in the range of approximately 10 to 11 months at planning-level estimates, assuming full field correction is achieved and maintained.

Beyond the financial recovery, the operational benefits are concrete: transformer thermal burden drops significantly, reducing winding stress and extending equipment service life. Cooling load decreases, recovering an estimated 128.4 kW of cooling overhead. Amperage peaks are reduced, improving conductor life and reducing the probability of thermally-driven breaker trips. And voltage stability at the transformer secondary improves, directly benefiting the motor and drive loads that are currently cycling through the bidirectional voltage stress profile we observed in the V view.
