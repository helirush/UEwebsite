**REND2 — Reactive and Harmonic Findings**

The reactive signature on REND2 is one of the most prominent features of this field. The average reactive draw of 847.2 kVAR represents a massive standing reactive burden — this is not a light inductive load. The peak reactive demand reaches 1,608.2 kVAR, which is nearly double the average. That spread between average and maximum tells you something important: the reactive load on this transformer is highly dynamic. It swings hard during production peaks, particularly during motor-intensive phases of the rendering and cold-storage operation. The transformer is not just dealing with a steady reactive overhead — it is absorbing large reactive surges that push the apparent power envelope toward its 2,162.9 kVA recorded maximum.

At a power factor of 73.4%, approximately 26.6% of every kVA this transformer delivers is reactive current that performs no useful work. In raw terms, the transformer is carrying roughly 847 kVAR of dead weight alongside its real power delivery of 890.8 kW. That dead weight occupies conductor capacity, generates resistive heating in windings and cables, and forces the transformer to operate closer to its thermal and capacity limits than the actual production load alone would require.

Harmonic distortion adds another layer. The 5.7% average iTHD is moderate, but the 11.0% maximum is notable. At that peak level, harmonic currents are contributing measurably to winding losses and transformer core heating — losses that accumulate across every production shift. For a transformer already running at 88.7% of rated capacity, any additional thermal input from harmonics narrows the operational margin further. The combination of high reactive burden and moderate-to-elevated harmonics creates a compounding stress environment. Reactive correction through MPTS addresses the dominant component directly, while the resulting field cleanup also reduces harmonic amplification effects on the bus — a dual benefit that the raw numbers reflect clearly.
