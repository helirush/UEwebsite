**REND2 — Reactive and Harmonic Findings | May 2026 | Untuned**

The reactive profile on REND2 is one of the most openly stressed in this dataset. The CSV confirms an average reactive load of 827.4 kVAR across the full month, with a planning-level peak estimate of 1,324 kVAR. The M view callout records a Max REACTIVE of 1,142 kVAR visually — and per cross-check protocol, the CSV estimate of 1,324 kVAR takes precedence as the sizing anchor. Either figure represents a substantial reactive burden on a transformer of this scale.

The minimum power factor recorded during the month, as called out in the M view header, is 56.30%. That is a striking data point. At 56% power factor, the transformer was delivering more than half of its apparent current as reactive — wasted circulatory current that builds heat, stresses insulation, and contributes nothing to the facility's productive output. These are not momentary spikes. The power factor trace in the M view wanders persistently, confirming that the reactive field has no stabilizing correction acting on it at any point in the operating cycle.

The average kVAR of 827.4 against an average kW of 890.8 gives a reactive-to-real ratio of approximately 0.93. That means for every kilowatt of real work this transformer delivers, it is also carrying nearly a full kilowatt-equivalent of reactive burden. That ratio is significantly elevated relative to a corrected field, where the ratio would fall to roughly 0.10 or below.

On the harmonic side, the M view reports an average iTHD of 5.7% with episodic peaks reaching 11.0% iTHD MAX. The harmonic trace is persistent and present through both production and rest periods, which indicates the harmonic sources are distributed across the facility's drive and control infrastructure rather than isolated to a single load bank. At 11% peak iTHD, harmonic events are meaningful enough to cause additional transformer losses and can contribute to nuisance relay trips and communication interference in sensitive control equipment. These harmonic peaks are most likely concentrated at drive-heavy load transition moments — start-up sequences and production ramp events — which aligns with the voltage dip clustering observed in the V view.
