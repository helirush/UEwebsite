**REND2 — Operational Stress Interpretation**

REND2 is under sustained operational stress across multiple dimensions simultaneously, and those dimensions interact with each other in ways that compound the risk. Start with transformer capacity: at 88.7% of rated kVA capacity during normal operations, this transformer has approximately an 11% margin before it reaches its thermal and electromagnetic design ceiling. That margin sounds modest — and it is. Industrial transformers are designed for continuous operation at their rated capacity, but they are not designed for sustained operation near that ceiling with simultaneous high reactive burden and harmonic loading. The combination of high kVAR draw, elevated iTHD, and a 1,256,097 BTU per hour thermal burden means this transformer is running hot by design of its load composition, not by accident.

The 1,256,097 BTU per hour thermal burden is the direct thermal consequence of the reactive and resistive losses accumulating in this field. To manage that heat, the facility is spending approximately $36.00 per hour in cooling costs — drawing 183.2 kW just to run the mechanical cooling load that keeps this transformer and its associated infrastructure from overheating. That is $315,354 per year in cooling expenditure alone, paid entirely because the field is not corrected.

The production rhythm makes this worse. REND2 follows a clear five-day-on, two-day-off weekly cycle. The transformer gets two days of relative rest on weekends, then ramps back into full production load Monday morning — cycling through large inrush events, voltage dips, and reactive surges throughout the week. That weekly thermal cycling, combined with repeated voltage sag events in the 451V to 460V range, is a mechanical and electromagnetic fatigue driver for every device on this bus. Motors, contactors, insulation systems, and control components all experience shortened service life under these conditions. The total identified waste of $938,187 per year is the financial expression of a field that is working against itself — and the operational stress interpretation is that this situation is not self-correcting. It requires active intervention.
