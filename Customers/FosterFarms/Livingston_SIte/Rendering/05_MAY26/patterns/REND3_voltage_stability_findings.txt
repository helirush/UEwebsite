**REND3 — Voltage Stability Findings | May 2026**

The voltage picture on REND3 is one of the more concerning elements of this analysis. The recorded voltage range for May spans from a minimum of 0.0 volts to a maximum of 500.7 volts, against a nominal reading of approximately 478.5 volts. That zero-volt floor is not a data artifact to be dismissed — it is a confirmed voltage collapse event. At least once during this month, the voltage rail on this transformer dropped to near zero. That is a hard fault or a complete momentary outage, and every motor, drive, and controller on this bus experienced it.

Beyond that single extreme event, the voltage trace itself is not stable. The blue voltage line holds in the 460-to-480-volt band for most of the month, but it is constantly oscillating — not holding a clean setpoint. The field is reacting continuously to load cycling rather than maintaining steady-state voltage. Triangular drop patterns are present and recurring throughout May, clustering noticeably in the mid-to-late month window. These sharp, pointed downward excursions are the electromagnetic fingerprint of sudden inrush loading — heavy motors starting, compressors cycling on, refrigeration systems pulling hard against an already-burdened bus.

The lower dashed reference line sits near 461 volts, and the trace crosses below it on multiple occasions. For reference, the nominal industrial standard for this bus is 480 volts line-to-line. Dips below that threshold mean motors are running on reduced voltage — increased slip, higher current draw, elevated winding temperature, and accelerated insulation degradation. When these dips are frequent and recurring across an entire month, as they are here, the cumulative stress on rotating equipment is significant.

The amperage trace confirms the picture — spikes up to 1,511.5 amperes MAX accompany several of the voltage dip events, indicating reactive load surges compounding the instability. The device-health risk on REND3's voltage rail must be rated HIGH.
