**REND2 — Recommended Engineering Attention Areas**

First priority: reactive compensation sizing and deployment. REND2 requires 4 H490 units at planning scale to fully address the 1,397.7 kVAR maximum reactive demand. This is a large correction requirement and the engineering scoping process should verify transformer secondary bus configuration, available switchgear space, and conduit routing before finalizing unit placement. Do not undersize — partial correction at this power factor level will still leave the field in a distorted, stressed state.

Second priority: harmonic assessment and mitigation. The 16.0 percent iTHD reading is above acceptable industrial thresholds and indicates active harmonic sources — likely variable frequency drives, soft starters, or non-linear load clusters — feeding back into the distribution system. A harmonic survey of the REND2 distribution panel should identify the primary sources. MPTS harmonic filtering capability should be confirmed against the specific harmonic spectrum present on this bus before equipment selection is finalized.

Third priority: voltage sag event correlation. The repeated triangular dips to the 453–454V floor during load transitions should be correlated against specific production equipment ramp cycles. Identifying which pieces of equipment are driving the sharpest sag events enables targeted mitigation — whether through soft-start upgrades, capacitor pre-charge adjustments, or MPTS deployment sequencing.

Fourth priority: thermal inspection of transformer and distribution conductors. With 1,348,346 BTU per hour of thermal burden being generated by reactive losses, the transformer winding insulation, bus connections, and high-current distribution conductors should be inspected via infrared thermography before the next production cycle. Elevated resistance connections under sustained high-current loading are a documented failure mode in reactive-heavy industrial fields.

Fifth priority: outage event logging. The two brief outage events observed in April should be cross-referenced against the facility's SCADA or protection relay event log to confirm cause — switching, grid interruption, or internal fault initiation. Pattern-tracking these events month over month provides early warning of protection system degradation.
