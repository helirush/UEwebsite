**REND1 — Foster Farms, Livingston HQ | April 2026 | Untuned Field**

The REND1 transformer is carrying a heavy, untuned electromagnetic field at a large food processing and cold storage facility in Livingston, California. The field is untuned — no MPTS correction is currently assigned — and the data from this full thirty-day April window tells a consistent story of reactive burden accumulation, thermal loading, and slow field degradation.

The power factor is averaging 83.4 percent across the month, with a minimum measured dip to 70.0 percent during peak inductive load events. For a transformer serving a facility of this scale — with a peak demand of 1,348.7 kilowatts and a supply target of 1,390 kVA — that power factor gap is not a minor inefficiency. It represents a meaningful portion of every kilowatt-hour being delivered to this bus as unproductive reactive current rather than real work.

The peak reactive demand reaches 1,113.9 kVAR. The average reactive load is 641.9 kVAR. Those numbers tell you that at virtually every point during active production, this transformer is hauling a large reactive tail — current that stresses windings, heats conductors, and contributes nothing to running compressors, motors, or process equipment.

The resulting energy waste is estimated at approximately $27,729 per dataset period and roughly $337,375 per year in direct reactive losses alone. When thermal burden penalties and cooling overhead are added in, the total estimated annual waste climbs to approximately $548,238. That is not a projection built on hypotheticals — it is derived from thirty days of measured field behavior on this transformer. The field is stable enough in its production cycle to make those estimates reliable. The problem is that without correction, this cost accumulates every month, and the reactive burden tends to drift upward as equipment ages and additional inductive loads are added to the bus.
