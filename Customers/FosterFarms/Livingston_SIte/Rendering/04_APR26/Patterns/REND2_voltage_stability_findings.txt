**REND2 — Voltage Stability | April 2026 | Untuned Field**

The voltage health picture on Rendering-2 is a study in compounding stress. On the surface, the average voltage of 476.2 volts with a maximum of 489.5 volts might suggest the field is running close to nominal. Look deeper and the picture changes significantly. That sustained elevated voltage — riding 6 to 9 volts above nominal 480V for extended production windows — is characteristic of an uncompensated reactive field. When reactive current is not being corrected locally, the supply voltage cannot be drawn down cleanly. The field floats high, and that elevated baseline makes the voltage drop events even more damaging when they occur.

And they occur frequently. Triangular voltage-dip markers are densely clustered throughout every production cycle in April. The annotated dip values are unambiguous: 453.2V, 453.8V, 457.6V, 460.7V, 458.3V, 453.7V, 454.8V, 453.1V, 455.6V, and multiple additional events in the same range. The chart floor reference confirms a minimum of 453 volts. That is approximately 27 volts below nominal 480V — a sag of roughly 5.6 percent. For heavy motor loads and refrigeration compressors, a 5.6 percent sag is not a minor event. It creates measurable torque reduction, increases motor slip, and elevates winding current draw at the moment the motor needs stable voltage most.

The dip pattern is not random. Events cluster at load-onset and load-shedding transitions — the field is being mechanically shocked at every ramp-up and every ramp-down across each production block. Multiple dip events per production cycle are visible, with some blocks showing four to six distinct triangular dip markers. This frequency, combined with the 16.0 percent iTHD harmonic distortion reading visible in the dashboard header, creates a compounding insulation and mechanical stress environment. Device-health risk classification for this field: **HIGH**.
