**REND1 — Operational Stress | April 2026**

The REND1 transformer is operating under sustained, compounding stress. The facility is running a clear weekly production cycle — electrically active for approximately 70 to 75 percent of the month, with scheduled shutdown valleys appearing roughly every five to six days. During those active production periods, the transformer is loaded at or above the 1,390 kVA supply target for the majority of operational hours.

That is a high sustained loading condition. When you layer in a power factor averaging 83.4 percent, you are asking the transformer to carry significantly more apparent power than the real work it is doing justifies. The gap between 1,348.7 kilowatts of real demand and 1,390 kVA of apparent supply represents the reactive overhead that is riding along with every production shift, heating conductors, stressing insulation, and occupying transformer capacity that could otherwise serve additional productive load.

The 1,701.1 amp peak current reading is the sharpest single indicator of stress on this bus. Conductor and transformer winding heating scales with the square of current — I-squared-R heating — which means that at those peak moments, the thermal stress on the REND1 transformer windings and the associated distribution conductors is substantially elevated above what the real-load requirement alone would impose.

For a food processing and cold storage facility like Foster Farms Livingston, the compressor and refrigeration loads are continuous and non-negotiable. They cannot be curtailed when thermal stress builds. That means the thermal burden accumulates without relief during production windows, and the cooling infrastructure — currently running 106.7 kilowatts of cooling load and burning an estimated $184,879 per year — is working to counteract a problem that originates in the electromagnetic field quality, not in the ambient environment.

The untuned field condition means this stress profile will not self-correct. Without reactive compensation assigned to REND1, the power factor will continue averaging in the low-to-mid eighties, the reactive peak will continue reaching above 1,100 kVAR during high-load events, and the cumulative device stress from repeated sub-nominal voltage dips combined with elevated current will continue aging equipment on an accelerated curve. In an industrial food processing environment, that trajectory leads to unplanned downtime — and unplanned downtime in cold storage and rendering is not an abstract operational inconvenience.
