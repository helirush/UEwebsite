**REND3 — Voltage Stability Findings | April 2026**

The voltage field on REND3 presents a two-sided problem, and both sides carry real risk for the equipment connected to this transformer.

On the high side: the blue voltage trace holds persistently in a band roughly between 484 and 511 volts across most of the month. The nominal reference for this class of system is 480 volts line-to-line. Running continuously 4 to 31 volts above nominal is a sustained overvoltage condition. Insulation systems are rated for their nominal voltage — sustained overvoltage accelerates dielectric aging in motor windings, drives elevated hysteresis losses in iron cores, and shortens equipment service life on a schedule that is not visible until a failure event occurs. The annotated maximum of 511 volts represents a 6.5 percent overvoltage excursion. In isolation, a brief 511-volt spike would be a nuisance. As a persistent upper operating band, it is chronic insulation stress.

On the low side: the annotated minimum of 444.1 volts — visible near the April 14 zone — represents a drop of approximately 36 volts below nominal, or roughly 7.5 percent undervoltage. That is a meaningful sag event. Motors on this transformer would have responded to that sag by drawing elevated current to maintain torque, which in turn generates additional heat in motor windings already stressed by the facility's high reactive burden. The voltage minimum annotation of 0.0 volts in the header is also significant — it indicates at least one interval where voltage collapsed entirely, consistent with an outage or field dropout event.

For a food processing operation, unplanned production interruptions of this scale carry their own cost implications beyond energy waste.
