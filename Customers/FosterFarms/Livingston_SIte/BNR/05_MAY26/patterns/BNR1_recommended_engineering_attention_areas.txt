**BNR-1 Engineering Priorities | May 2026**

Based on the full May 2026 analysis of BNR-1 at Foster Farms Livingston HQ, the following engineering attention areas are recommended in priority order.

**First — Reactive compensation deployment.** Three H490 units sized to the 1,017 kVAR CSV-confirmed peak. This is the highest-leverage single action available for BNR-1. Every month without correction is another month of avoidable thermal burden, reactive energy waste, and accelerating insulation aging on a transformer already running at high utilization. The planning-level investment of approximately $600,000 installed should be evaluated against the estimated $425,000-plus annual waste cost confirmed by the BT analysis.

**Second — Voltage baseline investigation.** The chronic 1.2 percent average undervoltage and the recurring 4.8 percent dip events every six to eight days warrant a dedicated voltage survey. Engineering should confirm whether the BNR-1 supply tap position is optimized, whether transformer on-load tap changer settings (if equipped) can be adjusted, and whether reactive compensation deployment itself will partially correct the voltage suppression by reducing the inductive voltage drop in the supply circuit.

**Third — Outage root-cause review.** Two power interruption events totaling approximately 2 to 3 hours of estimated downtime were identified in May. At a food processing facility, outage events of that duration during production hours carry food safety, product loss, and regulatory implications. Engineering should confirm whether these were planned maintenance windows, protective relay operations, or unplanned supply disturbances, and whether any upstream protective coordination improvements are warranted.

**Fourth — Harmonic survey and mitigation assessment.** The 10.8 percent iTHD peak, occurring at nearly double the 6.2 percent average, indicates load-switching harmonic events that are contributing to transformer core heating and conductor losses. A dedicated harmonic survey during peak production transitions is recommended to identify the primary source loads — likely VFD-driven equipment — and evaluate whether passive or active harmonic filtering should be incorporated into the correction scope alongside reactive compensation.

**Fifth — Tuning assignment and monthly trend monitoring.** As an untuned field, BNR-1's efficiency will continue to drift downward and heat burden will tend to rise month over month without a correction assignment. Establishing a baseline Mercury trend cadence and assigning a tuning target are essential for tracking whether field conditions are stable, improving, or deteriorating between the current analysis period and correction deployment.
