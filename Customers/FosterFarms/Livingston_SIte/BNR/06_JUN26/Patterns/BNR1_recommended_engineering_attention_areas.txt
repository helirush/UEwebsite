**BNR-1 — Engineering Attention Areas | June 2026**

Several areas warrant engineering attention on BNR-1 before and alongside MPTS deployment.

First, the voltage collapse events documented in the V view — five to six distinct momentary interruptions across June, totaling an estimated 10 to 18 minutes of accumulated outage time — should be investigated for root cause. In a cold-chain food processing environment, these events carry food safety implications beyond their electromagnetic significance. Identifying whether the driver is large motor starting inrush, compressor cycling, or utility-side fluctuations will determine whether soft-start upgrades, inrush limiting, or utility coordination are warranted in parallel with reactive correction.

Second, the iTHD environment at 6.3 percent average and 11.4 percent peak exceeds IEEE 519 limits for this facility class. Engineering should assess whether passive harmonic filtering or active front-end treatment on the largest VFD loads would bring the harmonic environment within limits and reduce the compounding insulation stress currently accumulating on transformer windings and cable infrastructure.

Third, the kVAR MAX figure discrepancy observed in the BT header — where a secondary value of 152.1 kVAR was labeled alongside the primary 692.6 kVAR figure — should be clarified in the detailed engineering data pull. If 152.1 kVAR represents a sub-circuit or a specific meter point rather than the transformer-level reactive maximum, the MPTS sizing recommendation may need adjustment once the full reactive profile hierarchy is mapped.

Fourth, given the untuned field status and the documented thermal burden of 602,911 BTU per hour, transformer insulation condition assessment — including winding temperature trending and insulation resistance testing — is advisable before summer peak loading arrives at this California facility. June's numbers establish the baseline; July and August ambient conditions will stress this transformer further without correction in place.
