**BNR Transformer | June 2026 — Tuned-Field MPTS Outcome**

The TS (tuned simulation) view for BNR provides a clear, quantified picture of what the MPTS correction system delivers when operating at full optimization across this transformer's load profile. The contrast between the TBT baseline and the TS outcome is the optimization gap this facility is actively working to close.

**Power factor:** From 89.7% at baseline to 99.5% fully corrected — and notably, the TS header shows both the Max and Min power factor holding at 99.5%, meaning the correction is flat and consistent across all load conditions, not just average performance. That stability is the signature of a well-sized and properly operating MPTS field.

**Reactive burden:** Average kVAR compresses from 692.6 down to 133.1 — a reduction of approximately 559.5 kVAR on average. At the peak, kVAR drops from 730.8 to 152.1 — a 578.7 kVAR reduction at maximum reactive demand. This is the reactive burden that the facility's utility infrastructure was carrying at cost, and the MPTS correction eliminates the dominant portion of it.

**Transformer headroom:** Apparent power drops from an average of 1,571.8 kVA to 1,332.9 kVA — freeing approximately 238.9 kVA of transformer capacity. That recovered headroom is available for production expansion, equipment additions, or simply as reliability margin against overload events — without requiring transformer upgrade capital.

**Thermal and cooling burden relief:** With 578.7 kVAR of peak reactive load removed, the thermal generation inside the transformer core and windings decreases proportionally. The 602,911 BTU per hour baseline thermal burden — and the 88.4 kilowatts of cooling draw associated with it — both compress meaningfully as the reactive field tightens. The precise post-optimization thermal figure from the TS header carries a planning-level estimate character, but the directional relief is substantial and confirmed by the reactive reduction magnitude.

**Harmonic improvement:** iTHD average improves from 6.3% to 5.0% — a meaningful reduction toward the IEEE 519 compliance boundary, reducing transformer core heating and improving control system voltage quality.

**Waste cost context:** The TBT header documents approximately $274,706 per year in reactive waste, with a total wasted figure of approximately $449,367 per year including thermal and cooling burden. The TS outcome positions the facility to recover the dominant share of those costs — the exact captured fraction depends on current asset deployment state and operating conditions, but the optimization gap is real and the recovery opportunity is fully quantified in these views.
