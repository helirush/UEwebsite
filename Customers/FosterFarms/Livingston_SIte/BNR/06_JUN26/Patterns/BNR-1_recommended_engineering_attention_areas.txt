**BNR-1 — Recommended Engineering Attention Areas | June 2026**

The following attention areas are framed as incremental refinement and diagnostic actions consistent with a tuned field already carrying MPTS correction assets, not as greenfield recommendations.

**Priority 1 — Voltage Collapse Event Root-Cause Diagnostics.** The confirmed 0.0-volt minimum recorded during the June monitoring window requires investigation. The specific timestamp of the collapse event should be pulled from the eSTREAM dataset to correlate with any operational log, utility event, or protective relay record. Understanding whether this was a utility-side interruption, a site-side fault, or a protective trip-and-reclose sequence determines whether the response is operational, protective relaying, or utility coordination. For a cold-chain food processing facility, establishing the root cause and implementing targeted mitigation is a food safety and business continuity priority.

**Priority 2 — MPTS Tuning Refinement Toward Target Reactive Envelope.** The gap between the active TM reactive ceiling of 683 kVAR and the TBT optimization target of 152.1 kVAR maximum is approximately 530 kVAR of remaining reactive suppression opportunity. Engineering should review the current MPTS operating parameters against the designed correction capacity to identify whether tuning adjustments, capacitor bank setpoint refinement, or switching logic updates can close this gap without additional capital deployment.

**Priority 3 — Harmonic Content Trend Monitoring.** The iTHD maximum of 11.4 percent should be trended across successive monthly datasets to establish whether harmonic content is stable, drifting upward, or correlated with specific operational events. If iTHD continues climbing toward the 15 percent range, active harmonic filtering supplementation to the existing correction assets should be evaluated. Current levels are manageable, but the trend direction is the critical variable.

**Priority 4 — Inrush Voltage Drop Pattern Analysis.** The recurring triangular voltage drop pattern, particularly the clustering observed around apparent shift-start intervals, warrants closer examination. Staggered motor starting sequences or soft-start coordination adjustments for the largest connected loads could reduce the inrush magnitude and improve the voltage floor during those intervals, complementing the reactive correction that the MPTS assets are already providing.

**Priority 5 — Thermal Burden Monitoring and Cooling System Review.** The 602,911 BTU per hour thermal burden should be trended against seasonal ambient temperature data — June conditions in California's Central Valley represent a significant ambient heat load, and the interaction between transformer waste heat and facility cooling capacity deserves a seasonal load analysis. Confirming that cooling infrastructure remains adequate as summer peaks approach is a proactive reliability action.
