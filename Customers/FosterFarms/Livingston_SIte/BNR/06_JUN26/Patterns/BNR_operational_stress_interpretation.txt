**BNR Transformer | June 2026 — Operational Stress Interpretation**

Taken together, the voltage, reactive, and harmonic profiles documented for BNR in June paint a picture of a facility operating under layered and compounding electromagnetic stress — stress that the existing MPTS investment is actively working to reduce, but has not yet fully resolved.

The thermal burden of 602,911 BTU per hour is the most immediate operational consequence. Transformer cores and windings operating at elevated thermal levels experience accelerated insulation aging on a well-documented exponential curve — every 10°C of sustained excess temperature roughly halves insulation service life. The 88.4 kilowatts of cooling load at $17.55 per hour is not just a utility line item; it is the facility's mechanical response to heat that the reactive field is generating inside the electrical infrastructure.

The voltage profile compounds this stress. An operating average of 476.5 volts — below the 480-volt nominal — combined with recurring triangular voltage drops during load cycling means that motors, drives, and contactors at this facility are routinely exposed to sub-nominal conditions. Motor torque output drops roughly as the square of voltage reduction. At 476.5 volts average, that translates to motors working harder to deliver rated torque — drawing more current, generating more heat, and wearing faster than their design life assumes.

The 11.4% peak iTHD harmonic content adds a third stress layer. Harmonic currents cause additional transformer core losses, increase winding temperatures beyond what fundamental-frequency load alone would produce, and create voltage distortion that ripples into sensitive control systems. In a food processing environment where process continuity is a product safety requirement — not just an efficiency preference — harmonic-driven instability in control systems represents real operational risk.

The 4 to 5 outage events observed across June, totaling an estimated 5 to 10 minutes, may appear minor in aggregate duration, but in cold-chain operations each interruption triggers restart protocols, product temperature monitoring reviews, and potential hold events on product batches. The operational cost of these interruptions exceeds the electrical cost by a significant multiple.

The MPTS system already deployed is demonstrably cutting into this stress profile — the TS outcome shows what full optimization looks like — but the gap between the TBT baseline condition and the TS target outcome represents real, ongoing operational exposure that rewards continued attention.
