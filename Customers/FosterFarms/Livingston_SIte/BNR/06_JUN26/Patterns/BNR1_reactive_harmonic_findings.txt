**BNR-1 — Reactive and Harmonic Stress | June 2026**

The M view for BNR-1 is a textbook portrait of a transformer under sustained electromagnetic stress from two compounding sources: reactive power burden and harmonic distortion. Both are persistent, both are structural in character, and both are measurable in their consequences.

On the reactive side, the amperage trace averaged 1,918.5 amps across the month, with a documented maximum of 2,059.5 amps. That's an impressive and concerning current burden for this transformer. The power factor orange trace holds in the 89 to 90 percent band for most operating hours — consistent with the BT header's 89.7 percent average — but the 70.0 percent minimum confirms that during high-reactive events, the current-to-work ratio deteriorates sharply. The green kVAR reactive trace runs flat and persistent in the lower portion of the M chart at approximately 692.6 kVAR average, with a referenced MAX figure of 683 kVAR visible in the chart annotation. This flat, non-episodic reactive profile is characteristic of a large induction motor population operating continuously — think refrigeration compressors, cold storage fans, and processing drive motors cycling around the clock.

On the harmonic side, the purple iTHD trace at the base of the M chart shows dense, continuous harmonic activity throughout the full 30-day period. The average iTHD measured at 6.3 percent, with a documented peak of 11.4 percent iTHD — both values exceed the IEEE 519 recommended threshold of 5 percent for this class of industrial facility. Critically, the harmonic signature is non-episodic and distributed, meaning this isn't a single VFD running a particularly aggressive load profile. It's a facility-wide harmonic environment generated by multiple variable frequency drives, switching power supplies, and motor control systems operating simultaneously. At 11.4 percent iTHD peak, transformer derating is a real concern, neutral conductor heating is elevated, and insulation stress compounds on top of the thermal burden already driven by reactive losses. These two stressors — reactive current and harmonic distortion — do not add linearly; they interact, and their combined effect on transformer and cable insulation aging is accelerated beyond what either would cause alone.
