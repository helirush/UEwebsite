**Transformer: PL1MS — Voltage Stability | May 2026**

The voltage field on PL1-MShop is running warmer than it should across the full month of May. The CSV-confirmed average voltage of 282.81 volts line-to-neutral translates to approximately 489.7 volts line-to-line — sitting above the nominal 480-volt reference. That elevated baseline is not catastrophic, but it represents a sustained swell condition that is quietly stressing motor insulation, capacitor banks, and sensitive control electronics across every operating hour of the month.

That is a 54-volt spread — more than 11 percent of nominal — riding across a facility running food processing loads including compressors, refrigeration systems, and motor-driven conveyors. Those loads are sensitive to both the chronic high-side swell and the episodic low-side dips.

One discrete voltage dip event is clearly identified in the V view at 454.1 volts, occurring in the 260520 time region. That dip represents a 5.4 percent sag below nominal 480V — meaningful for motor windings under load. This event correlates directly with the amperage spike visible in the M panel near the same date, confirming that a demand surge event briefly pulled the voltage field below nominal as the reactive burden spiked. In a tuned field, reactive compensation would absorb that reactive surge and stabilize the voltage through the event. In this untuned condition, the full reactive shock passes directly through the transformer and into the voltage field.

No clustered triangular dip patterns were observed — the field is not exhibiting rapid-cycling voltage instability. However, the combination of a chronic high-side swell above 480V throughout the month, one confirmed below-nominal sag event, and the untuned reactive burden creates a compound device-health risk. Motor windings accumulating thermal stress from the chronic swell are less tolerant of the occasional sag event. The two conditions together accelerate insulation aging in ways that neither condition alone would produce at the same rate.

No outage events — defined as gold top-to-bottom vertical streaks in the chart body — were detected in any view panel for PL1MS during May 2026.
