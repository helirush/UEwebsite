**PL1P2 — Recommended Engineering Attention Areas | May 2026**

The following areas warrant prioritized engineering attention based on the May 2026 field capture for transformer PL1P2:

**1. Reactive Compensation Assignment — Immediate Priority**
PL1P2 is carrying an average of 284.2 kVAR of reactive load continuously through every production hour, with estimated peaks at 455 kVAR. Two H490 units represent the planning-level MPTS solution for this transformer. Until a tuning assignment is made, every additional month of operation in this untuned state accumulates avoidable thermal burden, accelerates insulation aging, and sustains the energy waste trajectory estimated at six figures annually. This should be the first engineering action queued for this transformer.

**2. Voltage Depression Investigation — Load-Event Correlation**
The recurring triangular voltage drops into the 308 to 315-volt range on a 480-volt system need to be correlated with specific load events. The two-to-four-day recurrence pattern suggests a specific operational trigger — likely a large motor start sequence, a compressor bank restart, or a refrigeration pull-down cycle. Identifying and sequencing these events through soft-start or staged-start controls can reduce the depth and frequency of voltage sags independent of reactive compensation.

**3. Thermal Management Review**

Confirm that cooling airflow paths are unobstructed, that cooling system capacity is matched to actual thermal load rather than nameplate assumptions, and that winding temperature monitoring is in place. Thermal imaging of the transformer enclosure and associated switchgear is recommended given the sustained high-load operating profile.

The image-extracted kVAR MAX of 948 kVAR is more than double the CSV-estimated 455 kVAR peak. While the CSV figure is used as the engineering basis per cross-check protocol, the image spike reading warrants investigation. If instantaneous reactive spikes are genuinely reaching 948 kVAR — even momentarily — they represent transient events that could be tripping protection systems or causing the brief load interruption events observed near 260514 and 260523. A dedicated power quality meter with sub-cycle capture capability should be installed to characterize these transient events precisely before finalizing MPTS sizing.

**5. Operational Interruption Events**
Two brief near-zero load collapse events were observed in the BT view — estimated at one to three minutes each. These should be reviewed against the facility's SCADA event log to determine whether they are planned shutdowns, protection trips, or grid events. If protection-related, the trigger conditions — likely overcurrent or undervoltage — should be evaluated in the context of the reactive stress profile described here.
