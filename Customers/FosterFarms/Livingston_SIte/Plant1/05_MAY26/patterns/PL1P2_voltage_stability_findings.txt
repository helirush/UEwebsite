**PL1P2 — Voltage Stability Findings | May 2026**

The drop pattern is not random. The recurrence interval — roughly every two to four days — is consistent with cyclical peak-load stress events driven by compressor restarts, large motor starts, or refrigeration system cycling. This is not grid instability. This is reactive-load-induced voltage collapse driven by the untuned state of this field.

For a food processing facility, the device-health implication is serious. Motors operating during these voltage depressions are drawing higher current to maintain torque — which generates more heat, accelerates insulation degradation, and shortens winding life. Refrigeration compressors under sustained low-voltage conditions develop slip, lose efficiency, and wear bearings faster. Control systems and drives may trip on undervoltage faults, adding unplanned downtime events to the operational risk profile.

Device stress risk on PL1P2 is rated **high**. The minimum voltage floor touches approximately 307 volts as annotated in the BT view tooltip — representing a worst-case depression of 173 volts below a 480-volt nominal. That level of sag, recurring weekly across an untuned field, is the voltage signature of a transformer that is being asked to do more than its uncompensated reactive current budget allows.
