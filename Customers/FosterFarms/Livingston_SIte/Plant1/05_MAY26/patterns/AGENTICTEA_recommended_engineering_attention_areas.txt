**Transformer: AGENTICTEA — Recommended Engineering Attention Areas**

The primary engineering attention item for transformer AGENTICTEA is completing a full measurement capture session. Without Mercury CSV data and without rendered image views — voltage, baseline/target, savings, and multi-view — the field profile for this transformer cannot be assessed, quantified, or acted upon. Every other engineering recommendation flows downstream from that data foundation.

Once data capture is complete, the recommended engineering attention areas for AGENTICTEA will follow the standard untuned field evaluation sequence.

First, voltage stability review. The voltage view should be examined for triangular dip patterns indicating load-transition sag, sustained drops below the 480-volt nominal reference, and gold-streak outage events. If outage events are present, their frequency and duration should be documented and correlated with operational records.

Second, reactive power quantification. The kVAR maximum and reactive average must be confirmed to size the MPTS correction unit accurately. If kVAR maximum exceeds 400 kVAR, two H490 units will be required; if it falls within 400 kVAR, one H490 unit is the planning baseline.

Third, thermal burden assessment. The BTU-per-hour thermal burden figure, once extracted from the baseline image, should be evaluated against the transformer's rated thermal capacity. If thermal burden is running at a significant fraction of rated capacity, the insulation aging timeline becomes a reliability concern independent of the efficiency discussion.

Fourth, cooling cost attribution. In any facility where active cooling is present — refrigerated warehousing, food processing, climate-controlled manufacturing — the portion of cooling load attributable to transformer thermal losses should be isolated and included in the total waste cost calculation for AGENTICTEA.

Fifth, MPTS tuning assignment. Once sizing is confirmed, a tuning assignment should be entered in the system so that the correction timeline can be established. An untuned field without a scheduled tuning assignment will continue to accumulate waste and thermal stress without a defined endpoint for that accumulation.

This transformer should be moved to active data capture status without delay.
