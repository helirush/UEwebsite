**Transformer: PL1MS — Operational Stress Interpretation | May 2026**

PL1-MShop is operating in a manageable but unresolved stress condition across May. The transformer itself is not overloaded — 600 kVA at the 95th percentile against a 2,500 kVA nameplate means the facility is operating at roughly 24 percent of rated capacity on peak demand days. That headroom is significant. But headroom at the transformer nameplate level does not translate to a clean or efficient field condition when the power factor is running at 86 percent and reactive surges are reaching 228 kVAR on their worst events.

The operational stress is expressing itself in three compounding layers. First, the thermal layer: the BT header confirms 147,430 BTU per hour of thermal burden and an associated 21.5 kW of cooling load at approximately $4.25 per hour. That cooling overhead is a direct consequence of the reactive burden — heat that the facility's mechanical systems must continuously work to remove. In a food processing environment where refrigeration is already a primary operating cost, adding avoidable electrical heat burden to the thermal envelope is a meaningful operational inefficiency.

Second, the electrical efficiency layer: at 86.2 percent average power factor, the facility is paying for 100 percent of the apparent power its transformer handles while only productively using 86.2 percent of it. The gap — roughly 13.8 percent of apparent energy — is reactive circulation. It is consuming conductor capacity, transformer capacity, and generating heat without doing any productive work for the facility's processes.

Third, the cumulative decay layer: this is the most important stress vector for an untuned field. Each month that passes without correction, the reactive burden continues to stress insulation, the harmonic content continues to add incremental thermal loading, and the voltage field continues to swing between its chronic high-side swell and its occasional demand-driven dips. The damage mechanisms are slow but they are continuous. Insulation rated for 20 years under clean field conditions can be compressed to significantly shorter life under sustained reactive and harmonic stress. For a 2,500 kVA transformer serving a food processing facility — where unplanned downtime has direct production and food safety consequences — managing that decay risk is not optional.

The BT panel confirms the production rhythm is healthy and predictable. Foster Farms' Livingston HQ facility is running consistent shift-based industrial production with clean weekend valleys. That predictability actually makes MPTS correction sizing straightforward — the reactive profile is well-characterized, the peak demand is bounded, and the correction requirement is moderate.
