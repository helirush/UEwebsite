**Transformer: PL1MS — Reactive and Harmonic Burden | May 2026**

The reactive profile on PL1-MShop tells a consistent story: a moderate but chronic reactive tax with sharp episodic amplification during load events. The CSV-confirmed average reactive draw is 142.7 kVAR. At 86.2 percent average power factor, the transformer is managing roughly 42.5 kVAR of reactive overhead for every 100 kVA of apparent power on average — all of it generating heat, occupying conductor capacity, and adding to the thermal burden on transformer windings.

The M panel confirms the reactive surge behavior clearly. The maximum amperage recorded is 710.4 amps, and the power factor minimum during surge events drops to 77.3 percent. When power factor falls to 77 percent during a peak event, the ratio of reactive-to-productive power swings sharply unfavorable. The transformer is pushing significantly more apparent power through its windings than the facility's processes are actually consuming as productive work during those moments.

The CSV-estimated kVAR MAX is 228 kVAR, which is the appropriate sizing reference for MPTS equipment specification. The BT header image value of 2,197.8 kVAR has been flagged as anomalous — likely a dataset-scope or unit artifact — and is not used for sizing. Using 228 kVAR as the peak reactive reference: 228 ÷ 400 = 0.57, rounded up to 1. **One H490 unit at approximately 400 kVAR correction capacity covers the PL1MS reactive profile with margin.**

The harmonic picture adds a supplementary concern. The M panel reports an average iTHD of 5.0 percent with a peak of 10.3 percent. At 10.3 percent peak total harmonic distortion, the transformer is experiencing measurable additional heating from harmonic currents during production peaks. This is not at crisis levels for a 2,500 kVA transformer, but in an untuned field, harmonic content acts as an accelerant on the underlying thermal burden. Every additional degree of winding temperature compresses insulation life. Without correction, harmonic-driven thermal accumulation continues quietly across every production shift.

The reactive surge events — confirmed in both the M amperage peaks and the BT kVA spikes — are load-event driven. The primary drivers are motor starts, compressor cycling, and refrigeration load switching. These are characteristic of a food processing facility operating scheduled production runs. They are predictable, and they are correctable with properly sized reactive compensation.
