PL1-MShop is running at 86.2 percent power factor through all of May. That means roughly 14 percent of every apparent energy dollar your transformer handles is reactive waste — circulating heat, not productive work. Your average reactive draw is 142.7 kVAR, peaking at an estimated 228 kVAR during surge events. One of those surges — around May 20th — pushed amperage to 710 amps and pulled your voltage down to 454 volts, a 5.4 percent dip below your 480-volt nominal. That kind of event stresses motor windings and insulation on a facility carrying refrigeration and processing loads. Your thermal burden is running at 147,430 BTU per hour with a about $4.25-per-hour cooling overhead. The BT panel estimates total annual waste in the range of approximately $112,000 — a planning-level figure that includes energy waste, thermal burden, and cooling costs. The correction path is one H490 unit — approximately $200,000 installed, planning estimate — which brings power factor to 99.5 percent and eliminates the reactive circulation driving that thermal and voltage stress.
