**PL1P2 — Operational Stress Profile | May 2026**

Taken together, the four data streams — voltage, baseline loading, reactive field, and harmonic content — describe a transformer that is working significantly harder than it needs to in order to deliver the same real kilowatts to the process floor. The 84.8% average power factor means that approximately 15% of the electromagnetic energy circulating through this transformer at any moment is doing no useful work. It is generating heat, it is stressing insulation, and it is pulling current through conductors that would otherwise be available for productive load.

The production pattern itself is a standard industrial rhythm — strong weekday load blocks, clean weekend valleys, approximately four full production cycles across the month. The facility is operating predictably. But predictability in a poorly compensated field means predictably accumulating reactive losses every single week. The 943 kVA supply target from the BT view serves as the demand ceiling, and the trace spends roughly 80 to 85% of operating time below that line — which means the transformer is not constantly overloaded. But the reactive burden that drives the thermal heating and the voltage instability is present regardless of where the kVA sits relative to the target. Reactive losses do not require peak loading to cause damage. They are active at every point in the production cycle.

The one mid-week interruption event — estimated at 60 to 90 minutes — occurred during what appears to be an active production window. Whether that event was planned or unplanned, it is a data point worth tracking. In an untuned field with high reactive content and recurring voltage dips, unplanned interruptions carry a higher restart risk because the reactive surge on motor restart compounds against an already-stressed voltage profile.

The forward-looking concern is drift. An untuned field does not stabilize on its own. Reactive content tends to increase as motor insulation ages, as load mix shifts, and as maintenance cycles accumulate. The $251,065 per year in total waste measured in May is the current baseline — without tuning assignment, that number has a natural upward pressure month over month.
