**PL1P2 — Operational Stress Interpretation | May 2026**

Read together, the voltage findings and reactive findings describe a transformer under compounding operational stress. The field is not failing — but it is working harder than it should, every hour of every production day, and the consequences are measurable and accumulating.

The BT production pattern confirms a strongly rhythmic five-day operating cycle with clear weekend valleys. Weekday operating peaks regularly push into the 750 to 950 kVA band, with the p95 load confirmed at 942.7 kVA — nearly matching the 943 kVA supply target. That means on peak production days, this transformer is operating at its practical ceiling. There is minimal headroom between the delivered supply and the transformer's operating target, and during those peak hours the reactive burden is simultaneously at its highest — pulling voltage down, increasing current, and generating heat at the moment when the system can least afford additional thermal load.

The BT view shows the transformer exceeding the supply target on a significant portion of weekday hours — approximately 70 to 75 percent of weekday operating time appears at or above the 943 kVA threshold. This is consistent with a facility running continuous or near-continuous production shifts through the full weekday window.

Two brief load interruption events were observed in the BT view — one near 260514 and one near 260523. These present as rapid near-total load collapse events spanning approximately one to three minutes each. They are not gold-fill outage streaks in the classic sense, but they represent operational interruption events — whether grid-driven or internally driven — that add to the reliability risk profile of this field.

The thermal burden is the compounding factor that makes this an urgent conversation. The BT header reports approximately 424,981 BTU per hour of thermal burden, with a baseline of 267,364 BTU per hour. Cooling overhead is estimated at $12.20 per hour and approximately 62 kW of cooling load. In an untuned field, that thermal burden does not stay flat. It grows as reactive current increases conductor and core temperatures over time, accelerating the very insulation degradation that the voltage sags are simultaneously driving. These two stressors — voltage depression and reactive heat — are reinforcing each other, and without a tuning assignment, the trajectory is downward.
