**Transformer: PL1-Proc1 — Recommended Engineering Attention Areas | May 2026**

Four areas warrant priority engineering attention on PL1-Proc1.

**First: MPTS reactive compensation assignment.** One H490 unit (400 kVAR) should be scoped for installation on this transformer. The kVAR peak of 317.3 as captured in the BT header drives this recommendation. The untuned reactive burden is costing this facility an estimated $78,922 per year in combined waste — that number is sitting on the table unaddressed every month without a correction assignment.

**Second: Voltage floor investigation.** The 462-volt minimum recorded in May represents an 18-volt dip below the 480-volt nominal. Engineering should validate whether this floor is being driven by upstream supply variability, local load-switching events, or transformer impedance under peak reactive loading. If local reactive correction with the H490 does not fully resolve the voltage floor condition, additional voltage regulation investigation may be warranted.

**Third: Harmonic assessment and mitigation review.** The iTHD peak at 9.7 percent is approaching the threshold where active harmonic filtering becomes a meaningful consideration alongside reactive compensation. Engineering should review the harmonic spectrum at PL1-Proc1 — particularly around VFD-connected loads and refrigeration compressor controls — and determine whether the MPTS installation should incorporate harmonic filtering capability or whether a separate mitigation strategy is appropriate.

**Fourth: Outage event root cause review.** Three power interruption events totaling an estimated 60 to 90 minutes of downtime during May warrant review against operations and maintenance logs. Unplanned outages in a food processing and cold-chain environment carry costs well beyond the energy field — product safety compliance, equipment restart sequencing, and refrigeration recovery time all factor into the true cost of each event. Determining whether these outages are supply-side events, local protection trips, or equipment-driven interruptions informs both the operations team and the MPTS installation design.
