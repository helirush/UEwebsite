**Transformer: PL1MS — Simulated MPTS Correction Outcome**

Based on the CSV-confirmed reactive profile — 142.7 kVAR average, 228 kVAR estimated peak, 86.2 percent average power factor — one H490 unit provides the appropriate correction platform for PL1-MShop. The H490 delivers up to 400 kVAR of reactive compensation capacity, giving the PL1MS field approximately 175 kVAR of headroom above the measured peak reactive demand. That margin is important in a food processing environment where load events can be abrupt and the reactive compensation needs to respond without lag.

Post-correction, the projected field behavior shifts significantly. Power factor climbs from 86.2 percent to the MPTS-corrected target of 99.5 percent. That means the gap between apparent power and productive power essentially closes. The transformer is no longer handling 142.7 kVAR of circulating reactive energy on every average operating hour. Conductor loading drops, transformer winding temperature drops, and the voltage field stabilizes as the reactive demand that was pulling voltage down during surge events is now absorbed by the compensation system before it can stress the field.

The thermal burden reduction is a direct consequence of the reactive correction. When reactive circulation drops and the transformer's apparent loading decreases, the heat generated in windings and conductors decreases proportionally. The 147,430 BTU per hour thermal burden confirmed in the BT header decreases materially — reducing both the mechanical cooling overhead and the rate of insulation aging. The estimated $4.25 per hour cooling cost associated with the current thermal burden represents a component of recoverable waste once the reactive burden is corrected.

The BT image provides waste framing of approximately $5,964 per dataset period and an estimated $112,820 per year in total wasted costs — encompassing energy waste, thermal burden, and cooling overhead. These are image-sourced planning estimates and should be treated as directional. The CSV does not provide direct waste dollar confirmation, so final financial modeling will require a full Mercury analysis with utility rate inputs. However, the structural case is sound: at 86.2 percent power factor with 142.7 kVAR average reactive draw and confirmed thermal burden, the waste components are real and measurable.

Planning investment for one H490 unit installed is estimated at approximately $200,000, depending on engineering scope and site conditions. That is a planning-level estimate — not a binding figure. Final installed cost will depend on switchgear configuration, cable routing, and any site-specific requirements identified during engineering assessment.
