Transformer: PL1M — Operational Stress Interpretation

Operational stress in an industrial transformer is cumulative. Every hour that the transformer carries reactive burden it shouldn't be carrying, it runs hotter than it should. Every degree of excess heat shortens insulation life, increases the risk of a thermal event, and forces cooling systems to run harder. In a tuned-field environment, the goal of MPTS correction is to reduce that stress load — to bring the transformer closer to its designed operating point and let the insulation and cooling infrastructure operate within their comfort zones.

For PL1M, the thermal burden figures — measured in BTU per hour — are unavailable from both image and CSV sources. The cooling cost overlay, which would show the kilowatt load and hourly cost of compensating for excess heat, is similarly unavailable. Without these values, I cannot quantify the stress reduction that the installed MPTS assets have already achieved for PL1M, nor can I characterize the residual stress that remains in the optimized field gap.

What I can tell you structurally is this: in tuned-field optimization mode, the thermal story has two chapters. Chapter one is the reduction already achieved — the difference between the original untuned field and the current TBT state. Chapter two is the remaining optimization gap — the further thermal relief that would come from closing the distance between TBT and TS. Both chapters require live numeric data to tell properly.
