**Transformer: PL1P2 — Operational Stress Profile | June 2026**

The BT view lays out PL1-Proc2's operational profile in unmistakable terms. This transformer serves a facility with a clear weekday production rhythm — high-demand plateaus during active shift operations, followed by deep demand valleys on weekends or during scheduled maintenance intervals. The two most prominent low-demand windows, visible around June 7 through 10 and again near June 28 through 29, represent either weekend shutdowns or planned outage intervals. These appear as near-zero kVA events in the stacked area chart rather than confirmed gold-streak outage markers, so they are characterized here as probable production shutdowns.

What stands out operationally is what happens to power factor during those valley periods. When production demand drops and kVA draw falls, the reactive load does not fall proportionately. Inductive loads — transformer magnetizing current, motor windings, refrigeration compressor coils — remain partially or fully energized even when the production line slows. The result is a power factor collapse during low-demand windows, with the orange PF trace dropping sharply toward the floor in the BT view. This is the reactive baseline burden: the inductive infrastructure of the facility drawing reactive current even at rest.

During peak production — the periods where kVA climbs above the 993 kVA target — the combined demand of active motors, cooling loads, processing equipment, and HVAC systems drives both real and reactive demand upward simultaneously. The 773.4 kVAR maximum spike recorded this month almost certainly corresponds to one of these peak production moments, when multiple large inductive loads switched on in close temporal proximity.

The cumulative operational stress picture is clear. This transformer is managing a dynamic, high-inductive-load environment with no reactive compensation in place. The voltage is sagging repeatedly under transient load switching. The current is elevated above what a corrected field would require. The heat burden is continuous and substantial. And the facility is absorbing approximately $261,609 per year in total waste costs — energy waste, thermal burden, and cooling overhead — from this transformer alone.

The untuned designation is not a technicality here. It is the operative condition driving every one of these outcomes. Each week that passes without MPTS correction assigned is another week of progressive insulation stress, elevated maintenance risk, and waste cost accumulation. The reactive burden does not plateau — as motors age and insulation quality gradually declines, reactive demand tends to drift upward. The cost trajectory for PL1-Proc2, without intervention, points in one direction.
