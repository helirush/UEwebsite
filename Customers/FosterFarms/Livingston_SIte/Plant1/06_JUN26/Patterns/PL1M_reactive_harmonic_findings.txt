Transformer: PL1M — Reactive and Harmonic Findings

Reactive power is the silent load on your transformer — it doesn't do productive work, but it absolutely consumes transformer capacity, heats the windings, and forces your cooling systems to compensate. In a tuned field, the MPTS assets are actively suppressing reactive demand, and the TBT (tuned baseline target) view is the key reference for understanding how much reactive burden remains after correction. The TS (tuned savings) view then shows the projected outcome if the field reaches its optimized state — the gap between TBT and TS is the remaining opportunity.

For PL1M, reactive load figures — including average kVAR and peak kVAR maximum — are unavailable from both the TBT and TS image views and from the Mercury CSV output. Without a confirmed kVAR maximum value, I cannot apply the H490 sizing rule (kVAR MAX divided by 400, rounded up) to evaluate whether the installed assets are appropriately sized for the current reactive profile, or whether any residual reactive load exceeds the correction capacity in place.

On the harmonic side, harmonic distortion interacts with reactive compensation and can reduce the effective performance of installed MPTS equipment if not properly accounted for in the system design. Without waveform or harmonic content data for PL1M, no harmonic assessment can be offered here.
