What you are looking at with DELIF is a transformer that is working significantly harder than it needs to — and paying a measurable price for it every single day. Across all of May, your average power factor on this transformer sat at 85.26 percent. That means for every hundred units of electrical capacity moving through this transformer, roughly fifteen of them are circulating reactive current — spinning through motors, cycling compressors, sustaining inductive loads — without producing a single watt of useful output. At an average draw of 770 kVA and a peak demand pushing 1,544 kVA — which is also your supply ceiling — you are running this transformer close to its limit during production hours with a significant reactive penalty baked in. The voltage field confirms the stress. Your average voltage sits right at nominal — 277.77 volts line-to-neutral — but the operating band tells the real story. You are seeing sags down to 456.9 volts line-to-line during inductive load events. That is nearly 5 percent below nominal, and in a food processing environment those sags accumulate insulation stress on every motor winding, every drive, every control system that experiences them. And the data confirms at least one complete field collapse during the month — a zero-volt event lasting an estimated 60 to 90 minutes near the end of May. The reactive draw is averaging 347.7 kVAR with a planning-basis maximum of 556 kVAR. The thermal burden from all of this reactive cycling is sitting at 323,371 BTU per hour — heat that costs roughly $82,759 per year just to manage. Total estimated annual waste is approximately $248,000. With two H490 units correcting this field to 99.5 percent power factor, that waste largely disappears. The planning investment estimate is approximately $400,000 installed. That is the correction this field needs. ---
