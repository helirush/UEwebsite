The DELIF transformer is running at 85.26 percent average power factor across May — meaning about 15 percent of its capacity is tied up circulating reactive current that does no real work. Peak demand reaches 1,544 kVA, which is the supply ceiling, so the transformer is regularly at its limit during production windows. Voltage quality is a concern — sags to 456.9 volts line-to-line are recurring during production, and there was one confirmed field collapse of approximately 60 to 90 minutes near month-end. The thermal burden is 323,371 BTU per hour, with cooling costs running roughly $82,759 per year. Total annual waste is estimated at approximately $248,000. MPTS correction brings power factor to 99.5 percent. Two H490 units are sized for this transformer. Planning-level investment is approximately $400,000 installed. The waste recovery offsets that investment while also improving voltage stability and reducing thermal stress on equipment that is currently operating under chronic reactive burden. That's electromagnetic stewardship — and that's the Unity standard. --- Pricing note: these figures are planning-level estimates, not final quotes.
