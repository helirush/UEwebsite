**Transformer: Deli.G — DELIG | May 2026 | Untuned Field**

The Deli.G transformer is a 2,500 kVA unit currently operating in an untuned field state — and the data makes clear this field is under real stress. Every major indicator in the May dataset points to a transformer that is being pushed hard, carrying reactive burden it shouldn't have to carry, and delivering power that costs significantly more than it should.

Start with the headline number: the field is running at a power factor of 86.0 percent. That means for every unit of useful work this transformer delivers, it is simultaneously hauling a substantial reactive load that does no productive work — it just circulates, generates heat, and occupies transformer capacity. In a 2,500 kVA unit, that reactive drag is not trivial. The transformer hit 2,494.3 kVA maximum during the month — that is 102.3 percent of nameplate capacity. This transformer is not operating with comfortable headroom. It is at its ceiling.

The financial consequence of that untuned state is direct and measurable. The field is wasting approximately $133,139 per year in reactive losses alone, and when you fold in the thermal burden and cooling overhead, the total wasted figure reaches approximately $215,326 per year. That is real operational cost being spent on an energy field problem that has a known engineering solution.

The operating pattern shows a clear five-day production cycle with heavy weekday loading and reduced weekend activity. Deli.G appears to be in active electric production operations roughly 70 to 75 percent of the month by time, but during those production windows the loading is dense and consistently near or above transformer capacity. This is a field that is working at its limit — and doing so inefficiently.
