**DELIF Transformer — Reactive and Harmonic Findings | May 2026**

The field never relaxes below a substantial reactive current draw during production hours because the inductive load base — motors, compressors, refrigeration systems — requires continuous magnetizing current. The planning-basis kVAR MAX used for MPTS sizing is 556 kVAR, derived from the CSV P95 profile. The image header records a transient peak of 1,150 kVAR during the most intense surge events — a reminder that instantaneous reactive demand during large motor starts can spike far above the sustained average.

That is nearly double the average current occurring during surge events — a severe inductive surge profile that represents real mechanical and thermal stress on transformer windings, bus connections, and protective devices.

The power factor story compounds the reactive picture. The CSV-confirmed average is 85.26 percent, and that already signals meaningful inefficiency. But the M-view image reveals the true operational range: the minimum power factor dips to 72.58 percent during surge events. At 72.58 percent, nearly 28 percent of the apparent power flowing through this transformer is doing no useful work whatsoever. For a transformer processing nearly 800 kVA on average and peaking well above 1,500 kVA, that represents a substantial volume of wasted electrical energy.

The harmonic picture adds a compounding stress layer. The image confirms 5.2 percent iTHD average with a 10.4 percent maximum — a persistent harmonic contamination floor that never reaches zero during production. Sustained harmonics at these levels generate additional winding heating beyond the fundamental-frequency reactive losses, accelerating insulation aging and contributing to the 323,371 BTU per hour thermal burden reported in the BT header. The combination of high reactive surge current and continuous harmonic contamination is one of the most thermally corrosive pairings a transformer can experience in continuous operation.
