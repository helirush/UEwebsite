**DELIF Transformer — Simulated MPTS Outcome | May 2026**

Power factor corrects from 85.26 percent to 99.5 percent — confirmed in the simulation header as the MPTS target condition. At 99.5 percent power factor, essentially all of the apparent power flowing through this transformer is doing real productive work. The reactive circulation current that currently consumes roughly 15 percent of the transformer's capacity without contributing useful output is neutralized at the point of generation, before it burdens the supply infrastructure.

For MPTS sizing, using the CSV-anchored planning kVAR MAX of 556 kVAR: 556 divided by 400 rounds up to 1.39, which means **2 H490 units** are indicated for DELIF correction. At approximately $200,000 per H490 unit installed, the planning-level investment estimate for DELIF is approximately **$400,000** — subject to final engineering scope and site conditions, and not a binding quote.

The reactive kVAR draw collapses toward the residual correction floor. The 347.7 kVAR average reactive burden the field currently sustains is redirected — the MPTS units supply local magnetizing current, eliminating the reactive component from the upstream supply path. The transformer processes real power only, and the amperage on the supply conductors drops accordingly. Lower current means lower I²R losses in conductors and windings, which directly reduces the thermal burden.

The energy waste recovery — estimated at approximately $153,543 per year in direct losses, with total waste savings approaching $248,000 annually when thermal and cooling overhead are recovered — represents the financial return this correction delivers. The thermal burden of 323,371 BTU per hour drops substantially. Cooling costs estimated at $82,759 per year are recovered or significantly reduced. The voltage field stabilizes as reactive surge currents are damped at source, reducing the triangular sag events and narrowing the voltage operating band.

This is not theoretical. This is what reactive power compensation at unity power factor does to a field that is currently running at 85 percent. The simulation makes the before-and-after visible and quantifiable.
