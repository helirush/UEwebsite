**DELIF Transformer — Recommended Engineering Attention Areas | May 2026**

The following areas warrant priority engineering attention for the DELIF transformer based on the May 2026 field analysis.

**Reactive compensation — MPTS sizing and placement:** Two H490 units are indicated based on the 556 kVAR planning maximum. Engineering site assessment should confirm optimal placement relative to the dominant inductive load centers — refrigeration compressor banks and processing line motor groups are the likely primary targets. Placement at load rather than at the transformer bus maximizes reactive current path reduction.

**Voltage quality investigation:** The confirmed 0.0-volt minimum and the recurring 456.9-volt sag events warrant a dedicated power quality investigation to identify whether the protection event near month-end is related to field stress, upstream supply, or a specific load switching sequence. The 41-volt peak-to-trough voltage spread across May is wider than a healthy industrial field should exhibit.

**Outage event review:** The approximately 60 to 90 minute power-off interval near May 29 to 30 should be characterized — whether planned maintenance, upstream fault, or protection trip — to assess whether field stress contributed and whether recurrence risk exists.

**Harmonic mitigation assessment:** The 5.2 percent iTHD average with 10.4 percent peaks warrants evaluation of whether passive or active harmonic filtering is warranted alongside reactive compensation. Variable frequency drives and switching power supplies in the load mix are likely harmonic contributors, and MPTS correction alone does not eliminate harmonic distortion.

**Thermal management review:** At 323,371 BTU per hour of thermal burden, the transformer and surrounding electrical infrastructure should be assessed for cooling adequacy, particularly during summer months when ambient temperatures in the Central Valley will compound the thermal stress significantly. The $82,759 annual cooling cost estimate underscores the financial stakes of allowing this burden to persist.

**Load growth headroom:** With P95 demand already at the 1,544 kVA supply ceiling, any planned production expansion requires reactive correction first — otherwise load growth pushes the transformer into sustained overload territory.
