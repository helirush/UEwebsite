Let's walk through what we're seeing in the Deli.G field for May. This is a 2,500 kilowatt-apparent transformer — and it spent May operating at 102.3 percent of its nameplate capacity. That's not comfortable. That's a transformer running at its ceiling, during production, on a regular weekly cycle. The power factor on this field is 86 percent. What that means practically is that for every unit of real, productive work the transformer delivers, it's simultaneously carrying a substantial reactive load that generates heat, occupies capacity, and does no useful work. The reactive demand averaged 319.8 kVAR throughout the month, and it peaked at 1,779.6 kVAR during the high-demand windows. Those reactive peaks are directly connected to the voltage behavior you're seeing. Voltage averages 480.4 volts — right at nominal — but during peak loading events, it drops to approximately 460 volts. That's about 4 percent below nominal. And it happens repeatedly, in a clustering pattern tied to the production cycle. Every VFD, every refrigeration control board, every motor starter in the Deli.G zone is experiencing that voltage stress on a predictable, recurring basis. The financial consequence is real. The field wastes approximately $133,000 per year in reactive losses, and when you add the thermal burden — 280,066 BTU per hour — and the $71,900 per year in cooling costs to manage that heat, the total wasted figure reaches approximately $215,000 per year. MPTS correction sized for this field — five H490 units — projects a correction to 99.5 percent power factor. That relieves transformer loading, stabilizes voltage, and recovers the full waste stream. The planning-level installed investment is approximately $1,000,000 — with final cost depending on engineering scope and site conditions. That's the Deli.G field. A hard-working transformer carrying a burden it shouldn't have to carry. ---
