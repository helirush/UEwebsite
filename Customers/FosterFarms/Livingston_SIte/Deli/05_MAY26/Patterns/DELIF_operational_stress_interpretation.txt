**DELIF Transformer — Operational Stress Interpretation | May 2026**

Taken together, the voltage findings, reactive findings, and harmonic findings converge on a single operational conclusion: the DELIF transformer is under chronic, compounding stress, and without correction, that stress grows over time. This is the nature of an untuned field — it does not hold steady. It drifts. Equipment ages, insulation degrades, and the reactive burden tends to rise as motors and compressors accumulate wear and their power factor characteristics worsen.

Cooling costs are estimated at approximately $9.45 per hour, representing roughly $82,759 per year just to manage the excess heat this inefficient field generates. That cost is paid every month, every shift, whether production is running at full capacity or not.

When a transformer routinely operates at or above its supply ceiling in an untuned, reactive-heavy field, the cumulative effect is accelerated aging of insulation systems, elevated risk of thermal protection trips, and reduced headroom for load growth or process expansion.

The one confirmed outage event — approximately 60 to 90 minutes near the end of May — may or may not be operationally connected to field stress. But it is noted: a transformer operating under sustained reactive burden and voltage turbulence is statistically more vulnerable to protection events than one operating in a clean, corrected field. The untuned condition is not a background nuisance. It is an active contributor to reliability risk.
