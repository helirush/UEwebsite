**DELIG Transformer — Recommended Engineering Attention Areas**

**Priority 1 — MPTS Tuning Assignment:** The DELIG transformer has no reactive compensation in place. With 293.7 kVAR average reactive load and peaks reaching approximately 470 kVAR, the case for installing two H490 units delivering 800 kVAR combined capacity is supported by the field data. This is the highest-priority corrective action.

**Priority 2 — Outage Root Cause Investigation:** One field collapse event was detected in the final week of May, with an estimated duration of 20 to 40 minutes and a recorded minimum voltage of 9.6 volts. In a food processing and cold storage environment, this event warrants root cause investigation. Source could be upstream utility event, internal fault, or protection device operation. Event logging and protection coordination review are recommended.

**Priority 3 — Voltage Sag Mitigation:** Recurring triangular voltage-drop signatures reaching 460 volts — approximately 20 volts below nominal — are associated with large motor start events. A motor soft-start or VFD review for the largest inrush contributors would reduce sag depth and improve device longevity across the facility's motor population. Voltage sag events in a 480-volt industrial environment impose cumulative insulation stress that shortens equipment service life measurably.

**Priority 4 — Harmonic Load Audit:** The 5.1 percent average iTHD with peaks to 10.6 percent indicates a continuous nonlinear load base — likely VFDs and switching supplies. While currently below IEEE 519 action thresholds, a harmonic load audit should be conducted before MPTS installation to confirm filter configuration requirements and ensure the H490 units are optimally specified for this site's harmonic environment.

**Priority 5 — Thermal Management Review:** The 280,066 BTU per hour thermal burden and approximately $8.21 per hour in cooling overhead suggest the transformer electrical room thermal environment may benefit from an airflow and cooling capacity audit independent of MPTS correction, particularly given the facility's cold storage adjacency and food safety temperature requirements.
