**Transformer: Deli.G — DELIG | Operational Stress Assessment**

The stress picture on Deli.G in May 2026 is best understood by layering the individual indicators together, because each one reinforces the others.

First, transformer loading. A peak of 2,494.3 kVA against a 2,500 kVA nameplate is not routine — it means this transformer touched its physical design ceiling during the month. Operating at 102.3 percent of nameplate capacity, even briefly, accelerates insulation aging in the transformer windings and core. IEEE loading guidelines for liquid-filled transformers acknowledge that sustained operation above nameplate accelerates insulation life consumption at a non-linear rate. For a facility that runs production five days a week with heavy, consistent loads, this is an ongoing wear dynamic, not a one-time event.

Second, thermal burden. The field carries 280,066 BTU per hour of thermal burden, with a cooling cost of $8.21 per hour running continuously to manage it. That is $71,933 per year in cooling costs alone — just to counteract the heat that the reactive loading and harmonic environment are generating. That heat is not only a cost; it is a direct stressor on transformer insulation, switchgear, and any equipment in the electrical room environment. Heat accelerates every aging mechanism in power systems.

Third, amperage. The average amperage of 834.6 amps is already substantial for a distribution transformer. The peak of 2,864.9 amps is striking — it represents a very high instantaneous current demand event, well above what typical steady-state loading would suggest. Those peak current events are the moments when voltage dips to 460V, when reactive demand spikes toward 1,779.6 kVAR, and when the transformer is simultaneously at or above nameplate apparent power. Everything converges at those peak moments, and those are exactly the moments that do the most accelerated damage to insulation and connected equipment.

The cumulative picture is a transformer and distribution system that is working hard, running hot, carrying reactive burden, and delivering distorted voltage — all at the same time, all month long. This is the operational stress profile of an untuned field that has been in this condition for an extended period.
