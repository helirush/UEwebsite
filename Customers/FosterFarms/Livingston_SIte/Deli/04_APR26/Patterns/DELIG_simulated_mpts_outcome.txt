**DELIG — Projected MPTS Correction Outcome | Planning Estimate**

Based on the Deli G field profile — kVAR MAX of 1,875.2 kVAR, average power factor of 86.7%, and total annual waste of approximately $193,550 — the MPTS correction sizing and projected outcome are as follows.

**Sizing:** Using the standard sizing rule — kVAR MAX ÷ 400, rounded up — 1,875.2 ÷ 400 = 4.69, which rounds up to **5 × H490 units**. The H490 delivers 400 kVAR of reactive compensation per unit, yielding a combined correction capacity of 2,000 kVAR — sufficient to neutralize the measured reactive maximum with modest reserve margin.

**Planning-Level Investment Estimate:** Five H490 units carry a planning-level installed estimate of approximately $1,000,000, subject to final engineering scope, site conditions, and installation complexity. This is a planning-range figure, not a binding quote.

**Projected Post-MPTS Power Factor:** Correction to approximately 99.5% power factor — bringing the field from its current 86.7% average to near-unity. The minimum power factor events that currently collapse to 69.81% would be substantially mitigated, with reactive surges absorbed by the MPTS correction bank rather than propagating through transformer and wiring impedance.

**Projected Thermal Relief:** With reactive current burden substantially reduced, transformer thermal burden would decrease proportionally. The 251,363 BTU/hr thermal load would decline meaningfully — reducing both winding stress and active cooling demand. The approximately $64,823 in annual cooling costs would see significant recovery.

**Voltage Stability Improvement:** Reactive compensation at the point of load prevents reactive surge events from expressing as voltage sags. The 458.2V, 460.0V, and 460.7V dip events are direct consequences of reactive surges pulling down the field — MPTS correction interrupts that mechanism at its source.

**Estimated Annual Recovery:** The approximately $193,550 total annual waste figure represents the recovery ceiling. Actual recovery will depend on final correction depth, utility rate structures, and site-specific load profile. At planning-level recovery of that full waste figure, the estimated simple payback on a $1,000,000 investment would be in the range of approximately 5 years — a reasonable horizon for a high-utilization food processing circuit. Payback timeline is a planning estimate and should be validated through formal engineering assessment.
