**DELIF — Recommended Engineering Attention Areas**

**1. Reactive Compensation — Priority High.**
Two H490 MPTS units sized to 800 kVAR combined capacity are the primary corrective action for this transformer. The 726.7 kVAR MAX is the governing design parameter. Compensation should be sized to cover the peak, not the average — average kVAR of 407.4 would undercorrect the field during peak production loads, leaving voltage dips and thermal burden partially unaddressed.

**2. Voltage Stability During Load Transitions — Priority Moderate-High.**
Repeated voltage dips to 458–461 volts during production ramp events indicate reactive overcurrent stress on the supply path. Post-MPTS installation, voltage behavior during load transitions should be monitored and documented to confirm stabilization. If dips persist post-correction, an additional investigation into transformer impedance and feeder impedance is warranted.

**3. Harmonic Assessment — Priority Moderate.**
The 10.5 percent iTHD maximum exceeds the IEEE 519 general recommended limit of 5 percent at the point of common coupling for industrial facilities. A formal harmonic survey should be considered alongside MPTS installation to evaluate whether passive or active harmonic filtering is appropriate. MPTS correction addresses the reactive component — harmonic content may require supplemental filtering depending on the sensitive equipment inventory in this building.

**4. Outage Root Cause Review — Priority Moderate.**
One confirmed outage event near April 14, estimated at 40 to 60 minutes, should be reviewed for root cause. In a food processing environment, even a single unplanned outage carries product safety, sanitation, and restart cost implications. Determine whether this was a utility interruption, a protective relay event, or a switchgear operation, and assess whether MPTS correction reduces the likelihood of similar events by reducing reactive current stress on protective devices.

**5. Cooling Load Efficiency Review — Priority Moderate.**
At $92,299 per year in cooling overhead and 52.7 kilowatts of cooling draw, the thermal burden from this transformer's reactive losses is a secondary energy cost center worth tracking independently. Post-MPTS correction should produce a measurable reduction in cooling load — this is a verifiable ROI data point that can be captured in the first operating season after installation.
