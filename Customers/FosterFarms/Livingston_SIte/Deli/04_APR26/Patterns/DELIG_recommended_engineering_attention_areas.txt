**DELIG — Engineering Attention Areas | April 2026**

Several engineering attention areas emerge from the Deli G field analysis and warrant priority consideration before or concurrent with MPTS sizing and installation planning.

**1. Reactive Surge Root Cause Investigation:** The kVAR MAX of 1,875.2 kVAR — substantially above the 298.8 kVAR average — indicates reactive surges that are episodic and likely tied to specific load events: large motor starts, compressor staging, or conveyor drive engagement. Identifying and time-stamping these surge events against the production schedule will clarify whether soft-start or VFD upgrades on specific loads could reduce the reactive peak before MPTS sizing is finalized, potentially reducing the required correction bank size.

**2. Voltage Sag Event Correlation:** The three annotated voltage dip events (458.2V, 460.0V, 460.7V) should be correlated against production logs to confirm their triggering sources. If compressor staging or motor starts are the confirmed drivers, coordinated start sequencing or start-delay logic may reduce clustering severity and device stress between now and MPTS installation.

**3. Harmonic Filtering Consideration:** With iTHD averaging 5.1% and peaking at 10.2%, harmonic content is a background condition on this circuit. If MPTS correction capacitors are installed without harmonic filtering, there is a risk of harmonic resonance with the correction bank — particularly at the 5th and 7th harmonic frequencies common in drive-intensive loads. Engineering assessment should confirm whether passive detuning reactors or active harmonic filtering should be included in the MPTS installation design.

**4. Cooling Infrastructure Review:** At $64,823 per year in cooling costs and 251,363 BTU/hr of thermal burden, the transformer and associated switchgear are operating in a sustained high-temperature environment. A thermal imaging survey of the transformer, bus connections, and upstream switching equipment would quantify actual hot-spot severity and identify any immediate insulation or connection integrity concerns that should be addressed independently of MPTS correction.

**5. Minimum Power Factor Event Monitoring:** The 69.81% minimum power factor events are the most acute reactive stress moments on this circuit. Deploying interval-level power factor monitoring at 1-minute or sub-minute resolution during peak production shifts would build a precise map of when and how often the field drops to these extremes — supporting both MPTS sizing validation and utility rate management.
