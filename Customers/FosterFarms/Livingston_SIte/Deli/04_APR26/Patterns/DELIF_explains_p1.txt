Let's walk through what your Deli F transformer is telling us — and it has a lot to say. The headline number is your power factor: 85.3 percent. That means for every dollar of electrical capacity your utility is delivering to this transformer, about fifteen cents is going to waste. It's not powering a motor. It's not running a compressor. It's circulating as reactive energy — invisible, unconsumed, and expensive. That waste adds up to an estimated $276,493 every year. Your April month alone came in at roughly $14,056. Now layer in the thermal picture. Your transformer is generating 361,073 BTU of excess heat every hour — and your cooling system is working hard to manage it, costing approximately $92,299 a year just in cooling overhead. That's 52.7 kilowatts of cooling energy, running continuously, to compensate for a problem that can be corrected at the source. On the voltage side, your field looks stable at a 480-volt average — but the average is misleading. We recorded confirmed dips down to 458.2 volts during production load transitions. That's nearly 4.5 percent below nominal, and it happened multiple times across the month. Those dips are when your motors, your drives, your refrigeration contactors are under the most stress. Every one of those events shortens equipment life. And your reactive maximum — 726.7 kVAR at peak — is the number that tells us exactly what correction looks like.
