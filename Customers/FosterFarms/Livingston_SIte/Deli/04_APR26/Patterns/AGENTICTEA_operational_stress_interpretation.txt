Operational stress on a transformer is cumulative and largely invisible until something fails. For AGENTICTEA in its current untuned state, the stress interpretation has to be framed around what we know structurally rather than what we can measure numerically — because the measurement data is not available this session.

Here is what the untuned classification implies operationally: reactive current is flowing through every conductor and winding associated with this transformer. That current generates heat proportional to the square of its magnitude. Thermal burden — measured in BTU per hour in our standard analysis — accumulates in the transformer core and insulation system continuously during operation. In a tuned field, reactive current is compensated locally, which means the transformer sees only the real power component of the load. In an untuned field like AGENTICTEA's, the transformer carries the full apparent power burden, which is always larger.

Cooling infrastructure pays the secondary price. Whether AGENTICTEA's facility uses active cooling, ventilation, or ambient heat dissipation, the thermal overhead from reactive circulation adds to that cooling load — and that overhead has a dollar cost per hour that we normally quantify from the image header. That figure is value unavailable here, but the mechanism is not in doubt.

The longer an untuned field runs without correction, the more thermal fatigue accumulates in insulation, the more the transformer's service life shortens, and the more the facility pays in energy waste that never appears as productive output. AGENTICTEA deserves a clean dataset so we can put actual numbers to that stress interpretation.
