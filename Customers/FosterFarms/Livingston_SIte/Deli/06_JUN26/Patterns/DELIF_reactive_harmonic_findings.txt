This reactive demand is the portion of the transformer's apparent power capacity that is being consumed but never converted to productive mechanical or thermal work. It is circulating energy — back and forth between source and load — heating conductors, loading transformer windings, and contributing directly to the 364,267 BTU per hour thermal burden documented in the BT header.

Power factor behavior in the M view confirms the severity. The minimum power factor recorded is 73.38 percent. Harmonics at this level contribute to transformer core heating through eddy current losses, create elevated stress on neutral conductors, and accelerate insulation degradation in motor windings and drive components. The purple harmonics trace in the M view never disappears — it is a continuous background tax on this field's health, present at every hour of every production day captured in June.

Combined, the reactive waste and harmonic burden describe a transformer that is working significantly harder than its nameplate load would require if the field were properly tuned.
