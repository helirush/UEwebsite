**FSO-3 | Foster Farms, Livingston HQ | June 2026 — Reactive and Harmonic Findings**

The TM view for FSO-3 lays out three concurrent field behaviors that together define the reactive and harmonic character of this transformer: a steady reactive KVAR band running as a continuous moderate-amplitude floor, a dense high-frequency amperage trace characteristic of large motor populations, and a persistent low-amplitude harmonic signature that runs without interruption across the entire 30-day period.

Starting with reactive load: the KVAR trace (green) maintains a consistent band corresponding to the TBT header's 202.4 kVAR average. This is not a reactive load that spikes and falls — it is a standing burden that reflects the continuous reactive demand of refrigeration systems, cold storage compressors, and processing motors running around the clock. The single notable elevation in the reactive trace occurs at the June 13th outage and restart window, where the reactive demand briefly climbs consistent with large motor inrush. The iMax REACTIVE callout from the TM view records 182 kVAR as the reactive peak — slightly below the TBT header's 334.2 kVAR MAX, which likely captures a different measurement interval or integration window. Both figures confirm a substantial reactive burden.

The amperage behavior is where the load character of this facility becomes most visible. The iMax AMPERAGE callout reads 801.9 Amps — that is a significant peak current event, almost certainly tied to the mid-month disturbance and post-restart inrush. The red amperage trace shows the dense, layered variation pattern of a facility with many simultaneous motor loads operating at different duty cycles — refrigeration compressors, fans, conveyors, and processing equipment all contributing to a complex, overlapping current signature.

Harmonics are the third layer. The purple harmonic trace at the base of the TM chart is persistent across every day of the month. It is not episodic or event-driven — it is structural. This is the signature of a facility with deep variable frequency drive penetration. VFDs on refrigeration compressors and conveyor systems generate continuous low-order harmonic current that accumulates in the neutral and ground paths, stresses transformer insulation, and creates the standing harmonic floor visible here. The harmonics are not at an alarming amplitude individually, but their constant presence means the transformer insulation is operating under continuous low-level thermal and electrical stress on top of the reactive burden. That combination — standing reactive load plus continuous harmonic injection — is exactly what accelerates transformer aging in food processing environments.

The minimum power factor of 81.51% recorded in the TM view is the single most operationally significant number in this section. That is the field's worst-case reactive performance during the month, and it confirms that during peak stress events, FSO-3 is operating well below corrected industrial standards. A facility of this scale and criticality should not be touching 81.51% power factor under any operating condition in a tuned field environment.
