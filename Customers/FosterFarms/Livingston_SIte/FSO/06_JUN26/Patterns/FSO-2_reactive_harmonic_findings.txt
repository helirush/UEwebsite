**FSO-2 — Reactive and Harmonic Findings | June 2026**

The reactive character of FSO-2's field is captured most clearly in the TBT header and the TM active-reactive view together. The headline number is 152.1 kVAR MAX — that is the peak reactive demand this transformer absorbed at its highest reactive moment during June. The reactive average is running at 96.4 kVAR, which means that even in a sustained, steady-state production sense, FSO-2 is carrying nearly 100 kVAR of reactive burden that the existing MPTS installation has not yet eliminated.

The power factor reading of 91.6% tells the story plainly. In a fully optimized field, power factor would be approaching 99 to 99.5 percent. At 91.6%, approximately 8 to 9 percent of the apparent power flowing through this transformer is reactive — doing no productive work, generating heat, straining the transformer's thermal margin, and driving up the cooling load on the facility. That 8-to-9-percent gap may sound modest in percentage terms, but on a transformer with a 347 kVA supply target and a 382.9 kW peak real demand, it translates into meaningful thermal and financial burden every operating hour.

The TM active-reactive view confirms the power factor is running high and relatively consistent across the month, which is itself a sign that tuning investment is working — the field is not wildly erratic. But the consistency of the reactive residual means this is a structural gap, not a transient anomaly. The 96.4 kVAR average is a persistent load the transformer is carrying continuously.

From a harmonic perspective, the TM view shows the active-reactive characteristic of a heavy industrial field. The load mix — motors, compressors, refrigeration systems, and processing equipment — is the classic source of both reactive demand and harmonic distortion. The micro-turbulence visible in the voltage trace on the TV view is consistent with harmonic content circulating in the field. The installed MPTS assets are providing reactive compensation, but the remaining 152.1 kVAR MAX indicates there is additional correction headroom that, if captured, would further improve power factor, reduce thermal burden, and smooth the harmonic content in the field.

The thermal burden figure of 72,923 BTU per hour is the direct physical consequence of the remaining reactive and harmonic load. That is heat being generated inside the transformer windings and distribution infrastructure — heat that the facility's mechanical cooling systems must then remove at an estimated cost of $2.46 per hour and a cooling energy draw of 11.2 kW. On an annualized basis, the cooling overhead alone accounts for approximately $21,507 per year of operating cost directly attributable to the residual reactive condition.
