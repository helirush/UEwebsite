**FSO-3 | Foster Farms, Livingston HQ | June 2026 — Tuned-Field Outcome: TBT vs. TS Gap Analysis**

The TS view for FSO-3 represents the projected post-optimization field state — what the field should look like when the existing MPTS correction is fully tuned and any residual optimization steps are implemented. The comparison between the TBT anchor and the TS outcome defines the remaining value available for capture.

With the current tuned field delivering an average power factor of 91.5% and a peak of 94.0%, the TS projection targets the 99.5% power factor level that a fully optimized MPTS field delivers. Closing that gap from 91.5% to 99.5% means substantially reducing the 202.4 kVAR average reactive burden — not eliminating it entirely, but driving it to a minimal residual that no longer generates meaningful thermal or cost impact.

The thermal burden implication is direct. At 149,513 BTU per hour in the TBT state, a fully optimized field would see that burden drop significantly — reactive heat is generated in direct proportion to reactive current, and reducing the reactive current from its current average toward a corrected floor translates immediately into lower winding temperatures, lower conductor losses, and lower cooling overhead. The 21.9 kW of cooling load and the approximately $4.55 per hour cooling cost are both driven substantially by the reactive and harmonic burden — optimization captures a meaningful portion of that cost back.

On an annualized basis, the TBT header estimates total wasted energy cost at approximately $115,776 per year. The energy waste component alone is approximately $70,580 per year. The TS outcome, when realized, drives the recoverable portion of that waste toward zero — the remaining waste after full optimization is expected to be a small fraction, representing only the irreducible losses inherent to any operating electrical system. For planning purposes, closing the current optimization gap represents an estimated annual benefit in the range of tens of thousands of dollars in direct energy cost reduction, plus the secondary savings from reduced thermal burden on cooling systems and extended equipment life.

Sizing context for the residual reactive gap: with a 334.2 kVAR MAX reading confirmed from the TBT header, the reactive correction demand at peak requires meaningful compensation capacity. Applying the sizing rule (334.2 ÷ 400 = 0.836, rounded up to 1 unit), the field peak is within the coverage range of a single H490 unit at the reactive maximum — though the installed equipment configuration and its current coverage level would need to be confirmed through a site engineering review before any incremental asset decisions are made. This is an optimization-gap refinement observation, not a greenfield sizing recommendation.

The field stabilization benefit already achieved is visible in the voltage trace: the 480V envelope is held across most of the month, and the reactive surges — while present — are contained within a range that the existing correction is managing. The TS outcome extends that stabilization, reducing the frequency and depth of the triangular voltage-drop events and providing a more resilient reactive buffer for outage-restart inrush events like the June 13th disturbance.
