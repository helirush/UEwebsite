**FSO Transformer — Reactive and Harmonic Findings | June 2026**

That peak current level is substantial for a 480-volt distribution system. The motor mix, compressor cycling, and cold-storage refrigeration create a continuous reactive draw that the distribution infrastructure must carry at all times.

Harmonic behavior adds another layer of stress. The iTHD average reads 8.0% — elevated but manageable. The iTHD maximum, however, reaches 31.1%. That maximum is meaningful. Harmonic pulses at 31% total harmonic distortion represent significant waveform distortion events that concentrate heat in transformer windings and create interference risk for sensitive controls and VFD-driven systems. The purple harmonics layer in the TM view shows these pulses concentrated in the same zones where amperage spikes occur — confirming motor-start events and nonlinear load switching as the primary harmonic drivers.

The combination of 250.5 kVAR average reactive burden, 918.5 kVAR peak exposure, 908-amp current spikes, and 31% harmonic peaks creates a compounding thermal and electrical stress environment on the FSO transformer and its downstream distribution. The tuned-field MPTS deployment is directly addressing the reactive component — and the TS simulation confirms it can deliver dramatic compression of that burden.
