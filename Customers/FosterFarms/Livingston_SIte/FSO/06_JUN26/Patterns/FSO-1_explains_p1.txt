Let's talk about what June is telling us on FSO-1 — your 2,500 kVA transformer at the Livingston HQ facility. The first thing worth saying is that the investment in field correction is working. Your Power Factor is holding in the 85 to 96 percent band for most of the month, and the voltage envelope is tracking the nominal 480-volt reference reasonably well across the full 30-day window. That is not background noise — that is your MPTS system doing its job. But here is where we need to be honest with each other: the field is not fully optimized yet, and the gap has a dollar sign in front of it. Your average Power Factor for June is 86.1 percent. Your reactive load averaged 250.5 kVAR across the month, and it peaked at 918.5 kVAR. That reactive drag is continuous — it does not rest when the line is quiet, and it does not stop generating heat when the facility is between shifts. The result is a thermal burden of 277,397 BTUs per hour — and your facility is spending roughly $8.75 every hour, all day, every day, to cool the heat that reactive waste is generating. That is about $76,600 a year in cooling cost, consuming nearly 373 megawatt-hours annually, just to manage this field's inefficiency. The total annual waste picture — reactive losses plus thermal burden plus cooling overhead — runs to an estimated $207,000 per year on this transformer alone. We also flagged two power outage events in the early-June window, in the 260607 to 260610 range. Each one lasted somewhere between 30 and 60 minutes. And the voltage data shows triangular dip patterns clustering in that same window — short, sharp excursions below 480V nominal, with a confirmed floor of 472 volts. In a food processing environment running continuous refrigeration and rendering loads, that kind of repeated sub-nominal voltage stress adds up in motor wear and insulation aging. The good news: the optimization pathway is clear, and the existing investment has already moved the field in the right direction. The next step is targeted refinement — not a new deployment, but closing the gap on what's already in place. ---
