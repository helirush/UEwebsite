**FSO-2 — Voltage Stability Findings | June 2026**

The voltage health picture on FSO-2 is a study in contrast. On one hand, the primary voltage band — running at a nominal 480.0 volts with a recorded maximum of 498.4 volts — sits in a generally coherent position throughout the thirty-day window. The blue voltage trace holds its elevation reasonably well, and the field's tuned character is visible in the overall stability of the envelope. The field is not drifting, not chronically elevated, and not chronically depressed. That is a positive outcome attributable to existing tuning investment.

On the other hand, the 9.6-volt minimum reading cannot be ignored. A voltage reading of 9.6 volts on a 480-volt nominal bus is not a sag — it is a near-total field collapse. That reading corresponds to at least one outage event where power to this transformer was effectively removed. The gold-streak outage markers in the TBT view confirm this: 4 to 5 distinct top-to-bottom gold streaks are visible in the June record, with the most prominent appearing in early June and likely representing either an extended outage or a scheduled maintenance window. The combined estimated outage duration across all events is roughly 20 to 35 minutes for the month.

The triangular voltage-drop signature is the third voltage finding. These characteristic pointed downward spikes are distributed throughout the trace — not clustered in a single problem window, but recurring broadly across all four weeks of the record. They appear with somewhat greater frequency and depth in the mid-to-late June portion of the dataset. Each triangular event represents a rapid voltage decrease followed by recovery, consistent with motor-start inrush current draws or sudden high-load step changes on a shared distribution bus. Individual events appear brief — seconds in duration — but their frequency across a thirty-day window means that sensitive equipment on this bus is accumulating repeated transient dielectric stress.

The device-health risk assessment from this voltage profile is **moderate.** The field is not in a dangerous condition, but variable frequency drives, programmable logic controllers, and precision instrumentation connected to FSO-2's bus are experiencing recurring sub-nominal voltage excursions month over month. That accumulation matters in a facility running continuous food processing and cold storage operations where unplanned equipment failure has direct production and food-safety consequences. Continued monitoring and consideration of additional voltage stabilization at the bus level is a prudent forward step.
