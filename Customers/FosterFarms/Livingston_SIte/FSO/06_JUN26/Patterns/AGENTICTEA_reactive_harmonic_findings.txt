That said, the reactive picture for any untuned industrial transformer follows a consistent structural pattern, and I can walk you through what that typically looks like. Inductive loads — motors, compressors, refrigeration equipment, HVAC systems — all draw reactive power as a byproduct of their electromagnetic operation. That reactive current flows back and forth between the load and the transformer, doing no productive work but consuming conductor capacity, generating heat in the windings, and increasing apparent power demand on the upstream supply. The ratio of real working power to total apparent power is your power factor. In an untuned field, that ratio degrades over time as load mix shifts and no correction mechanism adjusts to compensate.
