**FSO2 — Reactive and Harmonic Profile | TM View | June 2026**

The TM view for FSO2 in June is where the remaining optimization gap becomes numerically concrete. Reactive load — the kVAR trace running in green — is persistently present across all 30 days. The average reactive draw is 96.4 kVAR, with peaks reaching 152.1 kVAR. That is the inductive drag that the tuned field has partially corrected but not fully neutralized. Every kVAR of reactive demand that remains is current flowing through your cables, your transformer windings, and your switchgear — doing no real work, but generating real heat and appearing on your utility bill as reduced power factor penalties or inflated kVA demand charges depending on your tariff structure.

Current draw reads a maximum of 416.5 amps. For a 2500 kVA transformer, that is well within rated capacity — confirming the tuning work has kept this transformer from becoming thermally stressed on the current side. But the thermal burden is still real: 72,923 BTU per hour represents heat that has to go somewhere, and in a food processing facility, that heat is your enemy on two fronts — it degrades electrical equipment and it adds load to your cooling infrastructure.

The harmonic profile deserves direct attention. The iTHD (total harmonic distortion of current) reads 5.6% average with a maximum event reaching 10.6%. The 10.6% maximum is not a catastrophic number, but it is above the IEEE 519 general recommended threshold of 8% for this class of facility, and the maximum events appear as concentrated spikes in the TM trace, co-located in time with the voltage drop events visible in the TV view. That co-location is not coincidence — it confirms that specific load-switching events are simultaneously pulling voltage down and injecting harmonic content. VFDs, soft starters, and large motor starts are the usual suspects. This coupling of harmonic injection and voltage depression is the signature of a field that needs targeted diagnostic attention at the load level to fully close the optimization gap.

The power factor orange trace in the TM view holds consistently in the upper 80s to low 90s percent band across the month, with a recorded single-minute minimum of 86.77%. That floor confirms the field does not catastrophically collapse even at its worst individual minute — but the persistent 8-point gap below 99.5% is the quantifiable remaining work.
