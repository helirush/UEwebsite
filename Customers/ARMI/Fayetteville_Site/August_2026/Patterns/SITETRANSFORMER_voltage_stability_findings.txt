**ARMI Manufacturing — Site Transformer | Voltage Stability Findings**

The voltage field for August is running at an average of 492.7 volts — that is sitting above the 480-volt nominal line, and at first glance that looks comfortable. The utility is delivering on the high side of tolerance, which provides a small buffer. But the average value is not the story here. The story is the swing range.

Measured across the full 31-day August dataset, the Site Transformer's voltage field ran a live swing range of 16.4 volts — from a measured maximum of 500.6 volts down to a minimum operating floor of 484.2 volts. For a 480-volt system, a 16.4-volt excursion range represents meaningful variability. The system never technically dropped below the 480-volt nominal floor based on the minimum callout value, but the visual trace in the V view tells a more nuanced story. The triangular dip clusters are repeatedly pressing the voltage toward that lower reference band during production peak events. The minimum of 484.2 volts is the charted floor — but the trajectory of those dips is consistently downward during the moments of maximum load stress.

The triangular voltage-drop pattern is clearly present and it is not random. Approximately 20 to 22 distinct triangular dip clusters are visible across the 31-day span, aligned precisely to what appear to be shift-start load surges. The pattern is daily, rhythmic, and production-driven. Weekend intervals show shallower and less frequent dip events, confirming the source is on-site load behavior, not utility-side insturbance.

The coupling between these voltage dips and the reactive demand events is the most important finding. Every time amperage peaks — confirmed in the M view at a maximum of 302.1 amps — the voltage trace compresses in a triangular profile and the power factor orange band visibly narrows and drops. This is a coupled electromagnetic stress event. Reactive demand is pulling voltage down at the precise moments when current is already at its highest. That combination — high current and low voltage simultaneously — is what accelerates insulation aging in motors, stresses variable frequency drives, and shortens the effective life of capacitor banks if any are present on this circuit.

Device health risk rating for this field: **moderate to high**. The field is not in crisis, but it is absorbing these voltage excursions on every production workday. That is a 20-plus stress cycles per month pattern. Over a year, that is 240-plus coupled stress events absorbed by every device on this bus.
